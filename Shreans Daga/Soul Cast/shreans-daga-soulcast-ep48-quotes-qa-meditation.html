Soulcast Episode 48 — Quotes, Audience Q&A & Awakening the Buddha Within

Handwritten quotes born from three decades of spiritual practice under many masters, followed by live audience questions and the guided meditation “Awaken the Buddha Within.” Speaker’s words, context and intention preserved throughout.

Short Q&A — Complete Session at a Glance

Q1. When is power sought, and when is love enough?

A: When you want to harm, control or dominate, you seek power. When you want to heal, uplift and create, love is enough. Love itself is the greatest power. The true test of humility is staying in love even after power and position have come.

Q2. Why can pain be a blessing?

A: When life is comfortable we rarely look within. Pain disillusion us with the outer world and opens the possibility of transformation. From the soul’s view, pain is not punishment — it is happening for you so you can evolve. The deeper the pain, the stronger the possibility of transformation.

Q3. What is conscious breath, and why does it matter?

A: Conscious breath is full awareness of each natural in-breath and out-breath. Breath is more than survival; it is the flow of life, spirit and consciousness. It is the natural bridge between the physical body and the subtle dimensions. Every breath taken with awareness is a silent thread between the seen and the unseen, and a rise in consciousness.

Q4. Why do the same challenges keep returning?

A: We repeat what we don’t repair. Challenges are not coincidence; the soul plans them so it can evolve faster and demonstrate mastery. The true test is: in spite of challenges, do you keep your alignment with the inner being?

Q5. Is manifestation the main goal?

A: No. The real goal is to feel aligned, joyful and alive moment by moment. Life is the coming together of all these moments. Manifestation is only one moment; life is every moment.

Q6. How do I stop living inside my limitations?

A: If you keep fighting for your limitations, you will keep them. Change the narrative. Challenge yourself. It is okay to be laughed at. Whether you think you can or you cannot, you are right — so choose “I can.”

Q7. What is alignment?

A: Alignment is connection with the inner being — bathing and resting in your source, in pure consciousness. When resistance to existence drops, alignment happens naturally. Meditation helps because the ego-identity falls away and the real Self is known.

Q8. How should I handle anger at work or in family triggers?

A: There are no justified resentments. No outer situation justifies losing alignment. Help others (clients, family) from a calm, aligned state. Energy speaks louder than arguments. Triggers are teachers; the point is to rise every time you fall, not to never fall.

Q9. What is the essence of the Buddha meditation?

A: The Buddha is not only a historical person; it is a possibility already within you. Through conscious breath, dropping identity, resting in stillness, and opening the heart in compassion, what was sought outside is recognised as already here. What you are seeking, you already are.

1. Core Quotes & Deeper Meaning

When you want to harm, control or dominate, you seek power.
When you want to heal, uplift and create, love is enough. — Shreans Daga

Many times we chase power and position. Often the motive is control, domination, or even the ability to harm. When the intention is to heal, uplift and create, love itself is sufficient — no power over others is required.

It is easy to stay humble when you have no power; you have no choice. The true test is: when power, position and money come, can you still remain humble, still stay in love, still keep the original motive of service and healing? Ego loves to enter from the back door. Motive can start pure and later change. Authenticity is remaining in love even when you have everything.

Love is the biggest power. No other power is needed when love is present.

The deeper the pain, the stronger is the possibility for our transformation. — Shreans Daga

When everything outside is going well, we rarely look within. Dopamine from the outer world keeps us busy. Pain and suffering disillusion us with the impermanence of people and things. The same love that was a source of joy becomes a source of pain when it is lost. In that space we begin to ask: what is permanent joy?

Many enter spirituality precisely because of some adversity — illness, loss, depression, contrast. Pain is not a curse or a punishment. From the soul’s perspective it is happening for you, so that you can evolve, heal and transform. When loved ones go through challenges, one need not only feel pity; one can also feel a quiet excitement for the possibility of transformation that is opening.

Breath is more than survival. It is the flow of life, spirit and consciousness moving through you. It is the most natural bridge between the physical body and the subtle dimension. Every breath taken with awareness becomes a silent thread between the seen and the unseen, the body and the being, the physical and the spiritual. Every conscious breath is a rise in consciousness. — Shreans Daga

We usually treat breath only as survival. The moment awareness is brought to it, the same breath becomes cosmic life-force. It heals at physical, mental, emotional and spiritual levels. It is no longer only a biological process; it becomes sacred.

We are not only a physical body. We have multiple layers (koshas). Breath is the bridge that gives access to the subtler dimensions. Those dimensions are not “up there”; they are vibrational and already within us. When vibration changes, one is already in that dimension. Jesus pointed to the same truth: the kingdom of heaven is within.

What the eyes see is less than 1% of the light spectrum. Gravity, quantum field, 5G, X-rays — all exist though unseen. Conscious breath and the activation of inner vision allow us to tune into frequencies the naked eye cannot register. Out-of-body experiences, past-life and between-life states become possible because vibration has shifted. Every conscious breath is therefore a rise in consciousness.

What is conscious breath? Being fully aware of each and every breath — not shallow, not automatic — a silent witness to the natural rhythm.

When we are faced time and again with challenges, it is not a coincidence. Your soul is trying to tell you to wake up now. We repeat what we don’t repair. As a soul we plan the challenges. When the soul wants to evolve faster, it plans more challenges so that mastery of thoughts and emotions can be demonstrated. The true test is: in spite of challenges, you do not lose your alignment with your inner being. — Shreans Daga

Challenges are co-created incidents, not random misfortune. They are teachers. The issue is not how many times you fall; it is rising every time you fall. What is life without a royal challenge?

The goal is not just to manifest what you want. The real goal is to feel aligned, joyful and alive moment by moment as life unfolds. After all, life is nothing but the coming together of all these moments. Manifestation is only one moment. Life is every moment. — Shreans Daga

People become obsessed with manifestation. The deeper goal is transformation — what you become in the process. Overcome and overcome until you become. Experience can only happen in the present moment. Present moment is all you have. Happiness is possible every moment; it does not have to wait for a particular manifestation.

If you keep fighting for your limitations, then you’ve got to keep them. Whether you think you can or you cannot, you are right. Change the narrative. Challenge yourself. It is okay to be laughed at. At least you are doing what you want to do. — Shreans Daga

Stage fear, “I can’t,” repeated limitation stories — these keep the limitation in place. Reputation is often the investment of ego in others’ opinions. When one is willing to lose reputation, the restlessness of protecting it drops. Mind over matter: change the inner narrative from “I can’t” to “I can; let me try.” Courage is required, but fighting for the limitation only guarantees that it stays.

2. Audience Questions & Answers

Question — on manifestation & controlling a child

“When we aim for basic manifestation like ‘my son should listen to parents,’ is that not lifetime happiness rather than single-moment happiness? Is seeking that not like seeking power so I can control my child?”

Shreans: The foundation itself is wrong. Wanting the child to listen so that you can feel happy is the ego trying to tell you that control will give permanent happiness. It will not. Alignment with your own being is independent of whether the child behaves as you wish.

Question — anger in the legal profession

An advocate shares that clients’ suffering and the way courts function trigger strong anger. Even when trying to witness, the anger still comes out. How to handle this?

Shreans: There are no justified resentments. No matter what justification you give for anger toward courts, government or the ecosystem, there is no justification that allows you to lose alignment with your own being.

The best way to help a client is from the right state of being. Arguments, case laws and volume matter, yet when they come from calm, aligned energy they carry a different power. The quantum field does not care about your reasons for the vibration you are offering; it simply matches the vibration. If the profession itself keeps you in resistance, no real good happens for the client or for you.

You can argue loudly and firmly while the inner state remains peaceful. That state of being gives an edge that pure intellectual argument cannot. Energy speaks to the judge more than the words alone.

Question — parent–child friction & karmic bond

A mother living with her daughter feels the daughter does not listen or behave as desired. She wonders if it is a past karmic bond and finds herself losing alignment despite daily practice.

Shreans: Why should that make you lose your alignment? It is not a justification. These triggers are teachers. There is no coincidence; it is co-creation at its best. The issue is not how many times you fall, but rising every time you fall.

Question — What exactly is alignment?

Shreans: Alignment is connection with the inner being — bathing in your source, marinating in your source, resting in your consciousness. When you lose resistance to all forms of existence, you naturally come into alignment.

Meditation is a powerful tool because you lose identity, forget who you are (the ego self), resistance drops, and you connect to the consciousness self — not the selfie, but the real Self.

3. Guided Meditation — Awaken the Buddha Within

Before we begin, take a comfortable position. Sit up straight. When ready, gently close the eyes and allow yourself to arrive here.

There is a story that changed the course of human consciousness. A young prince, Siddhartha, had everything the world could offer — comfort, luxury, protection — yet something within remained unsettled. A quiet question rose: “Is this really all there is?”

One day he stepped outside the palace walls and saw life as it truly is: an old man, a sick man, a lifeless body. The question deepened: if everything changes and fades, what is real? That question did not leave him. It pulled him inward until he left everything behind — not out of rejection, but because the question mattered more. It became a quest.

Years of seeking passed. Finally he sat under the Bodhi tree with a simple, unwavering resolve: “I will not get up from here until I see the truth.” No more searching outside. No more running. Just sitting, observing, being.

In that stillness something revealed itself — not something he achieved, but something he realised: what he was seeking was never outside. It was always within.

The Buddha is not only a person from the past. It is a possibility within you — a state of awareness that is not created but recognised. What you are seeking, you already are.

The practice of conscious breath

One of the simplest and deepest ways into that awakening is the natural, conscious, sacred breath. Through awareness of the breath the Buddha touched the silence within.

Bring awareness to the nostrils. Observe the natural rhythm of in-breath and out-breath. Feel a gentle wave of relaxation. Notice the cool sensation of air entering and the gentle warmth as it leaves. With every breath, be here now — fully present, fully alive.

Dropping identity

Turn attention to the sense of “I” — the ego that holds names, roles and positions. Ask: “Am I the roles I play, or is there something deeper?” Realise that the identity held so dearly is a construct of the mind. Drop language, labels and titles. When you detach from identity you dissolve into nothingness — and that nothingness contains everything. What remains is pure awareness, witnessing the breath.

Hook onto the breath. Let not a single breath slip by without awareness. If the mind wanders, neither cling nor push away; simply return. Create no relationship with anything that appears. You are the witness to both appearance and disappearance. Past and future exist only as thoughts; when thoughts recede, past and future disappear and you arrive fully into the stillness of the present.

Just as muddy water must become still to clear, the mind must become still to realise the clarity of the Self. Lose yourself in nothing to become everything.

Resting in stillness

In alert stillness, feel surrender. There is nothing to change, nothing to achieve, nothing to seek, nowhere to go. When you realise there is nothing lacking, the whole world belongs to you. To the mind that is still, the whole universe surrenders. Dissolve into the space between thoughts — an experience of emptiness that is full of innate silence. This stillness is the mind of God in you. With each breath, merge with the greater Self and become pure consciousness.

The ancient refuge

Buddham saranam gacchami — I return to the awakened awareness within me.
Dhammam saranam gacchami — I rest in truth, in the deeper intelligence that guides life.
Sangham saranam gacchami — I settle into the wholeness where nothing within me is divided.

Notice the part of you that is already aware — aware of breath, body and every passing thought, yet untouched by all of it. Thoughts appear and dissolve; sensations rise and fade; awareness remains. Rest here without trying. Just awake. Just here.

There is an intelligence quietly moving through breath and heartbeat. For a moment, stop interfering. Stop managing every experience. Move with it like a river that does not force yet never loses its way. Life no longer feels like a weight you carry; it begins to carry you.

As effort and resistance drop, thoughts, emotions and body settle into a shared rhythm — distinct yet in harmony. A wholeness reveals itself that was never missing, only waiting to be remembered.

Heart and compassion (Green Tara)

Gently move attention into the space around the heart. Allow the breath to move in and out of that centre. A warmth may gather — unforced, unhurried. Green Tara is the embodiment of compassionate action, a living potential within you. Allow love and compassion to awaken. Judgment dissolves; compassion flows; forgiveness no longer feels forced. Feel connection and oneness as boundaries soften.

Remain here a few moments. The breath slow and steady, the body rested, the mind quieter. What began as the story of Siddhartha is now unfolding as Buddha within you. A calm awareness, still and unchanging, always here — and within it a quiet kindness that holds and asks for nothing.

Let this feeling extend into relationships, conversations and the world: a little more patience, presence and peace. Choose love where there was distance, compassion where there was resistance, empathy where there was judgment. Let this become your way.
