18 Guiding Principles
Complete Practical Guide

A single, deep, practical reference that brings together the teachings of Brahmarshi Patriji, Suvarna Suvvi and Padmavathi garu. Use this page every day for self-reflection, self-checking and steady growth as a Pyramid Master.

Foundational teaching · Brahmarshi Patriji · Consolidated with insights from Suvarna Suvvi & Padmavathi garu

Principle 1

Right Way of Anapanasati – Daily Meditation

Meditation is compulsory every day — rain or shine, pleasure or pain. From age 5 until the last breath of the body.

  • Sit comfortably, interlock fingers, cross feet, close eyes.
  • Simply observe the natural, continuous breath. No forcing, no controlling.
  • Anapanasati is Raja Yoga and the highest of 108 methods. It is different from Pranayama.
  • Forced breathing (rechaka, kumbhaka) causes energy loss. Natural breath gives energy.
  • Your own breath is your Guru. It touches the soul every moment and takes you inside.
  • When eyes are closed the outer world disappears; you rest in the Self.
  • As the mind empties, the soul receives solace, clarity, energy and higher vision.
  • Meditation prevents unnecessary interference in others’ lives and reduces karma.
  • Without daily Anapanasati, all other principles remain incomplete.
Practical: Fix a definite daily time. Start with whatever minutes you can and gradually increase. Never skip. The world is a stage; meditation is the time you step off the stage.
Remember: “My breath is my Guru. Today I will sit with my breath, no matter what.”
Principle 2

Read the Right Spiritual Books (Swadhyaya)

God gave two hands not only for work but for holding two books. Continuous Swadhyaya raises the soul.

  • Not every “spiritual” book uplifts the soul. Stories and Puranas may calm the mind temporarily but do not raise the soul.
  • Read books that give true Atma-vijnana / Soul Science — works of top masters (Seth, Illusions, Autobiography of a Yogi, recommended Western & Eastern masters).
  • Patriji himself rose higher because he read thousands of books.
  • Without Swadhyaya, even satsang remains incomplete.
  • Keep at least one right book always with you.
Practical: Read minimum two pages every day — train, free time, anywhere. Create the habit first; quantity will grow later.
Remember: “Today I fed my soul with at least two pages of true wisdom.”
Principle 3

Share Meditation Experiences

True satsang is the sharing of real spiritual experiences among people who know Truth.

  • Sharing does not reduce energy. This is a common myth.
  • Two benefits: (1) Others get inspired and try deeper, (2) You realise we are one — their experience becomes yours.
  • Hearing of long meditations or astral experiences motivates others to attempt more.
  • Never compare and feel inferior. Patriji said: “Everyone’s experience is mine because we are one.”
  • Listen with full attention and share without pride or fear.
Practical: In every satsang or conversation with another meditator, share at least one genuine experience and listen to one.
Remember: “My experience belongs to all. Their experience belongs to me.”
Principle 4

Practise Mouna (Silence)

As far as possible, keep the mouth shut. Silence conserves energy and deepens the inner journey.

  • Speak only when necessary, kind and true.
  • Unnecessary talk drains soul energy.
  • Right silence + love changes people more than arguments.
  • Silence is a daily practice, not only a retreat activity.
Practical: Before speaking, pause and ask: Is it necessary? Is it kind? Is it true? If not, remain silent.
Remember: “Today I will speak less and listen more.”
Principle 5

Utilise Full-Moon Energy & Group Meditation

The 13th, 14th and 15th lunar days carry abundant energy. Harvest it.

  • Full-moon nights are specially powerful for intensive meditation.
  • Group meditation multiplies the energy field.
  • Participate in every possible full-moon and Amavasya group sitting.
Practical: Mark full-moon and new-moon dates in your calendar. Keep those evenings free for longer meditation.
Remember: “I will never miss the energy of the full moon.”
Principle 6

Use Pyramid Energy

Sit inside or beneath a pyramid. This is why the movement is called Pyramid Spiritual Societies Movement.

  • Pyramid energy multiplies the power of meditation.
  • Every house should have at least one small pyramid.
  • Construct pyramids everywhere possible.
  • Spirituality belongs to metaphysics — beyond ordinary physics yet more real.
Practical: Meditate under or inside a pyramid daily. If you don’t have one, arrange for a small pyramid soon.
Remember: “Pyramid + Anapanasati = accelerated growth.”
Principle 7

Give Up Medicines – Heal Yourself with Soul Energy

Every disease is self-created because soul energy fell. Therefore it must be self-solved by raising soul energy again.

  • Doctors, medicines, surgery and hospitals are not the ultimate solution.
  • Meditation, pyramid energy, full-moon energy, books, silence and service are the real medicines.
  • When soul energy rises, the problem becomes small and vanishes.
  • Give up allopathic medicines only after sufficient sadhana is established; otherwise the name of Pyramid Meditation suffers.
Practical: For any new discomfort, first sit in longer meditation for 1–3 days and observe. Use medical help only when truly necessary, while continuing sadhana.
Remember: “My soul energy is my primary medicine.”
Principle 8

Strict Vegetarianism & No Garlic

No meat, no birds, no fish, no eggs. Humans are herbivorous. Killing for the belly is anti-spiritual.

  • To be spiritual is to be compassionate. Vegetarianism is practical Ahimsa.
  • Garlic specifically damages the astral body, prevents it from flying, and cuts connection with higher masters.
  • Onion is acceptable; garlic is not for those who want higher connection.
  • The movement gives strong peer support for anyone who wants to switch.
  • Eat according to need, never according to greed.
Practical: Completely stop meat, eggs and garlic. If guests cook with garlic, politely decline or eat only the items free of it.
Remember: “My plate must never contain violence or garlic.”
Principle 9

Live Close to Nature

Nature is pure energy. Time spent in forests, mountains and river-sides multiplies energy three times.

  • Whenever possible, leave the house and meditate in pure natural environments.
  • Trekking and sitting by rivers are powerful energy practices.
  • Do not remain only inside four walls.
Practical: At least once a week, sit for meditation in a park, garden, riverside or open natural space.
Remember: “Nature recharges my soul faster than any indoor place.”
Principle 10

No Special Clothing or Body Marks

Remain completely ordinary. Special clothes or marks only display stupidity, never greatness.

  • The sun does not need to declare “I am the sun”.
  • If real spiritual power is present, people will automatically recognise it.
  • Showing off increases followers and binds us further in the cycle of birth and death.
  • Dress simply and remain indistinguishable from ordinary people.
Practical: Check your wardrobe and body. Remove any item whose only purpose is to announce “I am spiritual”.
Remember: “I am ordinary outside, extraordinary inside.”
Principle 11

Teach Meditation to Children from Early Age

If a child learns meditation early, you need not worry about them later. They will use even a knife or gun rightly.

  • Make children independent through meditation from childhood.
  • Early Anapanasati builds lifelong spiritual strength and discrimination.
  • The next generation must grow up with soul power from the beginning.
Practical: Sit with children for a few minutes of simple breath awareness every day. Make it joyful, never forced.
Remember: “The greatest gift I can give a child is Anapanasati.”
Principle 12

Live as a Master – Never as a Disciple

There are no disciples in this movement. From day one you are a Master.

  • A person who comes on the first day is a “first-day master”. After one month, a one-month-old master.
  • The words “disciple” and “discipline” do not exist here.
  • Everything is of your own accord — you come, stay, learn and teach freely.
  • Mastery means command over your own body, mind and intellect.
  • Always think positively and miraculously. Cut negative thoughts yourself; no one else can do it for you.
  • All are Masters, all are Friends, all are Gods.
Practical: Stop calling yourself a disciple. Stand on your own feet. When any negative thought comes, immediately cut it with “Correct, correct, correct” and return to the breath.
Remember: “I am a Master. I take full responsibility for my thoughts and my life.”
Principle 13

No Money for Spiritual Teaching

Knowledge and wisdom are the birthright of every human being. Never charge for teaching meditation.

  • This is a strict thumb rule of PSSM.
  • Taking money turns a sacred act of punya into business and cancels the punya.
  • Go from village to village, home to home and teach whoever is willing.
  • Voluntary donations for the work may be accepted if freely offered, but never demanded.
Practical: Whenever you teach or share, keep the energy of free giving. If someone insists on offering, receive it as donation for the larger work, never as fee.
Remember: “I give what I received freely. Knowledge has no price.”
Principle 14

No Idol Worship & No Worship of Living Masters

Idols are for inspiration only. Living masters are sources of inspiration, never objects of worship.

  • Seeing Krishna should remind us of the Gita; seeing Rama should remind us of Dharma.
  • Worshipping the form itself does nothing.
  • Daily praise of a living person creates discomfort and dependency.
  • Real bhakti is research into one’s own true form (Swarupa Anusandhana) through meditation.
  • No one is higher or lower. All are equal divine souls.
Practical: If you feel inspired by someone, try to become like them through your own sadhana. Do not put them on a pedestal or yourself below.
Remember: “The Divine is inside me. I meet it through my own breath.”
Principle 15

Solve Your Own Problems with Your Own Energy

The moment you beg others to solve your problems, you give away your soul power.

  • Every person is responsible for their own problems and their own solutions.
  • The purpose of incarnation is to develop soul power by owning complete responsibility.
  • Sit in meditation when any problem comes. In 1–3 days the solution arises from within.
  • Even great masters had problems; they solved them themselves.
  • As knowledge grows, the very way of looking at problems changes.
Practical: When a problem appears, first sit longer in Anapanasati instead of immediately asking others or worrying. Watch what solution emerges from inside.
Remember: “I own this problem. My meditation will dissolve it.”
Principle 16

Live a Happy Family Life – The Middle Path

Do not renounce the world. Living in the family while walking the path of meditation is the highest and fastest way.

  • Sannyasa may take 100 births; family life with meditation can give everything in one birth.
  • Participate fully in family joys, spend time with children, remain balanced.
  • This is the Middle Path taught by Buddha and practised in PSSM.
  • Do not become serious or withdrawn in the name of spirituality.
  • Enjoy normal sexual life and care for fellow human and animal beings.
Practical: Be fully present with family. Laugh, play, share meals. Let meditation make you more loving and joyful, never dry or distant.
Remember: “My family is my first field of practice. Happiness at home is part of the path.”
Principle 17

Establish Meditation Centres Everywhere

Every Pyramid Master must become available. Put up a board: “Meditation is taught here – Free.”

  • You do not need a big building. Even one room in your house can become a centre.
  • Teach whatever you know — ABC or A to Z.
  • When neighbours also meditate, the whole atmosphere becomes divine and protects your growth.
  • Every home should become a centre; every centre is a temple.
  • This is how the movement spreads across the entire earth. The final goal: all 7 billion souls become masters of meditation.
Practical: Decide a day and time every week when anyone can come to your home (or a fixed place) for free Anapanasati introduction. Start small and be consistent.
Remember: “I am available. Meditation is taught here free.”
Principle 18

Write and Publish Your Experiences

Patriji waited one full year for this last principle. Whoever builds pyramids and writes their experiences has a confirmed seat in Satyaloka.

  • Your book will live even after you leave the body and will guide thousands.
  • When you feel you have become a complete Master, write your book.
  • Every Master should eventually document their transformation and insights.
  • Writing clarifies your own understanding and becomes a gift to future seekers.
Practical: Start a simple notebook or digital file today. After important sittings or realisations, write a few lines. Over years this becomes your book.
Remember: “My experiences are not only for me. One day they will help many.”

Daily Self-Reflection Checklist

Every night before sleep, quickly scan these points:

  1. Did I sit in pure Anapanasati today?
  2. Did I read at least a little true spiritual literature?
  3. Did I share or listen to any genuine experience?
  4. Did I practise silence and pure speech?
  5. Did I keep a smile and say “Yes” to service?
  6. Did I remain vegetarian (no garlic)?
  7. Did I cut negative thoughts and stand as a Master?
  8. Did I take responsibility for my own problems?
  9. Did I remain ordinary, loving and available?
  10. Did I move one small step closer to starting or supporting a centre / writing my experiences?

Where I slipped today → I will improve tomorrow.
This single habit keeps the pure-pearl quality alive.
