Principle 1 of 18 · Foundation of the Path

Right Way of Anapanasati – Daily Meditation

Meditation is compulsory every day — come rain, come shine, come misery, come pleasure. From the age of five until the last moment of leaving the body, every person must sit, close the eyes and rest within the Self.

Close the eyes. The outer world disappears. Rest in the Self through the breath.

Core Meaning

Anapanasati is the right way of meditation. Among 108 methods, it is the highest. It is Raja Yoga. It is different from Pranayama. Your own natural breath is your Guru. It alone takes you from mind-level to soul-level.

In-Depth Points

  • Sit in any comfortable, stable posture — chair, sofa or floor. Interlock the fingers of both hands. Cross the feet if possible.
  • Close the eyes. Do not think of any form, deity or shape.
  • Simply observe the normal, natural, continuous breath. No forcing, no controlling, no counting, no visualisation.
  • Anapanasati is Sukhamaya Pranayama — the pleasant, effortless observation of the breath that already flows.
  • Forced breathing (rechaka, kumbhaka, deep inhalations) is physical work and causes energy loss. Natural breath gives energy.
  • When the eyes are open, the outer world (non-self) is present. When the eyes are closed, the world disappears from perception. Now you must see within.
  • As thoughts arise, cut them gently: “Correct, correct, correct” and return to the breath. This is chitta-vritti-nirodha.
  • The more the mind empties, the more the soul receives solace, clarity, energy and higher vision.
  • Daily meditation prevents unnecessary interference in others’ lives and thereby reduces karma.
  • Without daily pure Anapanasati, all other 17 principles remain incomplete.
  • Start from age five and continue until the last breath of the body.
Practical daily practice
• Fix a definite time every day and never skip.
• Begin with whatever minutes you can. Gradually increase.
• Prefer early morning or before sleep, but any consistent time is good.
• If possible, sit under or inside a pyramid.
• The world is a stage; meditation is the time you step off the stage and return to the Self.
Remember 1: “My breath is my Guru. Today I will sit with my breath, no matter what.”
Remember 2: “Eyes closed = outer world gone. Now I rest in the Self.”
Remember 3: “No forcing. Natural breath only. Energy is gained, not lost.”
Remember 4: “Without today’s sitting, the whole path is incomplete.”
Self-reflection (ask yourself tonight)
  1. Did I sit in pure Anapanasati today — even for a few minutes?
  2. Did I force the breath or simply observe it?
  3. When thoughts came, did I cut them and return to the breath?
  4. Did I treat this sitting as compulsory, or optional?
  5. What will I do tomorrow to make the sitting deeper or longer?
