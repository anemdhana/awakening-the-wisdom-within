Express Your Love to the Elders in Your Family!

What this sankalp is pointing to

Love for elders is often assumed: “They know I care.” Yet what is not expressed can remain unfelt. This sankalp asks for a simple, concrete movement — to let the love become visible and audible while they are still here to receive it.

It is not about grand gestures. A call, a few true words, a touch, a patient listening, a small act of care — these are enough when they come from a real heart.

Why it matters

Elders carry the weight of years, of unfinished conversations, of the quiet fear of becoming a burden. When love is only in the mind and never reaches them, both sides remain slightly alone.

Expressing love is also a form of gratitude — for the life they gave, the sacrifices we may never fully know, and the simple fact that they are still present. Maitri begins at home. If it cannot be practised with those who raised us or walked with us, it remains only an idea.

How to practise today

1. Choose one elder — parent, grandparent, uncle, aunt, or anyone who has stood in that place in your life.

2. Do one concrete act today: a message, a call, a visit, a shared meal, or simply sitting with them without rushing.

3. Speak one true sentence of appreciation or love. It does not need to be perfect. It needs to be real.

4. Listen. Sometimes the deepest expression of love is to receive their words, their stories, or their silence without impatience.

5. If the relationship is difficult, begin with a small, honest step. Expression does not require that everything be resolved; it only requires that the heart not stay completely closed.

One gentle reminder

Time does not wait. The chance to express love to an elder is limited. Doing it today is not a duty imposed from outside — it is the natural movement of a heart that remembers what matters.

अपने परिवार के बुज़ुर्गों के प्रति अपना प्रेम व्यक्त करें — so that love does not remain only a feeling inside you, but becomes a lived warmth they can receive.
