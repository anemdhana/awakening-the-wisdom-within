Day 1 – Foundations

  • Anyone can start Anapanasati at any time — no special initiation required for this path.
  • Minimum starting duration ≈ your age in minutes.
  • Only one non-negotiable rule for real connection: vegetarian living.
  • No need for sannyasa or leaving family. Stay in family and meditate.

Day 2 – Spiritual Science (Six Laws)

  2. Punarjanma Siddhanta – The soul takes many births.
  3. Karma Siddhanta – Whatever we give returns.
  4. Purogamana Siddhanta – The soul only progresses upward.
  5. Yoga Siddhanta – Meditation is the catalyst that clears karma fast.
  6. Aneka Aneka / Law of Infinity – Infinite masters, worlds and help beyond the physical.

Day 3 – Patanjali Yoga Shastra (with Premnath Sir)

  • Ashtanga path: Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana, Samadhi.
  • According to Patriji, Yama-Niyama come last — they become natural after regular Dhyana and Samadhi.
  • Sthira Sukha Asana – stable + comfortable. Body movements are natural.
  • Three energy locks: eyes closed (~80 %), hands locked (~10 %), legs crossed (~10 %).
  • Samadhi = receiving clear answers from within.

Jnana Navaratnas – The Nine Gems of Knowledge (Simple Guide)

These nine truths keep a meditator steady when practice feels up and down. One day sitting is easy; the next day the mind is restless. The gems act as stabilisers. Patriji says every Pyramid Master should keep them in the mind at every moment.

Below they are grouped into three simple categories, with one clear meaning and one practical daily-life example for each.

Category A – Who I Really Am

1. Nene Anta — “I am everything”

Simple meaning: The real “I” is not the limited person with name and job. The real “I” is the same consciousness that fills everything (Aham Brahmasmi).

Daily life example: When someone criticises you at work or at home, instead of collapsing into “I am a failure,” quietly remember: “The real me is not this temporary story. I am the awareness that is watching.” The sting softens.

2. Jivude Devudu — “The living being is God”

Simple meaning: The one living in this body is not separate from the Divine. There is no “small me” and “big God somewhere else.”

Daily life example: Before speaking harshly to a child, spouse or colleague, pause and feel: “The same light is in them too.” The words naturally become kinder.

3. Dehame Devalayam — “The body is the temple”

Simple meaning: This body is a sacred temple; the soul lives inside it. Treat the body with respect, not as dirty or worthless.

Daily life example: When the body feels tired or in pain during meditation, instead of fighting it, say gently: “My dear body, thank you. Please relax.” Also choose clean food (vegetarian) and rest as care for the temple.

Category B – How I Practise

4. Shvase Guru — “Breath is the Guru”

Simple meaning: The natural breath itself is the teacher. Everything needed for the path is learned by staying with the breath.

Daily life example: In a traffic jam or a heated argument, bring attention to one or two natural breaths. The breath becomes the immediate guru that brings you back to calm without needing any book or person.

5. Samayame Sadhana — “Time itself is the practice”

Simple meaning: The more time you give to sitting with the breath, the more the practice deepens. Time given = sadhana.

Daily life example: Fix a minimum daily slot (e.g. age in minutes, or 30–40 minutes) the way you fix brushing teeth. Even on a busy day, protect that slot. Mastery comes from repeated time, not from waiting for a “perfect” day.

6. Sahaname Pragati — “Patience is progress”

Simple meaning: Progress is measured by the ability to stay with the practice even when it feels dull, difficult or empty.

Daily life example: One evening the mind says “Nothing is happening, let’s skip.” You still sit for the full time. That single decision is already progress — more than a “good” sitting followed by quitting the next day.

Category C – How Knowledge Grows & How I Live

7. Anubhavame Jnanam — “Experience is knowledge”

Simple meaning: Real knowledge is what you have lived and felt, not only what you have read or heard.

Daily life example: After a week of daily sitting you notice sleep is better or anger rises less. That felt change is jnana. Write it down or simply notice it — it is more valuable than ten more lectures.

8. Daname Dharmam — “Giving is dharma”

Simple meaning: True dharma is giving — time, help, wisdom, kindness — without calculating return.

Daily life example: Share a simple meditation tip with a stressed friend, help someone without announcing it, or give full attention to a child for ten minutes. That free giving is dharma in action.

9. Dharmame Punyam — “Dharma is merit (punya)”

Simple meaning: Every act of right living (non-violence, meditation, honest giving) becomes positive merit that supports the soul’s upward journey.

Daily life example: Choosing vegetarian food at a party, sitting for meditation instead of scrolling, or speaking truthfully when it is uncomfortable — each of these is a small deposit of punya. Over time the load of the path becomes lighter.

How to use the nine gems: When the mind complains “meditation is not working,” recall the matching gem. Restless body → Dehame Devalayam. Urge to quit → Sahaname Pragati. Feeling small and separate → Nene Anta / Jivude Devudu. Need for a teacher in the moment → Shvase Guru. Keep them simple and close; they will keep the practice steady.

Practical Attitude for Steady Practice

  • Do not expect the same experience every day.
  • When the mind says “meditation is not happening,” reply gently: “That’s okay. I will still sit.”
  • Make a strong inner resolve: “For the next half hour I will not open my eyes — no matter what.”
  • Talk to the body with the same love you show a close friend.
  • Increase duration gradually: 30 → 40 → 50 → 60 minutes and beyond.

Today’s Guided Sitting (~40 minutes)

Priyanka guided a longer group sitting. Key instructions:

  1. Sit comfortably in Sthira Sukha Asana. Lock hands, cross legs.
  2. Close the eyes. Strong determination: eyes will not open until the end.
  3. Relax the whole body — legs, back, shoulders, face. Speak kindly to the body.
  4. Take three slow, deep natural breaths.
  5. No mantra, no forced thought. Simply stay with the natural breath. When thoughts come, return to the breath.
  6. Hold a simple intention for wellness and joy.
  7. Invite the presence of the Gurus and Light Masters to sit with you.
  8. If body pain, vibration or unusual sensations appear — do not stop them. Continue.
  9. In the final minutes offer deep gratitude.
  10. Slowly bring the hands over the eyes and open with a soft smile.

Homework

  • Continue daily practice. Increase sitting time a little every few days.
  • Keep the vegetarian foundation firm.
  • Memorise the nine gems in the three simple categories and recall the matching one whenever the mind complains.
  • No class on Sunday; continue on your own and rejoin on Monday.
