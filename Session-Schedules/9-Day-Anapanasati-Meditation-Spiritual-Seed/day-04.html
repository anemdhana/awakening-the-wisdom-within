Revision · Jnana Navaratnas · Steady Practice

Why Revision Matters

Priyanka began by reminding everyone that the goal is not to hear everything and forget. Even if only five points out of a hundred are retained and lived, that is enough. Knowledge must sit in the head and become part of daily life.

She also clarified practical points: classes remain open for nine days so late-comers can still join. WhatsApp Channel (not multiple groups) is the main place for updates. Video-on helps connection and recognition.

Quick Revision – Days 1 to 3

Day 1 – Foundations

  • Anyone can start Anapanasati at any time — no special initiation, no guru-mantra required for this path.
  • Minimum starting duration ≈ your age in minutes.
  • Only one non-negotiable rule for real connection: vegetarian living. Without it many people eventually disconnect even after attending classes.
  • No need for sannyasa, special clothes, or leaving family. Stay in family and meditate.

Day 2 – Spiritual Science (Six Laws)

  1. Atma Siddhanta – We are the Soul.
  2. Punarjanma Siddhanta – The soul takes many births.
  3. Karma Siddhanta – Whatever we give returns.
  4. Purogamana Siddhanta – The soul only progresses upward.
  5. Yoga Siddhanta – Meditation is the catalyst that clears karma fast.
  6. Aneka Aneka / Law of Infinity – Infinite masters, worlds and help beyond the physical.

Day 3 – Patanjali Yoga Shastra (with Premnath Sir)

  • Ashtanga path: Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana, Samadhi.
  • According to Patriji, Yama-Niyama come last, not first — they become natural after regular Dhyana and Samadhi.
  • Sthira Sukha Asana – stable + comfortable. Body movements are natural; do not control them.
  • Three energy locks: eyes closed (~80 %), hands locked (~10 %), legs crossed (~10 %).
  • Samadhi = receiving clear answers from within.

Jnana Navaratnas – The Nine Gems of Knowledge

Today’s main topic: Jnana Navaratnas (ಜ್ಞಾನ ನವರತ್ನಗಳು) — the nine precious truths that every sadhaka, every meditator, every person walking the spiritual path must carry with them at all times.

Why are they needed?

The meditation graph is not a straight rising line. It has ups and downs, energy shifts, sudden changes in experience. One day the sitting feels wonderful; the next day the mind is restless or the body is heavy. There is no rule that today’s experience must repeat tomorrow.

Beginners often message after one or two days: “It’s not happening… I can’t even sit…” This is normal. The mind plays games. The body complains. Fear or boredom appears. If we do not carry the right understanding, we stop.

Therefore a sadhaka must keep these nine gems of knowledge close to the heart. They act as stabilisers when the graph goes down. Priyanka asked everyone to practise them the way small children practise — so that the ideas sit firmly in the mind and come automatically when needed.

(The detailed enumeration of each of the nine gems continues in the live session. The core teaching is that these truths must travel with the practitioner every single day.)

Practical Attitude for Steady Practice

  • Do not expect the same experience every day.
  • When the mind says “meditation is not happening”, reply gently: “That’s okay, my dear mind. I will still sit.”
  • Make a strong inner resolve: “For the next half hour (or whatever duration) I will not open my eyes — no matter what.”
  • Talk to the body with the same love you show a close friend: “My dear body, thank you so much. Please relax.”
  • Shoulders carry all our responsibilities — consciously loosen them.
  • Even the eyebrows often stay knotted; relax the face completely.
  • Increase duration gradually: 30 → 40 → 50 → 60 minutes and beyond.

Today’s Guided Sitting (~40 minutes)

Priyanka guided a longer group sitting. Key instructions:

  1. Sit comfortably in Sthira Sukha Asana. Lock hands, cross legs.
  2. Close the eyes. Strong determination: eyes will not open until the end.
  3. Relax the whole body — legs, back, shoulders, face, eyebrows. Speak kindly to the body.
  4. Take three slow, deep natural breaths, inviting divine power in and releasing the unwanted on the out-breath.
  5. No mantra, no forced thought. Simply stay with the natural breath. When thoughts come, return to the breath.
  6. Hold the intention: “May this meditation give me physical, mental and spiritual wellness. May I remain in the highest state both materially and spiritually. May my life flow in joy.”
  7. Invite the presence of Hanuman, Patriji, Paramashiva, Mahavatar Babaji, Angels and Light-world Masters to sit with you.
  8. If body pain, vibration, rotation or unusual sensations appear — do not stop them. They are natural. Continue.
  9. In the final minutes offer deep gratitude to the Gurus, Masters, parents, the Universe and your own body.
  10. Slowly bring the hands over the eyes and open with a soft smile.

Many participants sat for a full 40 minutes — a clear sign that capacity is already growing.

Homework

  • Continue daily practice. Increase the sitting time a little every few days.
  • Keep the vegetarian foundation firm.
  • Whenever the mind complains, remember the Jnana Navaratnas and the fact that ups and downs are part of the path.
  • No class on Sunday; continue on your own and rejoin on Monday.
