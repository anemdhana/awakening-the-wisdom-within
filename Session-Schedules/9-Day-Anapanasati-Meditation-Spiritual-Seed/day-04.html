Why Revision Matters

Priyanka began by reminding everyone that the goal is not to hear everything and forget. Even if only five points out of a hundred are retained and lived, that is enough. Knowledge must sit in the head and become part of daily life.

She also clarified practical points: classes remain open for nine days so late-comers can still join. WhatsApp Channel (not multiple groups) is the main place for updates. Video-on helps connection and recognition.

Quick Revision – Days 1 to 3

Day 1 – Foundations

  • Anyone can start Anapanasati at any time — no special initiation, no guru-mantra required for this path.
  • Minimum starting duration ≈ your age in minutes.
  • Only one non-negotiable rule for real connection: vegetarian living. Without it many people eventually disconnect even after attending classes.
  • No need for sannyasa, special clothes, or leaving family. Stay in family and meditate.

Day 2 – Spiritual Science (Six Laws)

  1. Atma Siddhanta – We are the Soul.
  2. Punarjanma Siddhanta – The soul takes many births.
  3. Karma Siddhanta – Whatever we give returns.
  4. Purogamana Siddhanta – The soul only progresses upward.
  5. Yoga Siddhanta – Meditation is the catalyst that clears karma fast.
  6. Aneka Aneka / Law of Infinity – Infinite masters, worlds and help beyond the physical.

Day 3 – Patanjali Yoga Shastra (with Premnath Sir)

  • Ashtanga path: Yama, Niyama, Asana, Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana, Samadhi.
  • According to Patriji, Yama-Niyama come last, not first — they become natural after regular Dhyana and Samadhi.
  • Sthira Sukha Asana – stable + comfortable. Body movements are natural; do not control them.
  • Three energy locks: eyes closed (~80 %), hands locked (~10 %), legs crossed (~10 %).
  • Samadhi = receiving clear answers from within.

Jnana Navaratnas – The Nine Gems of Knowledge

Today’s main topic: Jnana Navaratnas (ಜ್ಞಾನ ನವರತ್ನಗಳು / పిరమిడ్ జ్ఞాన నవరత్నాలు) — the nine precious truths that every sadhaka, every meditator, every person walking the spiritual path must carry at all times.

Why are they needed?

The meditation graph is not a straight rising line. It has ups and downs, energy shifts, sudden changes in experience. One day the sitting feels wonderful; the next day the mind is restless or the body is heavy. There is no rule that today’s experience must repeat tomorrow.

Beginners often message after one or two days: “It’s not happening… I can’t even sit…” This is normal. The mind plays games. The body complains. Fear or boredom appears. If we do not carry the right understanding, we stop.

Therefore a sadhaka must keep these nine gems close to the heart. They act as stabilisers when the graph goes down. Priyanka asked everyone to practise them the way small children practise — so the ideas sit firmly and come automatically when needed.

Patriji says: every Pyramid Master must keep these nine gems in the mind at every moment. Not even for one second should they slip away. Newcomers should understand them thoroughly and keep deepening the understanding day by day.

1. Nene Anta — “I am everything”

  • The first Vedic mahavakya: Aham Brahmasmi — “I am Brahman.”
  • The “I” we keep saying is not the limited personality; it is the original consciousness itself.
  • Just as the space inside a pot (ghata-akasha) and the space outside (chida-akasha) are not two different spaces, the individual soul and the infinite are not two.
  • Those who only understand this as theory are Vedanta-murtis; those who experience it are Yoga-murtis.

2. Jivude Devudu — “The living being is God”

  • The one who is here as the living being (jiva) is the same as the Divine (deva). There is no duality — only non-duality.
  • One who knows “I am Brahman” lives ananda-maya life. One who thinks “I am only a human and God is somewhere else” remains unhappy.
  • Fingers look separate, but their base is one. The base is non-dual; the fingers appear dual. Everything is already within this living being.

3. Dehame Devalayam — “The body is the temple”

  • Adi Shankaracharya: “Deho devalaya prokta, jivo devo sanatanah” — the body is declared the temple; the living being within is the eternal God.
  • From nail to hair-tip this body is a sacred temple. The jiva resides in this temple; therefore the body is pure.
  • Do not regard the body as impure, low or worthless. Feeling the sacredness of the body and experiencing the soul seated inside is true spirituality.

4. Shvase Guru — “Breath is the Guru”

  • To know all spiritual principles one must hold the breath.
  • Breath is part of Nature, yet it is not part of the body, mind or intellect in the ordinary sense. Breath is a portion of Parabrahman.
  • Whatever teaches us is called Guru. Here the breath itself teaches everything; therefore breath is the Guru.
  • When attention stays with this Guru, all spiritual secrets can be experienced. Being one with the breath is the Yoga principle. Practice begins with the breath.

5. Samayame Sadhana — “Time itself is the practice”

  • The more time you spend with your Guru (the breath), the more enlightened you become.
  • Minimum: sit for as many minutes as your age in years (15 years → 15 minutes; 40 years → 40 minutes).
  • Whatever field you give your full time to, you master: music → Balamuralikrishna; cricket → Sachin; painting → Leonardo; yoga → Patanjali; meditation → Buddha.
  • All Pyramid Masters have sat for hours and attained ananda. Time given is the sadhana.

6. Sahaname Pragati — “Patience is progress”

  • In any sadhana, patience is required. When you sit for meditation the cricket match on TV may call you. If you still choose to sit, you attain.
  • Progress depends on what we give priority. Many obstacles will come; if we continue with patience we reach the goal.
  • Without patience no vidya — music or meditation — can be learned. Patience itself is the progress.

7. Anubhavame Jnanam — “Experience is knowledge”

  • Book knowledge or heard knowledge becomes living knowledge only when it is experienced.
  • Reading about the ocean is one thing; standing in the ocean is another. Direct experience turns information into jnana.
  • Every day of sitting adds experiential knowledge that no lecture can replace.

8. Daname Dharmam — “Giving is dharma”

  • True dharma is not only ritual; it is the act of giving — time, energy, wisdom, help, compassion — without expectation.
  • When we give freely, the good-karma box fills and the path becomes lighter for ourselves and others.

9. Dharmame Punyam — “Dharma is merit / punya”

  • Whatever is done as dharma (right action, giving, non-violence, meditation itself) becomes punya — the positive merit that supports the soul’s upward journey.
  • Living these nine gems daily is itself the highest dharma and the highest punya.

These nine statements are to be kept in the mind at every moment. When the meditation graph goes down, when the body complains, when the mind says “nothing is happening”, these gems bring the practitioner back to the correct understanding and keep the practice steady. This is how one becomes a true Pyramid Master.

Practical Attitude for Steady Practice

  • Do not expect the same experience every day.
  • When the mind says “meditation is not happening”, reply gently: “That’s okay, my dear mind. I will still sit.”
  • Make a strong inner resolve: “For the next half hour (or whatever duration) I will not open my eyes — no matter what.”
  • Talk to the body with the same love you show a close friend: “My dear body, thank you so much. Please relax.”
  • Shoulders carry all our responsibilities — consciously loosen them.
  • Even the eyebrows often stay knotted; relax the face completely.
  • Increase duration gradually: 30 → 40 → 50 → 60 minutes and beyond.

Today’s Guided Sitting (~40 minutes)

Priyanka guided a longer group sitting. Key instructions:

  1. Sit comfortably in Sthira Sukha Asana. Lock hands, cross legs.
  2. Close the eyes. Strong determination: eyes will not open until the end.
  3. Relax the whole body — legs, back, shoulders, face, eyebrows. Speak kindly to the body.
  4. Take three slow, deep natural breaths, inviting divine power in and releasing the unwanted on the out-breath.
  5. No mantra, no forced thought. Simply stay with the natural breath. When thoughts come, return to the breath.
  6. Hold the intention: “May this meditation give me physical, mental and spiritual wellness. May I remain in the highest state both materially and spiritually. May my life flow in joy.”
  7. Invite the presence of Hanuman, Patriji, Paramashiva, Mahavatar Babaji, Angels and Light-world Masters to sit with you.
  8. If body pain, vibration, rotation or unusual sensations appear — do not stop them. They are natural. Continue.
  9. In the final minutes offer deep gratitude to the Gurus, Masters, parents, the Universe and your own body.
  10. Slowly bring the hands over the eyes and open with a soft smile.

Many participants sat for a full 40 minutes — a clear sign that capacity is already growing.

Homework

  • Continue daily practice. Increase the sitting time a little every few days.
  • Keep the vegetarian foundation firm.
  • Memorise the nine Jnana Navaratnas with their meanings and recall them whenever the mind complains.
  • No class on Sunday; continue on your own and rejoin on Monday.
