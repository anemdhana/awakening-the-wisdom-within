9-Day Anapanasati Meditation • Day 6

Forgiveness · Healing the Inner Load

Continuing from Karma

Yesterday we looked at the Law of Karma — why the challenges of this present life appear, and how we can clear them. Today is the natural continuation of that teaching: Forgiveness (ಕ್ಷಮೆ).

Priyanka said this is one of the hardest areas for almost everyone. Many people wrote in the chat: “The person in front of me has been so cruel… how can I forgive them? Why should I forgive them?”

Two things work together in healing our life:

  1. Accepting our own karma with understanding, and
  2. Learning how to deal with those who have caused us pain.

Today’s class is completely focused on this one topic. Stay fully present with what is happening right now. Do not drift into past or future. This is a skill we must practise every day — whether the subject is spiritual or personal.

Two Aspects of Forgiveness

Forgiveness has two clear sides:

  1. Forgiving others — those who have hurt us.
  2. Forgiving ourselves — the many ways we have wounded and neglected our own body and life without even realising it.

We already know: this body is the temple. The Supreme (Parabrahman) lives inside it. Yet how often have we tortured this temple with self-criticism, self-rejection, and self-abandonment?

Until the quality of forgiveness grows inside us, it is almost impossible to love anyone unconditionally — including ourselves. Great teachers (Louise Hay, Joe Dispenza and others) all say the same thing: as long as the capacity to forgive is missing, true inner peace remains out of reach.

The Heavy Bags Analogy

Imagine two friends of the same age, going on the same journey to the same destination.

One friend is carrying many heavy bags — one on each shoulder, two more in the hands — full of clothes, old papers, unnecessary weight.

The other friend is walking lightly, hands free.

Who finds the journey easier?

Obviously the one without bags.

We too keep carrying heavy suitcases and boxes inside us — the pain, the resentment, the memories of what others did. We look at someone whose life appears peaceful and wonder, “She is just like me… how is her life so easy?” The difference is simply the load she is not carrying.

As long as we do not forgive, that box stays inside us. Wherever we go, we take it with us.

The Snake-Bite Analogy

A snake bites you. What do you do first?

Do you run after the snake shouting “Why did you bite me?” or do you immediately take treatment?

Of course you take treatment. You first care for yourself.

Yet when a person hurts us, we do the opposite. We keep replaying the scene hundreds of times: “Why did they do this to me?” We grow anger. We forget the self-care and the release that we actually need in that moment.

If you keep holding the snake and arguing with it, what is the use? The poison is already inside.

In the same way, we keep unhealed pains stored inside us instead of healing them.

Drinking Poison Every Day

When someone hurts you once, and you keep replaying that hurt a hundred times in your mind, you are the one giving yourself the pain a hundred times.

Holding hatred, resentment or anger toward anyone is exactly like drinking one spoon of poison every single day.

Observe your body when you are about to shout or retaliate. The body shakes. The voice trembles. Hands and legs tremble. The body is trying to tell you: “This is not right for you.”

Many chronic diseases — high blood pressure, diabetes, cancer and others — grow from long-held, unresolved emotional loads. The body keeps the score of what the mind refuses to release.

Important Truth about Karma and Forgiveness

Many people think: “If I forgive, that person will become free from their karma.”

That is not how the universe works.

If someone truly caused harm, they remain under the law of karma whether you forgive them or not. Their own actions will bring the results according to the cosmic law.

You forgive for your health, your peace, your joy.

Forgiveness is not a favour you do for the other person. It is the medicine you give yourself.

Guided Forgiveness Meditation

Priyanka guided a complete forgiveness sitting. The process has two main movements.

Part 1 – Asking Forgiveness from All Beings

Silently or in the heart, say:

“In this birth or in any previous births, through ignorance or through ego, if I have caused trouble, hurt, pain, deceit or even death to any living being — I ask forgiveness from all of them with my whole heart. Please forgive me.”

Feel that every being you may have harmed is standing in front of you, whether you can see them or not. They are receiving your words and your feeling. Ask from the depth of the heart. Hold their feet if the feeling arises. Ask purely, without using the logical mind.

Invite the presence of the pyramids of the world of Forgiveness and the world of Love (ಕ್ಷಮೆ ಲೋಕದ ಪಿರಮಿಡ್‌ಗಳು, ಪ್ರೇಮ ಲೋಕದ ಪಿರಮಿಡ್‌ಗಳು). Some people may see faces or past-life scenes — ask forgiveness according to what appears.

The more sincerely and completely you ask, the faster the release can come.

Part 2 – Forgiving Those Who Hurt Us

Bring one specific person who has caused deep pain into your mind. See them standing in front of you. Look into their eyes.

First, express everything you have been holding — the anger, the words you never said, the pain. If you feel like shouting, allow the feeling to move. Do not suppress.

When you feel you have expressed everything, tell that person clearly:

“Until today I did not feel ready to forgive you. But for the sake of my own health, my own peace and my own happiness, from this day I am ready to forgive you. I forgive you. I forgive you from my heart. From today you are free and I am free.”

Repeat with force and sincerity. Feel the words land.

You can do this one person at a time for every major wound.

If at times anger or hatred arises without any clear face, simply say:

“In this birth or in previous births, if any living being has caused me trouble, pain or harm through ignorance, today I forgive all of them from my heart. I forgive everyone.”

Cleansing Light

Invite the pure light of the Sun world (Surya Loka) to enter through the Sahasrara and completely cleanse the ethereal body and the emotional body of all residual pain-waves and old memory impressions.

Rest in that light for some time.

Closing

Offer deep gratitude to all the astral masters present, to the parents who gave this birth, to Brahmarshi Pitamaha Patriji, to this precious life, to this meditation, and to this body.

Slowly place both hands over the eyes. Count down 5-4-3-2-1. Open the eyes with a soft, beautiful smile.

Key Points to Remember

  • Forgiveness is primarily for our own health, peace and joy.
  • The other person’s karma continues according to the universal law, whether we forgive or not.
  • Carrying unresolved pain is like carrying heavy bags on a long journey or drinking poison daily.
  • When the body shakes or the voice trembles in anger, it is giving us a clear signal.
  • Self-forgiveness is as important as forgiving others — we have also hurt the temple of the body many times.
  • Do the process with full heart and complete trust, without the interference of the logical mind.

Homework

  • Continue daily Anapanasati practice.
  • Whenever an old hurt rises, gently return to the forgiveness process rather than replaying the story.
  • Keep the vegetarian foundation firm.
  • Share any experiences on the WhatsApp channel if you wish.
