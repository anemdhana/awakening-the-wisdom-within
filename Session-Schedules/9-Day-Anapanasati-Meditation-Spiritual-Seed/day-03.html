Patanjali Yoga Shastra – Simplified

Everyone knows Buddha discovered Anapanasati, yet long before him Maharshi Patanjali systematised the science of meditation. The classical eight limbs are:

Yama – Niyama – Asana – Pranayama – Pratyahara – Dharana – Dhyana – Samadhi

Patriji’s unique and liberating insight (rarely taught elsewhere): Yama and Niyama come at the end, not at the beginning. They are the natural fruits that appear once a person regularly reaches Dhyana and Samadhi. Trying to force moral disciplines first often creates struggle. When the inner state is purified through meditation, Yama-Niyama become spontaneous.

As the official PSSM teaching states: for Yama-Niyama to become natural in physical life, there is no other refuge except continuous yoga (meditation) and spiritual knowledge.

Yama – The Five Restraints (as taught in Patriji / PSSM tradition)

Yama means control / mastery over the basic spiritual principles of living. Patanjali gave five Yamas. In the language of Patriji’s movement they are understood as:

  1. Satya (Truth) — Always speaking the truth of the soul. Not merely “not lying,” but living and speaking from inner truth.
  2. Ahimsa (Non-violence) — Completely abandoning violent actions. This includes the foundation of vegetarian living: not causing pain to animals. Ahimsa is the root of the other restraints.
  3. Brahmacharya (Middle Path) — Always following the middle path. In PSSM this is not only celibacy in the narrow sense; it is balanced living — neither extreme indulgence nor extreme denial — while remaining in family, work and society.
  4. Asteya (Non-stealing / Non-jealousy) — Freedom from jealousy towards others’ property and possessions. Not coveting what belongs to another.
  5. Aparigraha (Non-possessiveness) — Not taking what is not needed, even if others offer it. Living with only what is necessary; no unnecessary accumulation.

These are not rules to be forced by will-power alone. When daily Anapanasati deepens, the mind naturally becomes less violent, more truthful, more content, and less grasping. Vegetarianism (Shakahara) is the practical daily expression of Ahimsa in this path.

Niyama – The Five Observances (as taught in Patriji / PSSM tradition)

Niyama means the necessary daily activities of spiritual life. Patanjali gave five Niyamas. In the PSSM formulation:

  1. Shaucha (Purity) — Keeping the body and surroundings clean and pure. Outer cleanliness supports inner cleanliness.
  2. Santosha (Contentment / Joy) — Keeping the mind always joyful and content. Not depending on external conditions for happiness.
  3. Svadhyaya (Self-study) — Continuously reading good spiritual books. Study of the self through the words of masters and through one’s own experience.
  4. Tapas (Austerity / Discipline) — Gradually reducing excess in sleep, food and other habits. Simple living that supports deeper meditation.
  5. Ishvara Pranidhana (Surrender to the Divine) — Remaining always in the feeling “Everything is filled with the Divine / Ishvara.” Offering the fruits of action and resting in that awareness.

Again, these become natural when meditation is regular. Forced discipline without inner change creates tension. Meditation first; then the Niyamas flower by themselves.

Asana – Sthira Sukha Asana

Most people think “Yoga = complicated asanas”. Premnath Sir clarified: those are only physical postures for flexibility. True Asana for meditation is what Patriji named Sthira Sukha Asana — stable and comfortable.

  • Sthira = the mind does not run to past or future; it stays present. Also means sitting for a decided length of time (e.g. one full hour) without opening the eyes.
  • Sukha = the body is comfortable. No strain.

You may sit on a chair, on the floor, on the bed with legs stretched or crossed — whatever allows long, relaxed sitting. Body movements, rotations, spontaneous mudras and slight bending are completely natural during deep meditation. Do not control or suppress them. Energy is flowing; the body is adjusting.

Even bedridden patients who cannot sit can meditate lying down. But for those who can sit, sitting is preferred because the fruit is greater.

The only non-negotiable rule: never open the eyes until the sitting is complete.

The Three Energy Locks (Bandhas)

When thoughts stop, cosmic energy enters powerfully. To keep that energy inside:

  • Eyes closed → prevents ~80 % leakage
  • Hands locked (fingers interlocked) → prevents ~10 % leakage
  • Legs crossed or one foot over the other → prevents ~10 % leakage

Together these three locks retain almost 100 % of the energy. This is why the body sometimes moves, sways or feels pressure — pure energy is circulating. There is nothing to fear.

Pranayama, Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana

In this path Pranayama is simply the natural observation of the breath (Anapanasati) — no forceful retention (kumbhaka) is required.

Dhyana is the journey of the subtle bodies (sukshma, karana, mahakarana sharira). When the eyes are closed yet you clearly see the Himalayas, Mahavatar Babaji, or any divine scene, it is not imagination — the subtle body has travelled and is reporting through the divine eye (divya netra darshana). The physical body stays here; the inner journey continues.

A beautiful example given: a kite. The string is the breath. As long as you hold the string the kite soars. The moment you completely leave the string, the kite falls. In the same way, continuous gentle awareness of the breath keeps the consciousness elevated.

Samadhi – Answers from Within

Samadhi is the state in which answers arise from inside — pure Atma intuition. You no longer need to ask outside people for every decision.

Premnath Sir shared a real incident: on a bus a passenger was tense about his sister’s proposed marriage because someone had written that the boy was not good. Sir asked him to close his eyes, sit for one-and-a-half hours and put the question inside. When the man opened his eyes he said two messages had come: “The boy is good” and “Write down this phone number and address.” Six months later the man returned overjoyed — the marriage was happy. One year later the whole family wanted to learn meditation. That is the practical fruit of Samadhi.

God / Atman is not only outside; the same divinity is within. When we go deep in meditation we can ask and receive directly.

Experiment – Longer Sittings

Sir strongly encouraged experimentation. One lady sat for 72 continuous hours. Another, after hearing about it, sat 12 hours the very next day in the King Chamber at Kailasapuram and reported that many karmas and past births became clear. Why not try 12, 24 or 36 hours when the opportunity and guidance are available? Listening and clapping is not enough — implement.

He also remarked with a smile: “In our society those who come late or appear lazy are often great sadhakas from previous births. There are no real newcomers here.”

Today’s Guided Sitting

After the teaching the group sat in silence with soft Om / music.

  • Sit comfortably in Sthira Sukha Asana
  • Lock the hands, cross the legs
  • Close the eyes
  • No questions, no analysis — simply relax and remain with the breath
  • At the end, slowly bring the hands over the eyes and open with gratitude

Key Takeaways from Day 3

  • Yama-Niyama are the result of Dhyana and Samadhi, not the starting requirement. Meditation + spiritual knowledge make them natural.
  • The five Yamas (Satya, Ahimsa, Brahmacharya as middle path, Asteya, Aparigraha) and five Niyamas (Shaucha, Santosha, Svadhyaya, Tapas, Ishvara Pranidhana) are lived in the PSSM understanding given by Patriji.
  • Ahimsa in daily life includes vegetarian living (Shakahara).
  • Asana = Sthira + Sukha. Comfort first, then duration.
  • Three locks (eyes, hands, legs) conserve the cosmic energy that enters in thought-free states.
  • Natural breath awareness is the Pranayama of this path.
  • Dhyana includes subtle-body travel and divine vision.
  • Samadhi gives direct inner answers.
  • Longer experimental sittings accelerate clarity and karma clearance.
  • You are not a beginner — you are a returning sadhaka.
