Patanjali Yoga Shastra · Sthira Sukha Asana · Samadhi

Premnath Sir (working for more than 30 years under Patriji’s sankalpa of Dhyana Jagat – Pyramid Jagat – Shakahara Jagat) joined the session. He travels across Karnataka, India and abroad spreading meditation, vegetarianism and pyramid science.

Why Do We Need Dhyana Shastra? (Patriji’s Teaching)

Patriji explains the purpose of the science of meditation in three clear benefits:

  1. Friendship with all beings — Shiva wears the snake; Ganesha takes the mouse as vehicle. The stories teach us to live in friendship with every creature, not in fear or contempt.
  2. Mastery over our own life — mastery over actions, speech and thoughts. Without meditation practice we have no real control; words “just come out,” anger rises by itself. The one who does not practise is not a master of his life.
  3. Knowing “Aham Brahmasmi” — experiencing that we are a portion of the original consciousness of creation. Books say it; only practice makes it known from within.

Correct address is needed to reach the destination. Incomplete shastra produces incomplete practice. Complete shastra produces complete practice. That is why this teaching is given.

The Correct Order of the Eight Limbs (Patriji’s Correction)

Classical list printed in books:

Yama – Niyama – Asana – Pranayama – Pratyahara – Dharana – Dhyana – Samadhi

Patriji’s decisive correction: Yama and Niyama come last, not first. Treat the book order’s first two as the seventh and eighth. The living order of practice is:

  1. Asana
  2. Pranayama
  3. Pratyahara
  4. Dharana
  5. Dhyana
  6. Samadhi
  7. Yama
  8. Niyama

If a person already has perfect Yama-Niyama, he does not need meditation. The one who has defects and impurities needs meditation the most — just as the sick person needs medicine. Start from whatever condition you are in. Do not wait until you become perfectly pure before sitting.

Putting Yama-Niyama at the beginning has confused generations: people say “I still get angry, I still have desires, so I cannot meditate.” Patriji reverses this: meditate first; Yama-Niyama become natural afterwards.

1. Asana – Sthira Sukha Asana

Only one formula is needed: Sthira Sukha Asana — stable and comfortable.

  • Chair is perfectly fine. Floor is not compulsory.
  • Wall support is fine at the beginning.
  • Whatever posture allows long, relaxed sitting is correct.
  • If you cannot sit, lie down (especially elders). Those who can sit should sit.

Asana-jaya (mastery of asana) = ability to remain in one posture for about three hours without discomfort. We are not watching a film; we are simply sitting comfortably for a long time.

Do not worry about exact leg position. Sit as you like, as long as it is comfortable. Body movements, rotations and spontaneous mudras during deep sitting are natural — energy is adjusting. Do not suppress them.

Only non-negotiable rule: never open the eyes until the decided time is complete.

2. Pranayama – Sukha Pranayama (Anapanasati)

After stabilising the body, stabilise attention on the breath.

Patriji is absolute: no mantra, no japa, no forced kumbhaka, no breath retention. No Om, no Gayatri, no spoken sound. Mouth remains silent.

Sukha Pranayama = watching the natural breath exactly as it is. Just as asana must be comfortable, pranayama must be comfortable. Forced, uncomfortable breath control is rejected.

Gautama Buddha called this Anapanasati. In the Pyramid Spiritual Societies Movement it is called “attention on the breath” or “Shvasa Dhyana.” This alone is the pranayama of the path.

The Three Energy Locks

When thoughts stop, cosmic energy enters. To keep it inside:

  • Eyes closed → ~80 % of energy is retained
  • Hands locked (fingers interlocked) → ~10 %
  • Legs crossed or one foot over the other → ~10 %

Together almost 100 % is conserved. Body movements and pressure are signs of pure energy circulating — nothing to fear.

3. Pratyahara – Turning Inward

When the outer world (family, house, name, position, MLA/MP status) loses its grip, attention turns inward. That inward turning is Pratyahara.

“Ahara” means food/input to the senses. “Prati” means the opposite direction. Instead of taking input only from outside, the senses begin to receive from inside.

Subtle lights, colours, mountains, valleys, departed parents, divine forms may appear. A small white point of light may grow and fill the field of vision. These are not imagination; they are the beginning of inner vision. Continuous, unbroken practice is required — the same steady effort needed to master music, cricket or a language.

4. Dharana – Concentration on What Appears

Dharana is one-pointed attention on whatever arises inwardly — a light, a sound, a fragrance, a form, a scene.

Ordinary books say “repeat a mantra and concentrate on it.” Those who have actually practised write differently: whatever appears through the third eye or inner hearing, stay with that. When past karmas are sufficiently purified (Vishuddha), the Ajna centre opens more fully. Before that, opening is difficult. Purification through practice is the way.

5. Dhyana – Journey of the Subtle Bodies

Dhyana is the travel of the subtle bodies (sukshma, karana, mahakarana). Roughly 80 % of the subtle body can leave while 20 % remains to sustain the physical body. The journey that happens with this group of subtle bodies is Dhyana.

When you clearly see Himalayas, Babaji or any divine scene with closed eyes, it is not fantasy — the subtle body has travelled and is reporting through the divine eye.

6. Samadhi – Receiving Answers Directly

Samadhi is the state in which answers arise from inside without intermediary.

  • Savikalpa Samadhi — some answers come, some doubts remain.
  • Nirvikalpa Samadhi — no doubts remain; all needed answers are clear.

No middleman is required — not even a book or a living teacher for the final knowing. One can know directly where a departed mother has gone, what decision is correct, what the next step is. This is the practical fruit of Samadhi.

Premnath Sir’s bus incident remains a living example: a man received clear inner guidance about his sister’s marriage after one-and-a-half hours of sitting; the result later confirmed the guidance.

Stages of the Practitioner (Patriji)

  • Established in Pranayama → Dhyani
  • Established in Pratyahara → Yogi
  • Established in Dharana → Rishi
  • Established in Dhyana → Raja-rishi
  • Established in Samadhi → Brahmarshi

Health is the greatest fortune; ananda is the goal of life. Without meditation there is neither lasting health nor lasting ananda.

After Samadhi – Yama and Niyama Become Natural

Only after the practice matures into Samadhi do Yama and Niyama become the person’s natural qualities and life principles.

Yamas (now effortless)

  • Brahmacharya — not merely physical celibacy. It means never identifying oneself as the body; always experiencing oneself as the soul. The one who has reached Samadhi is always a brahmachari in this true sense.
  • Satya — whatever is spoken becomes true; there is no falsehood.
  • Ahimsa — when one experiences “I am in all,” violence towards any being becomes impossible.
  • Asteya — no jealousy towards another’s possessions.
  • Aparigraha — not taking what is unnecessary, even if offered.

Niyamas (now effortless)

  • Shaucha — real cleanliness is experienced and lived.
  • Santosha — continuous joy; even difficult news is met with equanimity.
  • Tapas — natural reduction of excess sleep and food; body needs become light.
  • Svadhyaya — any spiritual book is understood at once because the state of Samadhi has already been reached. One reading of the Gita can be enough.
  • Ishvara Pranidhana — seeing divinity everywhere becomes natural only when one has first realised “I am That.”

Therefore: forget forcing Yama-Niyama at the beginning. They arrive by themselves at the end.

Only One Meditation Method

Patriji is clear: there is only one true method of meditation for the whole of humanity — attention on the natural breath. Different names and cultures do not create different ultimate methods. Just as the world is becoming one global village, the science of meditation is one. The pyramid itself symbolises many at the base and one at the peak.

Experiment – Longer Sittings

Premnath Sir strongly encouraged experimentation. One lady sat 72 continuous hours. Another sat 12 hours the next day and many karmas and past births became clear. Listening is not enough — implement. Longer experimental sittings accelerate clarity.

Today’s Guided Sitting

  • Sit comfortably in Sthira Sukha Asana
  • Lock the hands, cross the legs
  • Close the eyes
  • No questions, no analysis — simply remain with the natural breath
  • At the end, slowly bring the hands over the eyes and open with gratitude

Key Takeaways from Day 3

  • Purpose of Dhyana Shastra: friendship with all beings, mastery over life, knowing Aham Brahmasmi.
  • Correct practice order: Asana → Pranayama → Pratyahara → Dharana → Dhyana → Samadhi → then Yama → Niyama.
  • Yama-Niyama are fruits, not entry tickets. Start from wherever you are.
  • Asana = Sthira + Sukha only. Comfort first.
  • Pranayama = natural breath observation only. No mantra, no forced retention.
  • Three energy locks conserve the cosmic energy that enters in silence.
  • Pratyahara, Dharana, Dhyana, Samadhi unfold through continuous practice.
  • After Samadhi, Yama-Niyama become natural character.
  • There is only one meditation method for the whole world.
