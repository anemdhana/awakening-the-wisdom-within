Seven Mirrors of Radiant Relationships · Day 5

Mirror 5 · Mother & Father

We Marry Our Parents

There is a saying: we marry our mother and father. Whatever remains unfinished with our parents tends to reappear in our closest adult relationships — especially with a spouse. Unhealthy patterns absorbed unconsciously from parents are acted out again until they are healed.

Story – The Alcoholic Father

A girl grew up angry with her alcoholic, irresponsible father. The mother carried the whole family. As an adult the daughter left home determined to create a different life. She fell in love, married… and within months discovered her husband was also an alcoholic and did not take responsibility — exactly what she had hated in her father.

The husband had come to show her the unhealed relationship with her father. She was also behaving like her mother — angry at the irresponsible man. The mirror was clear.

Parents and the Creator Principle

Mother and father are the creators of this lifetime’s body. Feelings we hold toward them are often reflected toward the Divine Mother, Divine Father or Creator principle. If the relationship with the earthly mother is unhealed, even if the Divine Mother sits in front of us we may not recognise or receive her love.

Simply saying “I accept my parents” at a superficial level is not enough. When this mirror appears, it shows that deeper healing is still needed. Respect and gratitude for the gift of this body and this opportunity to be here is the foundation.

Even if Parents Are No Longer Alive

Inner Child work and healing of the parental relationship can be done whether the parent is living or has passed, and whether the parent was loving or abusive. The pain of early loss, abandonment or abuse stays in the subconscious and keeps sending mirrors until it is faced and healed. Defences that protected the child (numbing, becoming “adult” too soon, closing the heart) later block intimacy and sacred relationship.

Key Remember Points

  • Unfinished business with parents reappears in spouse and close relationships.
  • We absorb both healthy and unhealthy patterns from parents and act them out.
  • Healing the parental relationship also heals the relationship with the Creator principle.
  • Gratitude for the gift of the body is the starting point of acceptance.
  • Inner Child work is essential; it can be done even if parents are deceased or were abusive.

Homework for Day 5

  • Write freely: “What I still feel toward my mother… toward my father…” (no censorship).
  • Notice one quality in your partner or a close person that strongly reminds you of a parent. What lesson might it be offering?
  • Offer a simple inner statement: “Thank you for giving me this body and this life. I am willing to heal what is still unfinished between us.”
  • If strong pain arises, consider proper Inner Child work with a trained facilitator.
