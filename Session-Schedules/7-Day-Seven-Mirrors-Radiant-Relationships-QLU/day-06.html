Seven Mirrors of Radiant Relationships · Day 6

Mirror 6 · The Dark Night of the Soul

The Fear We Do Not Want to Face

The sixth mirror shows our deepest fears and challenges — the darkness we prefer not to look at. Most of the time we divert ourselves the moment the fearful thought arises: watch a movie, read a book, stay busy. The more we avoid it, the more forcefully it eventually appears in outer life.

Story – Fear of Losing a Loved One

A man carried a deep, scary thought: “I might lose my loved ones.” Whenever it arose he immediately distracted himself. Life looked perfect — good job, loving wife, bright children. Then his five-year-old daughter died in an accident. Everything collapsed. He lost his job, sank into depression; after two years his wife could no longer bear it and left. He was left alone — exactly the fear he had avoided.

The deepest fear he refused to face manifested with full force. This is the dark night of the soul.

You Are Equipped to Face It

The teaching is clear: the Divine does not give challenges you do not have the strength to meet. You chose these challenges (at the soul level) because you have the courage and capacity to face them. Avoidance only postpones and intensifies the meeting.

This mirror requires great courage, strength and awareness. When the deepest fear is finally faced and felt through, movement and evolution become possible again.

Key Remember Points

  • The sixth mirror reveals the deepest fears we keep avoiding.
  • Avoidance causes the fear to manifest more forcefully in outer life.
  • You already have the strength to face what appears; that is why it is in your life.
  • Facing the fear (feeling it, understanding it, not running) is the way through the dark night.
  • After the meeting, evolution continues.

Homework for Day 6

  • In a safe, quiet space, ask: “What is the fear I least want to feel or think about?” Write it without forcing solutions.
  • Notice your usual escape routes (phone, work, food, entertainment). Practise staying with the feeling for a few breaths instead of immediately escaping.
  • Affirm: “I have the strength to meet whatever arises. I do not need to run.”
  • If the fear is overwhelming, seek support from a trusted facilitator or therapist experienced in deep emotional work.
