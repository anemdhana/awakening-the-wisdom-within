Seven Mirrors of Radiant Relationships · Day 2

Mirror 2 · The Mirror of Judgment

The Hidden Charge After 90% Clearing

You may have cleared from the conscious mind almost all junk thoughts, negative thoughts and negative vibrations. To a major extent — perhaps 90 percent — the conscious mind is clear. You have become a positive thinker. Yet negative things keep happening. You still attract difficult situations and very difficult relationships.

It is the second mirror that can reveal what is still operating. From the Nag Hammadi Library (Essene text):

“Recognize what is in your sight” — that is actually the first mirror.
“And that which is hidden from you will become plain to you.” — this hidden layer is the second mirror, much more subtle and deeper. It reflects your judgments.

Judgment Comes Back to You

Most of the time we judge and we blame. Jesus Christ said: “Judge not, otherwise you will be judged.” Your judgments come back to you. Whatever you judge, whatever you deny in your life, also gets magnetized into your experience.

Because the focus has shifted to what you do not want instead of what you want, you create the very experience you are fighting.

Example – Extreme Denial of Alcohol

You hate people who drink alcohol. You hate alcohol itself. You have become an extremist, protesting that alcohol is bad and very wrong. You are denying it and fighting with it. What happens? Whatever you deny, you receive in your experience. Whatever you do not want, you also magnetize and create in your life — because the entire focus has moved to the thing you reject.

Example – The Sudden Furious Reaction

You are producing only positive thoughts. Suddenly one day you are watching television or reading a newspaper. You read that somebody has betrayed a friend, or that a child has suffered a terrible injustice. Suddenly a charge comes out: “How can this person do this? How can they do this injustice?”

That is where the judgmental charge still lies. You may have worked on yourself 90 percent, but this remaining 10 percent is still active. When you judge someone as “dishonest,” the charge is showing you that a similar quality is still hidden deep down in you. The person or the news is simply bringing that charge to the surface. It is mirroring you.

Judgment vs Discernment

How can we live without judging people around us? We need a subtle distinction:

Discernment comes from wisdom. It sees clearly what is helpful and what is not. It does not disturb us.

Judgment comes from the ego and the reactive mind — the personality ego that is so consolidated and still carries judgmental charge. It reacts and labels. Judgment disturbs; discernment does not.

Look into your own life: what have you judged? Have you noticed that the same judgment comes back — that you are judged again by people around you, somehow, through somebody?

Story – “My Husband Is So Angry”

A woman wanted to divorce her husband because “he is so angry all the time.” When asked what happens when he becomes angry, she said he shouts and does all sorts of nonsense. When asked what happens inside her, she said: first pain and frustration… then “I become so angry I could kill him.”

What she was judging in her husband was reflecting her own hidden anger charge. The husband was the mirror of her judgment.

How to Work with This Mirror

When a situation or person triggers strong judgment:

  1. Ask: “Is this the first mirror (my present vibration)?” If the answer is no, it is likely the second mirror.
  2. Remove the mask. Ask honestly: “What emotional charge inside me is being activated?”
  3. Look at the part of yourself that still carries the same quality you are judging — even if only at a subtle, subconscious level.
  4. Work to understand and release that charge. Only then do the judgments begin to drop.

Even if you have meditated and studied for years, a strong judgment of another person shows a part that has not yet been fully seen or healed.

Key Remember Points

  • You can clear 90% of the conscious mind and still carry judgmental charge at a deeper plane.
  • “Recognize what is in your sight” = first mirror; “that which is hidden will become plain” = second mirror.
  • Whatever you judge and deny with force tends to return in experience.
  • Judgment disturbs; discernment does not.
  • Out-of-proportion reactions (to news, people, events) reveal the residual charge.
  • Your judgments come back to you through people and situations.

Homework for Day 2

  • Notice the next three times you strongly judge someone (in person, on news, or online). Write: “I judged ___ for ___. The charge I felt was ___.”
  • Ask gently: “Where might a similar quality still live in me, even in a subtle form?”
  • Practise one day of catching judgment and converting it into quiet discernment: “I see this behaviour; I do not need to hate the person.”
  • If a close relationship keeps pushing your buttons, treat it as the second mirror and journal what it is reflecting about your own charge.
