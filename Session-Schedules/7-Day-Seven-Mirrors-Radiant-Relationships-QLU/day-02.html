Seven Mirrors of Radiant Relationships · Day 2

Mirror 2 · The Mirror of Judgment

Deeper than Present Vibration

After the first mirror (who we are in the present moment), the second mirror is deeper. You may have cleared most negative thoughts from the conscious mind. You may be a positive thinker. Yet difficult relationships and situations keep appearing. This is often the mirror of judgment — a reflection of emotional charge that is still hidden at a deeper level.

From the Nag Hammadi Library (Essene wisdom): “Recognize what is in your sight… and that which is hidden from you will become plain to you.” The second mirror is more subtle. It reflects our judgments.

Judgment vs Discernment

Discernment comes from wisdom. You see clearly what is helpful and what is not. It does not disturb you.

Judgment comes from the ego and the reactive mind. It carries emotional charge. You label someone as “bad,” “dishonest,” “should be punished.” That charge disturbs your own consciousness.

When you point one finger at another person, three fingers point back at yourself. Whatever you strongly judge in others is often a part you have not yet looked at or healed within yourself.

Jesus said: “Why do you look at the speck of sawdust in your brother’s eye and pay no attention to the plank in your own eye?”

Example – The Newspaper Reaction

You are peaceful and joyful. Your vibration is fine. Then you read a news item: one best friend has cheated the other and taken all the money. Suddenly you react out of proportion — “How can someone be so dishonest? How can he cheat his own best friend?”

That out-of-proportion reaction shows an emotional charge. The news has activated a judgmental charge still living inside you. The mirror is not about your present vibration; it is showing a deeper unhealed judgment.

Story – “My Husband Is So Angry”

A woman wanted to divorce her husband because “he is so angry all the time.” When asked what happens when he becomes angry, she said he shouts and does all sorts of nonsense. When asked what happens to her, she said: first pain and frustration… then “I become so angry I could kill him.”

What she was judging in her husband was reflecting her own hidden anger charge. The husband was the mirror of her judgment.

Whatever You Deny Comes Back

Whatever you judge and deny with strong charge tends to be magnetized into your experience. If you hate alcohol so intensely that you fight it as an extremist, that denial can still draw related experiences into your life, because the focus has shifted to “what I do not want.”

In relationships, strong dislikes of certain qualities in others often mirror judgments still active inside us.

How to Work with This Mirror

When a situation or person triggers strong judgment:

  1. Ask: “Is this the first mirror (my present vibration)?” If the answer is no, it is likely the second mirror.
  2. Remove the mask. Ask honestly: “What emotional charge inside me is being activated?”
  3. Look at the part of yourself that still carries the same quality you are judging.
  4. Work to understand and release that charge. Only then do the judgments begin to drop.

Even if you have meditated and studied for years, strong judgment of another person shows a part that has not yet been fully seen or healed.

Key Remember Points

  • The second mirror reflects hidden judgmental charge, not just present mood.
  • Judgment disturbs; discernment does not.
  • What you strongly judge in others is often unhealed within yourself.
  • Out-of-proportion reactions are a clear signal of this mirror.
  • Whatever you deny with force tends to return in experience.
  • Courage to look at one’s own charge is the way through.

Homework for Day 2

  • Notice the next three times you strongly judge someone (in person, on news, or online). Write: “I judged ___ for ___. The charge I felt was ___.”
  • Ask gently: “Where might a similar quality still live in me, even in a subtle form?”
  • Practise one day of catching judgment and converting it into quiet discernment: “I see this behaviour; I do not need to hate the person.”
  • If a close relationship keeps pushing your buttons, treat it as the second mirror and journal what it is reflecting about your own charge.
