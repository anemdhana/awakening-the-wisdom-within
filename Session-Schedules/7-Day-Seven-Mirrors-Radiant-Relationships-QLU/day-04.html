Seven Mirrors of Radiant Relationships · Day 4

Mirror 4 · Forgotten Love & Past-Life Patterns

That Which You Bring Forth Will Save You

The fourth mirror is about reflections of your most forgotten love. The Nag Hammadi Library says:

“That which you have will save you if you bring it forth from yourselves. If you do not bring it forth from yourself, it will destroy you.”

The most forgotten love is the truth — and the truth is loving yourself. If you do not love yourself, you do not attract people who are truly loving toward you. This forgotten love often comes from many lives. This mirror reflects patterns that repeat like a tape.

Common Patterns That Repeat

Look at your relationship history. What feeling, sensation or imagery keeps repeating?

  • Victim pattern — constantly feeling like a victim; attracting perpetrators or abusers; putting yourself down.
  • Abandonment pattern — after some time in a relationship you feel abandoned, or you always land in situations where everyone leaves you alone.
  • Betrayal pattern — you attract people who are dishonest; trust is broken again and again.

From childhood onward the same feeling, the same sensation, the same memory can loop. The fourth mirror shows these patterns and their origins.

Case Study – The Queen Who Waited for the King

In a past life a woman was a queen (or princess). The king went to war and said: “I will come back and marry you / take care of you.” He never returned. She waited and waited. At the end of her life she concluded that the king had betrayed her.

The actual reality: the king went to war and died in that war. He could not come back to fulfil his promise. She made a wrong conclusion without knowing the big picture.

With that conclusion she entered this lifetime carrying the energy “he betrayed me.” In this life she enters relationships with the fear that people might betray her. That same energy creates the reality: whoever comes into her life eventually leaves. The pattern repeated many times because she needed to correct the wrong conclusion — the forgotten truth.

When she went back, saw the larger picture, and understood that he died and did not betray her, the pattern resolved.

Wrong Conclusions Without Context

Most of the time we do not know the full context. When we go back to the origins of our relationship patterns and see the context, we gain a broader viewpoint. Many relationship patterns exist because we came to quick, wrong conclusions. That energy keeps acting. Betrayal energy attracts dishonest people; you place yourself in situations where you will be betrayed — because at some level you are asking for the experience so that you can finally learn the larger truth.

Past-life and reincarnation journeys can bring the memories and origins of the patterns. The way to end a pattern is to register a new, correct conclusion:

Nobody can truly betray you. Nobody can really abandon you.
First you do it to yourself — you abandon yourself, you betray yourself — and then you project it onto people and recreate the experience.

That is the most forgotten truth revealed through the fourth mirror.

Key Remember Points

  • “That which you bring forth from yourself will save you; if you do not, it will destroy you.”
  • The most forgotten love is the truth of loving yourself.
  • Repeating patterns (victim, abandonment, betrayal) are tapes that need a new conclusion.
  • Wrong conclusions made without the big picture keep creating matching experiences.
  • First you abandon or betray yourself; then outer mirrors reflect it.
  • Seeing the larger context and registering the correct conclusion ends the pattern.

Homework for Day 4

  • Write the one or two relationship feelings that keep repeating (e.g. “I will be left,” “I cannot trust,” “I am always the one who gives”).
  • Ask: “When did I first decide this was true? What larger picture might I have missed?”
  • Practise the new conclusion daily: “I do not abandon myself. I stay with myself. Nobody can truly betray the truth of who I am.”
  • If a past-life or early-life memory surfaces, stay with the feeling and look for missing context rather than only the old conclusion.
