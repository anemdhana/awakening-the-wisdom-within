Seven Mirrors of Radiant Relationships · Day 4

Mirror 4 · Forgotten Love & Past-Life Patterns

What This Mirror Reflects

The fourth mirror is the reflection of forgotten love or forgotten truth. It often shows patterns that repeat across lifetimes — unfinished business, wrong conclusions we made, and the energy of those conclusions still creating reality today.

The Nag Hammadi text says: that which you have within you will save you if you bring it forth; if you do not bring it forth, it will destroy you. The most forgotten love is the truth of loving yourself and seeing the larger picture of events.

Case Study – “My Husband Will Abandon Me”

A woman constantly felt that her husband would abandon her, even though he was loving and caring. With that fear she tried to possess and control him. Whenever he went out for work the intense fear arose: “Now he will leave me.”

In a deeper session she connected to a past life: she was a young woman; a prince promised to return, take permission from his parents and marry her. He never returned. She waited decades and died feeling betrayed and abandoned.

In the higher state she was guided to look at what actually happened to the prince. She saw that when he returned to his kingdom a war began; he fought and was killed. He did not betray her — he died. Once she corrected the wrong conclusion (“he abandoned me”), the repeating pattern of abandonment fear in this life lost its power.

Common Repeating Patterns

Look at your relationship history. Do you notice the same feeling, sensation or outcome repeating like a tape?

  • Victim pattern — always feeling victimised; attracting perpetrators or abusers.
  • Abandonment pattern — people leave; you end up alone.
  • Betrayal pattern — trust is broken again and again.
  • Other fixed feelings that reappear in every important relationship.

These patterns often began with a conclusion made in a past life (or early this life) without the full picture. That energy continues to attract matching experiences until the conclusion is corrected and the truth is seen.

How Patterns Resolve

When you register a new, correct conclusion — “Nobody can truly abandon me; first I abandon myself” or “He did not betray me; circumstances took him” — the energetic tape stops. Past-life regression and life-between-lives work can reveal the origin, but even without formal regression, honest inquiry into “Where did I first decide this about relationships?” begins to loosen the pattern.

First you do it to yourself (self-abandonment, self-betrayal); then you project it onto others and recreate the experience so that you can finally learn the larger truth.

Key Remember Points

  • The fourth mirror shows repeating relationship patterns and forgotten truth.
  • Wrong conclusions made in past (or early) situations keep creating similar experiences.
  • Seeing the bigger picture dissolves the charge of “betrayal” or “abandonment.”
  • You first abandon or betray yourself; then outer mirrors reflect it.
  • Correcting the conclusion ends the tape.

Homework for Day 4

  • Write the one or two relationship feelings that keep repeating in your life (e.g. “I will be left,” “I cannot trust,” “I am always the one who gives”).
  • Ask: “When did I first decide this was true? What larger picture might I have missed?”
  • If a past-life or early-life memory surfaces, stay with the feeling and look for the missing context rather than only the old conclusion.
  • Practise the new conclusion daily for a week: “I do not abandon myself. I stay with myself.”
