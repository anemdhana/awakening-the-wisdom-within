Seven Essene Mirrors of Relationships · Day 6

Mirror 6 · The Dark Night of the Soul

The Quest for Darkness — A Test of Mastery

The sixth Essene mirror is also called the dark night of the soul. It reflects the quest for darkness — when we meet our greatest challenges and greatest fears, after we have been gathering the tools and understanding in life to confront them.

Even the greatest masters — Buddha, Jesus, Mahavira — faced extreme challenges. We wonder: if the law of attraction is true, why did an enlightened being attract crucifixion or such trials? This mirror answers: we only face these challenges when we have amassed the tools that allow us to move through them with grace and ease.

It is like claiming to have great stamina — until you actually go out and jog, how do you know? The challenge comes as a test of our evolution, of our wisdom, of whether we can demonstrate high levels of mastery. It is not a test to pass or fail; it is an opportunity to demonstrate mastery of spiritual growth.

Shreans’ Story – Arthur Road Jail, 1999 (14 Days)

From 1996 Shreans met Patriji, entered meditation, healed depression and chest TB within a month, and began manifesting. From 1997 to 1999 his business rocketed — he became the highest exporter in the country. Then ambition took over. He became obsessed with becoming “somebody,” with wealth. For about six months he stopped spreading meditation and focused only on business dinners and success.

At the end of 1999, customs officers came. A huge case was filed. Within two days he was in Arthur Road Jail, Bombay, for 14 days.

The first day was crushing: “What will the world think of me? What will my family, my business society think?” Sleepless, panic, anxiety. His sister brought him Ramtha – The White Book. He read the chapter on the Virtue of Experience — which mirrors the sixth Essene mirror: there is a virtue in every experience; challenges are tests of whether we have high levels of mastery.

His conscience was clear; he had done nothing wrong. Why this situation? Then he remembered a vision months earlier: he had wanted to teach meditation in jail. Here was the opportunity. All the anxiety of the previous 24 hours had been a mental construct about reputation — emotional investment in what others think.

He dropped the construct. The word “jail” carried social stigma; the truth was a large hall — perhaps ten thousand square feet, bigger than his own house. He made friends with hardcore criminals, heard their childhood stories, and developed compassion. He dropped judgment toward acts and people. Those 14 days helped him grow and evolve.

He stood “naked in his own eyes” — all constructs of security, family support, money, status dropped. For the first time he was given the chance to prove to himself that the process of life can be trusted, that stability exists beyond what we usually realise.

“What’s life without a royal challenge?” — what Patriji would say when people brought challenging situations.

Every great scientist, human being or master of the world was first ridiculed before they were revered. We are no exception.

Life crisis situations strip you of the filters of mental constructs. Then you see yourself without those lenses. Do not shy away from challenges. Do not resist them. They are opportunities to demonstrate mastery.

It is happening for you, not to you.

Key Remember Points

  • The sixth mirror is the dark night — greatest challenges after tools have been gathered.
  • Challenges come so we can demonstrate mastery, not to break us.
  • Reputation anxiety is often emotional investment in others’ opinions.
  • Dropping mental constructs (jail, status, security) brings peace and compassion.
  • “What’s life without a royal challenge?”
  • It is happening for you, not to you.

Homework for Day 6

  • Name one “royal challenge” in your life. Ask: “What mastery is this inviting me to demonstrate?”
  • Notice where anxiety is really about “what will others think.” Practise dropping that construct for one day.
  • Read or recall the idea of “virtue of experience” — find one gift in a past difficulty.
  • When fear arises, affirm: “This is happening for me. I have the tools.”
