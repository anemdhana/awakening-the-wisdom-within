Seven Essene Mirrors of Relationships · Day 7

Mirror 7 · Self-Perception

How Others Treat Us Reflects How We Treat Ourselves

The seventh Essene mirror reflects our self-perception. How others perceive and treat us is according to how we perceive and treat ourselves. The outside world is a reflection of the self-image we have created.

This mirror invites us to allow that each experience of life, regardless of outer outcome, is perfect in its nature. You do not need external reference. The only way to measure yourself is to keep yourself as your own yardstick — not society, not parents, not others’ expectations.

Success without contentment is the ultimate failure. Film stars and business tycoons can still feel empty if what they pursue is not aligned with purpose. Only when direction matches inner purpose does contentment come.

“Your perception of me is actually a reflection of you — not mine. My reaction to you is an awareness of me.”

Shreans’ Story – 2019 Pyramid Valley: Dropping the Money Addiction

By his mid-forties Shreans had decided that by age 45 he would leave active money-making and focus on teaching meditation — his real passion. By 40 he had made far more wealth than his original vision. Yet he stayed caught in the ambitious cycle: “commodity king,” trading any commodity, ego, addiction to making more.

From 2016 to 2019 he began losing money despite visualisation. In September 2019, at Pyramid Valley during a gathering, insight came in meditation: he was living by an external yardstick — proving what he could do, seeing himself through others’ lenses. He already had enough wealth to live as he wished. The push to keep making money was social conditioning and addiction.

He decided: from today I drop this addiction. It does not matter what society, family or business colleagues think. I will pursue what gives the highest joy — teaching and learning. He left active business. He now gives most of his time to his own growth and to sharing. Teaching became the best form of learning.

He has never been more peaceful, joyful or content. Relationships became more fulfilling; he is more present; synchronicities increased. The highest emotional payoff was no longer from money or houses or cars, but from evolution and sharing that growth.

Rumi: “Half of your life is lost in charming others; the other half is lost in going through anxieties caused by others. Leave this play — you have played enough.”

Focus on true passion and purpose. Align time and energy in that direction. Whatever you do is for an emotional payoff — choose the payoff that is highest and most lasting.

Be your own thermometer. Profession, body, academics, sports — all are perfect as they stand when measured against your own growth, not against others.

Key Remember Points

  • Others treat you according to how you treat yourself.
  • Be your own yardstick — not parents, society or external goals.
  • Success without contentment is the ultimate failure.
  • Your perception of me reflects you; my reaction to you reveals me.
  • Leave the play of charming others and fearing their opinion.
  • Highest emotional payoff comes from purpose and sharing growth.

Homework for Day 7

  • Ask: “Am I living by what I want, or by what parents / society wanted?” Write honestly.
  • Sit with Rumi’s lines. Notice where half your energy still goes to charming or fearing others.
  • Identify one area where you use an external yardstick. Practise measuring only against your own yesterday.
  • Review all seven mirrors. Note which are most active. Choose one small repair this week.
