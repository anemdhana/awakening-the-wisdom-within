Seven Essene Mirrors of Relationships · Day 2

Mirror 2 · What We Judge in the Moment

A Subtle Mirror That Transformed Him

The second Essene mirror is one of Shreans’ favourites — it brought a big transformation. It reflects what we judge in the moment: something we have an emotional charge with, something we were wounded by or have not forgiven.

In close relationships (spouse, children, parents) we live day in and day out, form strong judgments, and they push our emotional buttons. We wonder: “This is not my pattern — why am I facing this?”

Shreans’ Story – Spiritual Arrogance and Short Temper (1997–2002)

From 1997 he entered meditation; by 2002 he was teaching and organising workshops. He felt peaceful, happy, joyful — yet he kept encountering people who were angry, upset or short-tempered toward him.

He wondered: “If the law of attraction is real, and I am not feeling anger, why am I attracting this?” The answer was the second mirror. When we become spiritual we can become spiritually arrogant. He started judging everyone who showed anger or short temper — wife, parents, relatives, business partners: “These guys are stupid; they don’t know spirituality; they need to meditate; how can they feel angry for the smallest reason?”

The judgment carried a strong emotional charge. Even though anger was not his behaviour, the charge of judgment was in his field. What you resist, persists. By resisting anger he was feeding it and drawing more of it. The observer affects the observed. He was participating in the event by carrying the charge.

He was not seeing his wife as she was; he was seeing the memory of ten years of relationship. Before she finished a sentence he had already judged her. Expectancy determines outcome. The subjective mind impacts the objective world.

When he dropped the judgmental charge, relationships began to heal.

“A man is according to the manner you look at him.”

Wayne Dyer: “When you change the way you look at things, the things you look at change.”

J. Krishnamurti: “To observe without evaluating is the highest form of human intelligence.”

If her past were my past, if her pain were my pain, if her level of consciousness were mine, I would think and act exactly as she does. With that realisation comes forgiveness, compassion and peace.

Whatever irritates us about others only reflects the part of the ego still active in us. Ask: How would Buddha or Jesus behave in this situation? If they would not get irritated, something in us still needs healing.

All situations are fundamentally neutral; we assign meaning and thereby invite the experience. Come to each moment cleanly, without previous thought about the person — create who you are rather than recreate who you were. Meet people as if for the first time. You have control over the behaviour of others as it relates to you, because you were evoking it with your vibration.

The universe only brings aspects of others that are active in you.

Co-dependency: Don’t Lose Your Alignment

When someone in the family is alcoholic, lying, or misaligned, the whole family can become co-dependent. The person is obsessed with the habit; others become obsessed with changing them — calling, checking, full-time worry. Their obsession becomes our obsession. Out of love we lose our own alignment.

You cannot solve a problem from the same level of consciousness that created it. First break your own obsession. Accept that it is their journey and their choice. Let not anybody’s misalignment make you lose your alignment. No person or situation has more power over you than that.

Buddha, Christ, Guru Nanak all faced others’ challenges and misalignment — and did not lose their own centre. Your power is to stay aligned. When you are aligned, you become an inspiration; then they may ask how you changed. Trying to change them from misalignment only creates more misalignment.

One person in alignment is more powerful than millions who are not. Source never calibrates to misery.

Key Remember Points

  • Judgment carries emotional charge even when the behaviour is not “yours.”
  • Spiritual arrogance can create strong judgments of those who still show “lower” emotions.
  • What you resist persists; the observer affects the observed.
  • You often see memory, not the present person; expectancy shapes outcome.
  • If their past and consciousness were yours, you would act the same — compassion follows.
  • Observe without evaluating; come cleanly to each moment.
  • Drop judgment → drop resistance → acceptance → allowance.
  • Do not lose your alignment because of others’ misalignment; accept their journey.
  • One alignment is more powerful than millions misaligned.

Homework for Day 2

  • Notice the next strong judgment. Write: “I judged ___ for ___. The charge was ___.”
  • Ask: “If their past and level of consciousness were mine, would I act the same?”
  • Practise one interaction as if meeting the person for the first time — no old story.
  • Catch spiritual arrogance: “They should meditate / know better.” Soften into curiosity.
  • If you are obsessed with changing someone, pause: “Is this my alignment or their journey?”
