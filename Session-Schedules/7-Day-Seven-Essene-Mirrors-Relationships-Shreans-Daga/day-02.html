Seven Essene Mirrors of Relationships · Day 2

Mirror 2 · What We Judge in the Moment

A Subtle and Powerful Mirror

The second Essene mirror is very interesting and subtle. It reflects back to us what we judge in the moment.

People often frustrate us, push our emotional buttons of rage or anger. The moment we see that person, something starts happening inside — emotions we do not even recognise as “our nature.” Yet in their presence those emotions are triggered.

Shreans’ Own Experience – Judging Short Temper

When Shreans entered spirituality and meditation (from 1997), for a long time he carried a strong judgment toward people who were short-tempered. He would feel: “How stupid they are, how funny, what kind of idiots…” He might not say it out loud, but the judgment was strong.

He kept finding people around him demonstrating the same emotions toward him or in the environment. His judgment grew stronger. He wondered: “I am peaceful; this is not my attitude. Why am I creating this around me?”

When he understood the second Essene mirror, he could decode and resolve it. The mirror was reflecting the judgment itself — the strong emotional charge he was carrying toward that attitude. Because of that charge he was attracting and evoking more of the same response.

Judgment Carries Emotional Charge

A judgment has a strong emotional charge. The moment you start judging others, that charge lives in your energy field and you attract it into your life again. It is not necessarily your own behavioural pattern, but the charge toward that attitude in others is enough to evoke the response.

It can be judgment toward dishonesty, lying, lack of ethics — “I hate people who are dishonest” — said with intensity. Even toward virtues we can carry strong judgment. What you resist, persists. What you fight, you strengthen. You are giving energy to it.

The observer is not separated from what is being observed. The observer affects what is being observed. Your very act of observation, charged with judgment, participates in their behaviour. You are charging them with your judgment; they then behave accordingly, which strengthens your judgment further. Familiarity often breeds contempt — the more familiar we become (spouse, parents, children, siblings, close friends), the more judgments we form and the more cracks appear in the relationship.

You Don’t See the Person — You See the Memory

You don’t see your wife (or husband, or colleague). You see the memory of the last 10 or 30 years. Before she has spoken a word you have already judged. Either you can judge or you can love.

If her past were your past, if her pain were your pain, if her level of consciousness were yours, you would think and act exactly as she does. With that realisation comes forgiveness, compassion and peace.

Wayne Dyer: “When you change the way you look at things, the things you look at will change.”

Everybody is at a different stage of evolution, a different level of consciousness. We draw people of different levels into our lives so we can expand. Triggers are our best teachers — they show the part of the ego that still needs awareness, acceptance, understanding, compassion and healing.

Drop Judgment → Acceptance → Allowance

Whatever irritates you about others only reflects the part of the ego that is active in you. All resistance is futile. A large part of human pain is self-created by the unobserved mind.

Stop assigning negative meanings. Situations are fundamentally neutral; we give them meaning and thereby buy the experience. Once you stop giving negative assignments, you stop inviting that reality.

Whatever you sincerely believe is true of another, you will awaken within him or her. Others only echo what we whisper to them in secret. A judgment is our inner talk — what we are whispering — and that is what they echo back.

J. Krishnamurti: “The ability to observe without evaluating is the highest form of human intelligence.” That is also what we practise in meditation — thoughts come, we do not evaluate, we witness.

Come to each moment cleanly, without the previous story about the person. Then you create who you are rather than recreate who you were. You have control over the behaviour of others as it relates to you, because with your judgment you were participating in it. Drop the judgment; the response changes.

Find the most generous explanation of each other’s behaviour and truly believe it. What you appreciate, appreciates. Focus on the positive aspects to awaken the goodness in yourself and in them.

Key Remember Points

  • The second mirror reflects what we judge — the emotional charge of judgment itself.
  • What you resist persists; what you fight, you strengthen.
  • The observer affects the observed; you participate in their behaviour through judgment.
  • You often see the memory of years, not the present person.
  • Triggers are teachers showing the active part of the ego that needs healing.
  • Drop judgment → drop resistance → acceptance → allowance → freedom.
  • Observe without evaluating; come to each moment cleanly.
  • What you appreciate appreciates; others echo what you whisper in secret.

Homework for Day 2

  • Notice the next strong judgment that arises. Write: “I judged ___ for ___. The charge I felt was ___.”
  • Ask: “If this person’s past and level of consciousness were mine, would I act the same?” Let compassion arise.
  • Practise one interaction coming to the person “cleanly” — without the old story. Observe the difference.
  • Find one generous explanation for someone’s behaviour and hold it sincerely for a day.
