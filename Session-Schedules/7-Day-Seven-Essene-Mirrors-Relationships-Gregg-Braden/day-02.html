Seven Essene Mirrors · Day 2

Mirror 2 · That Which We Judge in the Moment

The second mirror is of a similar quality to the first, though more subtle. Rather than mirroring what we are in the moment, it mirrors to us that which we judge in the moment.

If you find yourself surrounded by individuals who demonstrate patterns that frustrate you, push your buttons of anger or rage — and you sense honestly that those are not your patterns in the moment — then there is a good possibility they are showing you that which you judge. When many different people demonstrate the same patterns to you, look for the charge of judgment.

Gregg’s Story – Three People in One Month

A few years ago three brand-new relationships came into Gregg’s life in the same month — that alone was the flag. One potentially romantic, one business (someone offering to handle seminar logistics), one a mix of friendship and business (a skilled carpenter who asked to house-sit on the Northern New Mexico property in exchange for caretaking and carpentry while Gregg led a group to Egypt).

Within that same month each of them began to drive him “right up the wall.” His old pattern was to use logic: “You’re just tired, under pressure — give it another week.” He would leave on a trip and return to find everything the same.

Then, on a Sunday afternoon at the Albuquerque airport ATM, the machine said the account was empty. He knew it was a mistake — he had just been approved for a construction loan. Monday the bank confirmed: not only empty, but overdrawn by 71 checks with service charges. A wire withdrawal had taken every penny. He did not even have money for gas or to get the animals from the vet.

At the same time, money from a 30-day Pacific Northwest tour was not coming through (accounting, reconciliation, excuses). And the caretaker had chosen a lifestyle illegal in New Mexico at that time and not aligned with the property.

He went for a walk down the road toward the Rio Grande Gorge, past Ute Peak. He asked: If the mirrors hold true, what are these people showing me? He looked for the common thread. Honesty. Integrity. Trust. Those three stood out.

Were they showing that he lacked honesty, integrity or trust? A feeling welled up: No. That’s what this work is all about. The realization was subtle and powerful: they were not showing the first mirror (what he was in the moment). They were showing the second — the mirror of what he judged in the moment. He had a tremendous charge on honesty, integrity and trust — such a charge that he was not willing to allow the absence of those qualities in others. When you have a charge on something, the promise is that you will see it in your life.

Each of those three people was a powerful, skillful master who impeccably held the mirror of his greatest charges. The mirrors may have been shown year after year on subtle levels until something happened that he could not look away from.

The “Evil Twin” and the Feeling He Discounted

The first moment he met the seminar organizer, on a landing in Northern California, he asked something he rarely asks on first meeting: “When’s your birthday?” June 28, 1954 — Gregg’s exact birthday. Both Cancerians. The man looked him in the eyes and said: “Ha — I’m your evil twin.” Gregg discounted it with logic as a joke, though a feeling was there.

The same with the caretaker and the potential romantic relationship: a feeling at the start, discounted by logic (“you don’t even know him,” “that’s the old hurt talking — give this one a chance”).

The week he recognized the pattern of judgment — that each was masterfully mirroring what he judged — every other relationship held in place by judgment began to disappear. There is a cascading effect. Consciousness is holographic: when a pattern is healed in one place, it is healed everywhere simultaneously.

Joanne Kornog – Same Birthday, Different Feeling

He ended the business relationship. As he hung up, he realized he had just cancelled every program and source of income for the next six months. That Saturday night a message came from a woman he had never met, asking to sponsor programs around the country. The first thing he asked: “When’s your birthday?” June 28, 1954. His first reaction was to hang up. He did not. He told her the story. She asked if he would give “us” a chance. He paid attention to the feeling — it felt different. He said yes. That woman was Joanne Carl Kornog — later seminar coordinator, workshop leader, author. Through her he met Melissa. Because he did not allow the first relationship to taint the second, and trusted the feeling, a powerful working relationship followed. Through discernment he later averted serious problems with publishers and contracts.

Look at the people you hold most dear — that is the magnet. Look at the characteristics that push your buttons right and left. Ask: Are they showing me me in the moment? If honestly no — are they showing me that which I judge in the moment? You may be surprised at what you find.

Key Remember Points

  • The second mirror shows what we judge — not necessarily what we are in the moment.
  • A charge on a quality (honesty, integrity, trust…) promises we will see its absence in our lives.
  • People can be impeccable masters of the mirrors we need.
  • Logic often discounts the feeling that already knew.
  • When judgment is healed in one place, it cascades; consciousness is holographic.
  • Do not let one failed mirror taint the next opportunity — trust the present feeling.

Homework for Day 2

  • List the people you hold most dear. Note the qualities that push your buttons.
  • For each: “Showing me me in the moment — or that which I judge?”
  • Where do you have a strong charge (honesty, loyalty, respect, etc.)? Notice where it appears.
  • Practise trusting the feeling at the start of a relationship without immediately overriding it with logic.
