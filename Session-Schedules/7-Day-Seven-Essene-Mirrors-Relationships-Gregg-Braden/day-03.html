Seven Essene Mirrors · Day 3

Mirror 3 · Lost Parts of Self

The third Essene mirror is one of the most commonly recognized. It is the mirror we sense every time we find ourselves in the presence of another and, looking into their eyes, magic happens — an electrical charge, hair on the arms or neck standing up, a sudden desire to share time with someone we may not even know.

Through the wisdom of this mirror we are asked to allow for the possibility that, in our innocence, to survive life’s offerings, we give away huge portions of ourselves. Pieces may be lost unknowingly, given away knowingly, or taken from us by those who had power over us. When we meet someone who embodies the very things we have lost, our body responds physiologically — we experience magnetic attraction. We are seeking that wholeness in ourselves through them.

Ask: What is it that I see in this person that I have lost, given away, or that was taken from me? You may be very surprised at what comes back.

In nearly every individual we will ever encounter we can recognize a familiarity — a piece of ourselves — because we see it in one another throughout our lives. The other is a reminder, not the source. When we reclaim the quality, relationship becomes sharing rather than completion.

“Getting Slapped” – The Engineer Who Fell in Love at Lunch

Gregg once worked with a group of engineers. One of them would leave for lunch on Friday after payday, cash the check, run errands — and return unable to work. “I fell in love over lunch.” It happened multiple times a day. The group had a name for it: getting slapped. “How was lunch?” “I got slapped five times.” He would call the bank to ask who the teller was… The mirror was showing him lost or disowned parts of himself reflected in each woman who crossed his path — and it wreaked havoc until the pattern was understood.

McLeod / Mount Shasta – Familiar Strangers

In 1992, during workshops at Stony Brook Inn near Mount Shasta, two travelers walked in while the group watched a video. One of the women looked very familiar though Gregg had never met her. That sense of familiarity — the electrical glimmer when eyes meet in an airport, mall, or grocery store — is the third mirror at work. In our culture we often break the moment (look at the gum on the sidewalk) because the charge is not “approved.” The invitation is to stay with the question: What of myself am I seeing?

Key Remember Points

  • The electric / magnetic charge points to a lost, given-away, or taken part of self.
  • We seek wholeness in others that already belongs to us.
  • Familiarity with strangers is common — we see pieces of ourselves in one another.
  • Reclaim the quality; then relationship is sharing, not completion.

Homework for Day 3

  • Notice the next strong unexplained attraction or familiarity. Ask: “What have I lost, given away, or had taken that I see in this person?”
  • Practise one small act of reclaiming that quality in yourself today.
  • When eyes meet with a stranger and the charge arises, stay present for a breath instead of breaking the moment.
