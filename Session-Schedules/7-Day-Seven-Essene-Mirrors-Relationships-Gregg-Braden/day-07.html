Seven Essene Mirrors · Day 7

Mirror 7 · Perfection Without External Yardstick

The seventh Essene mirror is the most subtle and for some the most difficult. It asks us to allow for the possibility that each experience of life, regardless of its outcome, is perfect in its nature — whether or not we achieve the lofty goals set by others.

We are invited to view our accomplishments without comparing them to anything else, without any external reference. The only way we see ourselves in failure or success is when we measure against an external yardstick. What do we hold ourselves accountable to? Body shape, weight, academics, business, athletic achievement, romance — all are perfect as they stand until compared to someone else’s standard.

We are invited to allow ourselves to be that reference. We are our own most difficult judges. Until we reference outside ourselves, this moment — our best in this moment — is perfect.

The Young Model – What Yardstick Cost a Life

A gifted young woman — athletic, academic, artistic — chose modeling after high school. Agencies asked for changes: tucks, implants, then jaws broken and reset to remove a slight overbite, then floating ribs removed because weight loss made them show. Her body entered a “lose weight” mode and could not stop. She died in hospital from complications of malnutrition. What standard was she holding herself to that made the body she was given feel imperfect? What yardstick required that degree of change?

Dallas–Fort Worth Airport – The Large Woman and the Couple

At DFW, waiting for the tram, an elderly couple (the man hard of hearing) evaluated everyone around them. A woman of about 400 pounds came down the escalator with an old suitcase in extreme heat. The man said, loud enough for all to hear: “Look at that woman — isn’t that terrible? Why doesn’t she do something with herself? She should be ashamed…” Tears welled in her eyes; she never spoke. The three of them — the woman who heard herself judged, the couple who judged, and Gregg who witnessed — each had a rare opportunity. Until we compare a life to an external reference, how can it be anything other than perfect?

What do you use as your yardstick? How do you know you have succeeded or failed until you reference outside yourself? If you are giving your best in this moment, until you compare yourself to someone else, this is perfect.

A paraphrase of a teaching attributed to Jesus: you think the things I do are cool — wait until you see what you are about to do. Don’t put me on a pedestal; you are far greater as you realize the power of thought, feeling and emotion within you.

Look at the places where you are not happy with yourself. The only way that happens is if you have not done your best — or if you have done your best and compared it to someone else.

Toward Compassion

The mirrors invite us to know ourselves in the presence of others. As relationships are reconciled we become the benefit of that healing, and we carry it as we walk between the worlds of heaven and earth.

Compassion begins when we can feel without polarity (anger that replicates the violence), without denial (feeling nothing and rationalizing), and without the need for retribution — simply: I miss those people; this did not have to happen. Thought without attachment to outcome. Feeling without distortion. Emotion without charge.

Key Remember Points

  • Each experience is perfect in its nature until compared to an external yardstick.
  • Be your own reference; you are your most difficult judge.
  • What standard are you holding yourself (and others) to?
  • Compassion: feel without polarity, denial, or need for payback.
  • We are initiates; the mirrors serve our mastery.

Homework for Day 7

  • List the yardsticks you use for body, work, relationships, “success.” Whose standards are they?
  • For one area, practise measuring only against your own best of yesterday.
  • Review all seven mirrors with the people you hold most dear.
  • When you witness suffering, notice: polarity, denial, or the doorway of compassion?
