Devi Navaratri · Day 1

Inner Meaning of Navaratri · Dissolving the Ego

1. Why these nine days?

These ten days — nine nights of Navaratri and Vijayadaśamī — are not ordinary festival days. They are inner-meaning festivals. Across Bhārat we celebrate Devi Navaratri; in Telangana the same current flows as Batukamma.

If we do not know the meaning, the festival becomes only ritual: dance, some joy, then life returns to normal. Every festival has a great meaning, a paramārtha. Our ancestors recognised and established these methods especially for sādhakas — those devoted to ātma-jñāna and dhyāna.

2. The Bhāgavata story — why the earth-touch?

Durgā as the infant girl

In the Bhāgavata, for the soul-plan of Kṛṣṇa’s birth and Kaṃsa’s end, the children are slain; the eighth is exchanged. The girl-child given to Kaṃsa is not ordinary — she is Jaganmātā Durgā, Mahāmāyā.

Kaṃsa seizes her by the feet to dash her on the stone. The moment her feet receive the touch of the earth, she asks Mahāviṣṇu — her brother — why this earth-touch was given. The answer: in the ages to come, much will unfold on this earth-sphere, and the role of Parameśvarī will be great.

Behind the story is a soul-plan. Parameśvara and Parameśvarī are the root of creation. She must come; the whole of nature is filled with her.

3. Nimajjanam — creation, sustenance, dissolution

In these festivals we make the mūrti and later offer it in water — nimajjanam. Gaṇeśa immersion has the same inner design. Whatever is created must also dissolve. We too came into the womb; we too will dissolve. What appears in creation must return into dissolution.

This is not pessimism — it is the structure of reality: sṛṣṭi–sthiti–laya. The outer custom encodes the inner work.

4. The asura who ate himself

Ego dissolves when awareness dawns

In a temple, there is a figure of an asura whose quality is to eat whatever he sees — driven by hunger. Once, when nothing remained, he began to eat himself: legs, arms, torso, until only the head was left. Then awareness (eruka) arose: if I eat this too, I will not remain.

Spiritual science, jñāna, and the path of dhyāna are exactly this: finishing ourselves as the limited person. We entered creation; we must also complete the dissolution. As we move toward mokṣa, the limited “I” dissolves.

5. Where does aham live? — Maṇipūra / Hara centre

Aham — “I, my name, my place, this is mine” — lives in the belly. That is why Rāma’s arrows on the head did not finish Rāvaṇa; the arrow to the belly did. The will-centre sits in Maṇipūra, also called the Hara centre.

Spirituality is finishing this limited aham — dissolving the narrow definition of “I”. That definition is small and full of limits. Our truth is the unlimited “I” that fills everywhere. To know that, this hara-work is needed: finishing our separate will, our “I will do this, I will do that.”

That is the real nimajjanam. Behind every festival structure our ṛṣis and yogis placed yoga-secrets and fine knowledge.

Hara means finishing the limited “I”. Without that, progress in dhyāna is blocked.

6. Night is for sādhana

People often take “night” as a symbol of tamas. For yogis it is different. Night is when no one disturbs you — the purest time for sādhana. Rise in the early hours; sit. Day is for work and the world; night is for the inner work.

Devi Navaratri names the nights because these are nights dedicated to the Shakti who is the root of all that is seen as nature and creation. We call her Durgā, Lalitā, Jaganmātā — many names, one Shakti.

7. Structure of the nine days

Each day has alankāra, colours, flowers — then on the last day, dissolution. This structure matches sṛṣṭi–sthiti–laya. What is in the macrocosm is in the microcosm; what happens above happens here; what happens in the causal worlds happens in the subtle and the gross.

As above, so below.

8. Nava-āvaraṇas · Śrī Cakra

The nine enclosures (nava-āvaraṇas) of the Śrī Cakra are also named for these nine:

  • Trailokya Mohana — she who enchants the three worlds
  • Sarvāśā Paripūraka — fulfilling all aspirations
  • Sarva Saṃkṣobhaṇa — moving all
  • Sarva Saubhāgyadāyaka — granting auspiciousness
  • Sarvārthasādhaka — accomplishing all purposes
  • Sarva Rakṣākara — protecting all
  • Sarva Rogahara — removing all disease
  • Sarva Siddhiprada — granting all siddhis
  • Sarvānandamaya — pure ānanda

She is Śrī Cakra-nivāsinī, Śrī Mātā, Śrī Mahārājñī, Śrīmat Siṃhāsaneśvarī — Lalitā, Durgā, Jaganmātā.

9. Three guṇas × three states = nine

Creation is woven of the three guṇas. We move in three states: jāgrat, svapna, suṣupti. Each state can be coloured by tamas, rajas, or sattva — 3 × 3 = 9.

Tamas is the lowest vibration; rajas the middle; sattva the finer. The goal is to cross the three guṇas. Beyond them is the nirguṇa state — our true ātma-svarūpa, the form of Parabrahman, of Parameśvarī and Parameśvara. In the world everything is with guṇa; our essence is without.

Navaratri is the laboratory to move from guṇa-bound living toward the nirguṇa centre.

10. How to hold these nine days

  • Do not treat the festival as entertainment only — hold the inner meaning.
  • Increase sādhana; night and early morning are especially favourable.
  • Watch the limited “I” (aham) — name, story, ownership — and practice dissolving it in awareness.
  • See difficult people (even family) as indirect teachers when old karma ripens.
  • Keep the feeling: everything is Amma / Shakti; first bond with her.

11. From the session — seeing children as teachers

Q&A essence

When children or family behave as enemies, the cause-effect link is real. Realisation “I gave this, so it returns” is already a shift. Go further: treat them as indirect gurus placed in the house to deliver lessons — kṣamā, karuṇā, unconditional love.

Do not only apologise in the mind; stay in sādhana. When the lessons are received, the hostility softens and the call “Amma” can return. Patience and continuous practice are required.

Homework

  • Morning dhyāna as in Jñānodayam — Anāpāna-sati; stay with the breath.
  • Listen to / recite Mahishāsura Mardinī stotra (Aigiri Nandini…). Read it two or three times; sit with its meaning in meditation. Tomorrow the story will be decoded further.
  • Through the day: notice aham — “I, mine, my story” — and gently release.
  • Extra sādhana if family difficulty is intense: even one hour more of practice raises energy and shifts the angle of seeing.

Remember

  • Festivals without inner meaning become empty ritual.
  • Nimajjanam = dissolution of the limited self.
  • Aham lives in the belly (Maṇipūra / Hara) — that is what must finish.
  • Night is sādhana-time for the yogi.
  • Nine days · nine āvaraṇas · three guṇas × three states.
  • Beyond the three guṇas is nirguṇa — our true nature.
  • Difficult people can be indirect teachers; respond with sādhana, not only reaction.
