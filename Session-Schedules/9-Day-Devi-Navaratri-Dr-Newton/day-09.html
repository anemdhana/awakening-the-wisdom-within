Navami · The Ninth Day · Karma Lessons · Practice Joy · Eve of Vijayadashami

Ninth day of Devi Navaratri, Jñānodayam Day 569. The programme has reached its final teaching day; Vijayadashami celebrations begin tomorrow. Newton guides the collective Anapanasati meditation and Sahasrara Pyramid sankalpa, then receives participant sharings. The session deepens into the understanding that recurring health issues are karma lessons to be learned, not punishments, and that the constant practice of Joy is the way forward. The gathering closes with invitations to the in-person Vanamaitri programme and the Master’s birthday celebrations.

1. Opening — the ninth day and the approach to Vijayadashami

Welcome to the 569th day of Jñānodayam and the ninth day of Navaratri. We have almost reached the end. Dasara celebrations begin tomorrow. Every morning through these nine days we have received the knowledge of Devi Navaratri from Dr. Newton and put it into practice. Today also we prepare ourselves to receive that knowledge, beginning with meditation.

The meditation is Anapanasati — simple and natural. Sit in Sukhasana (chair or floor), close the eyes, and observe the breath exactly as it is. When thoughts arise, the music gently brings the attention back. This is an inner journey. The breath itself is the path.

2. Collective sankalpa — Sahasrara Pyramid and peace on earth

In the final minutes of the meditation the attention is taken to the Sahasrara. The collective sankalpa is offered: that this earth become a peaceful, wise and powerful mandala; that the atma in every being be recognised; that the Sahasrara Pyramid under construction at Quantum Life University be completed and inaugurate a new age of self-knowledge.

The sankalpa includes the geometric structures, crystals and the land itself. The collective energy of all participants is released to the universe. Then the attention returns to the breath. The experience remains within.

3. Participant sharing — Vanajakshi on love of dhyana

Vanajakshi

A participant shared that she comes regularly for meditation and that her love for dhyana has grown. She wanted to speak today, near her son.

Dr Newton

Very good, mother. Those who love meditation are very dear to Sri Krishna Paramatma, to Jaganmata, and to Shiva. Anyone who practises dhyana with such affection is already doing something wonderful. Continue. All the best.

4. Recurring health issues are karma lessons

Prarthana’s question

A participant asked: “I am doing sadhana well, yet health issues keep repeating. You said health is a lesson, but I do not understand which lesson is being taught.”

Dr Newton

Some karmas are long-standing. Whatever has been troubling us for a long time is related to karma. Karmas are lessons, not punishments. They are not meant to hurt us; they are meant to teach. We must learn what is to be learned from them.

Hold the feeling: “Whatever is here is being released. I let go. Past-life patterns are leaving through these symptoms.” Then keep the focus on Joy — Practice Joy, Practice Being Joyful every moment. Remain aware and content in every instant. That is the practice.

For issues specific to femininity (for example menstrual or PCOS patterns), some blind spots may remain. Counselling or guidance from a senior who can hold space often helps the recognition. Take that support.

5. Practice Joy — the constant state

The instruction that has run through all nine days is repeated clearly: Practice Joy. Practice Being Joyful every moment. Whatever karma lessons are still playing out, meet them with the same ananda. The symptoms themselves become the vehicle of release when we stop resisting and stay in joy.

Awareness in every moment is the real practice. Theory has been given; the living of it is what remains.

6. Closing — Vijayadashami and upcoming gatherings

Tomorrow is Vijayadashami. Remaining sharings will be taken as part of the celebrations. The session ends with invitations:

  • In-person Vanamaitri programme on 24–25 October at Quantum Life University campus — meditation in the Master’s presence, connection with nature.
  • Dr. Newton’s birthday and the University’s annual day on Kartika Purnima (24 November this year) — a Jñānodayam reunion. All are welcome, with family and friends.

Until then, continue the sadhana. Meet tomorrow morning at five.

7. Devi Samarpan — continue

  1. Left-nostril inhalations only × 9.
  2. Namaskāra + bow the head toward the earth.
  3. In kumbhaka: mentally Śrīm × 9 (not aloud).
  4. Exhale with full śaraṇu — “I surrender all remaining karma lessons, all resistance to symptoms, all remaining coverings, to Jaganmātā.”
  5. Up to 9 rounds. Stay in the felt sense of Joy.

Homework

  • Continue Devi Samarpan morning (and optional evening).
  • When a recurring symptom or difficulty appears, pause and say: “This is a lesson leaving. I let go.”
  • Practise Joy deliberately for one minute whenever stress or discomfort rises.
  • If a blind spot is suspected, consider speaking with a senior or counsellor who can hold space.
  • Recall: the ninth day is the completion of the teaching; Vijayadashami is the celebration of the victory already won within.

Remember

Navami is the final teaching day of Navaratri. Recurring health issues are karma lessons, not punishments; they teach until the learning is complete. Hold the feeling of release and keep the focus on Joy. Practice Being Joyful every moment. Those who love dhyana are dear to the Divine. Tomorrow is Vijayadashami — the celebration of the victory that the nine days of sadhana have prepared. The same Shakti that has walked with us through all nine days receives the final offering.
