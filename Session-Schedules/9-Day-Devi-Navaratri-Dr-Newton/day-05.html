Devi Navaratri · Day 5

Two Navaratris · Sarvaṃ Khalvidam Brahma · Tṛpti · Six Arishadvargas

Fifth day. Newton distinguishes the two Navaratris and their twin purposes, then deepens the vision of sarvaṃ khalvidam brahma — all this is Brahman. From there he moves into true satisfaction (tṛpti), the zero state of the mind, Mahiṣāsura as the inner vighna, and the detailed mapping of the six arishadvargas (kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, mātsarya) to the six rākṣasas that Mahiṣāsura sends as envoys — together with the short stories of Garuda and of jealousy that ends in a heart attack.

1. Two Navaratris — Chaitra and Śārada

Navaratri comes twice a year. Both are special; both are for sādhana.

  • Chaitra Navaratri (around Rama Navami, March–April) — placed by the ṛṣis and yogis for the direct experience of Aham Brahmāsmi. “Whom am I searching for? Which God, which Brahman, which Kṛṣṇa, which Viṣṇu? Whatever I am searching for — that is I.” The first step is recognising the seeker as the sought.
  • Śārada / Āśvin Navaratri (the present one, roughly September–October) — for the experiential knowing of Sarvaṃ Khalvidam Brahma. Not only “I am Brahman,” but “everything that is, is Brahman.”

These are twin aspects. Aham Brahmāsmi alone can still leave a subtle spiritual ahaṅkāra: “I am the knower; the rest are ignorant.” That is not pure knowledge. Sarvaṃ Khalvidam Brahma completes it: as I am, so are all; the divinity in me is the divinity in all; the nature we see is filled with Paramātman.

If I stop at Aham Brahmāsmi, a spiritual pride can arise. Sarvaṃ Khalvidam Brahma dissolves that — I am not separate from the whole.

2. Prakṛti filled with divinity

Mā Śakti, Prakṛti, is not empty matter. Trees are divine forms, mountains are divine, even stones — all are filled with the Paramātma-tattva. “He is not somewhere else; he is everywhere — in every atom, every particle.”

The story of Prahlāda is recalled: when Hiraṇyakaśipu demanded “Where is your Viṣṇu?” the child pointed to the pillar. The pillar was not empty. The same vision is the fruit of the present Navaratri: not only “I am That,” but “all this is That.”

3. Tṛpti — satisfaction when buddhi expands

Tṛpti is not merely “I got what I wanted.” When buddhi (discriminative intelligence) expands, satisfaction arises with what is already present. A happy nature is the capacity to be satisfied with what is, without the constant demand for more.

The opposite is atyāśā — desire beyond measure, endless dissatisfaction. The fridge is there, a larger one appears in the mind; the phone is bought, next year’s model starts a new thirst. This is the asura pattern of perpetual wanting.

True tṛpti and the zero state of the mind support each other. When the mind settles toward zero (no restless vṛttis), the body recovers, psychosomatic burdens lighten, stress dissolves. Then knowledge can arise: why this illness, what is the cause-effect chain? Self-healing through dhyāna and jñāna is the primary method; external healing is secondary.

4. Aham Brahmāsmi, Ātma-jñāna, Brahma-jñāna, Mokṣa-jñāna

These are not identical labels.

  • Ātma-jñāna — knowing oneself as the ātman, the shift from body-story to awareness.
  • Aham Brahmāsmi — the direct recognition “I am Brahman.”
  • Brahma-jñāna / Sarvaṃ Khalvidam Brahma — the further recognition that this ātman is not separate from the whole.
  • Mokṣa-jñāna — the freedom that arises when both are lived, when the limited “I” no longer binds.

Balance is important. Vinaya (humility) is required. Without humility, even genuine insight can harden into spiritual ahaṅkāra. Dharma experiences — the actual living of right relation — keep the knowledge from becoming dry theory.

Jīvanmukti is possible while still in the body. The knowledge that, once known, all other knowledges are known — that is the one point: Aham Brahmāsmi, Tat Tvam Asi, Sarvaṃ Khalvidam Brahma.

5. Mahiṣāsura, Garutmanta, and the birth of the Śkti

The outer story and its sealed meaning

Mahiṣāsura declares war on Indra, terrifies the devas. They go to the Trimūrti. Viṣṇu comes on Garutmanta (Garuda). Even Garuda is struck and falls; Viṣṇu withdraws. Meaning: Garuda is the symbol of Veda / Upaniṣad knowledge. Mere theory — reading the Vedas, knowing the texts — scores zero in actual sādhana. Practice is required; theory alone does not protect. Rituals and ceremonies are fine when the inner meaning is known, but many get stuck there.

Mahiṣāsura conquers Indra and rules Indraloka. The gods meet. Viṣṇu reveals the secret: Mahiṣāsura cannot be killed by any man; only a woman can end him. From the combined aṃśas of Mahālakṣmī, Durgā, Sarasvatī and the other devatās, a Śkti with eighteen arms arises. Mahiṣāsura, hearing of her beauty, falls into moha and sends six rākṣasas as envoys.

6. The six arishadvargas — mapped to the six rākṣasas

Why six envoys? Ordinary diplomacy sends one or two. Six points to the six internal enemies (arishadvargas) that appear first in sādhana: kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, mātsarya. The Devi Śkti arises precisely to destroy these. In practice the first work is the elimination of these antar-enemies; the Śkti absorbs them into herself.

Rākṣasa name Arishadvarga Inner meaning
Bhāṣpāluḍu / Bhāṣkaluḍu Kāma Bhāṣpa = tears. Desire that brings tears and sorrow when unfulfilled. First enemy.
Durmukhuḍu Krodha When anger rises the face becomes ugly, cruel, distorted. Look at your own face in anger — that is Durmukha. Second enemy.
Tāmramuḍu Lobha Tāmra = copper / red — rajas, craving for wealth, miserliness, atyāśā. Third enemy.
Chichchuruḍu Moha Chit + chura (sword that cuts). Cuts the discrimination of jñāna. Attachment that blinds — like Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhārī unable to correct Duryodhana. Fourth enemy.
Asilomuḍu / Bidāluḍu Mada Pride, ahaṅkāra. Especially the subtle pride that arises in sādhakas. Fifth enemy.
(sixth) Mātsarya Jealousy, envy. The story below shows how it operates unnoticed. Sixth enemy.

After the six are destroyed, a subtler ahaṅkāra / mada still remains in Mahiṣāsura himself — the fine pride that sādhakas must also cut. The Devi Śkti finally destroys that too.

7. Short stories for lobha and mātsarya

Tāmramuḍu — the miser whose wealth brought no peace

A wealthy man thought only of how to hide his money — fear of thieves, fear of income-tax, how to show less, how not to lose it. One night thieves came and took everything. He became a beggar. The wealth never gave him peace or sukha. Lobha destroyed the very thing it tried to protect. A master is always generous — in knowledge and in behaviour. “Dāchi peṭte dōchukobadṭām; panchiṭte penchabadṭām” — hide and you are robbed; share and you are increased.

Mātsarya — the heart-attack wish

A man is granted three boons, with the condition that whatever he asks, his wife receives double. First he asks for gold — she gets double. Second he asks for money — she gets double. On the third wish, jealousy rises unnoticed: he asks for a small heart attack (not fatal). The envy (mātsarya) that wants the other to have less operates even when we do not name it. These are the antar-enemies.

8. Chichchuruḍu — moha of parents

Dhṛtarāṣṭra and Gāndhārī illustrate moha: the son walks the wrong path, yet attachment prevents correction. Parents normally say “become an engineer, a doctor, a business magnate” — things that perish and bring sorrow — but rarely teach the foundation of dhyāna and ātma-jñāna. Upanayana was placed in childhood precisely so that “Tat Tvam Asi” would be heard early and then practised. Foundation first; then any outer success is safe.

9. Śrīdevī Śkti and the washing of the six

Śrīdevī Śkti is the power that clears. In Devi Samarpan, when we fully surrender, that Śkti destroys the arishadvargas and the Mahiṣāsura patterning within. All durguṇas are washed. The bīja Śrīm is repeated only in the mind (para level) during kumbhaka — not aloud.

Manīṣā (the wise who has crossed) can explain to Indra (the mind) the real position. The teaching is practical: grow daivika guṇas by deliberate choice of sattva in food, sleep, speech, and by the daily offering of ahaṅkāra to Amma. Stay in the state “I do not know; what I have known is a grain; what remains is a mountain” — humility keeps the subtle mada from rising.

10. The ostrich and the body

Do not hide the head

Many act like the ostrich: bury the head and say “everything is fine, I have no faults.” While we wear this physical body, there will be faults to correct in the personality complex (body–mind–chitta–buddhi–ahaṅkāra). The ātman itself has no faults — in the ātma-kṣetra we are all divine. But the body-identification carries the dirt. Alertness (eruka) is required; otherwise sādhana is blocked.

11. Devi Samarpan — continue

  1. Left-nostril inhalations only × 9.
  2. Namaskāra + bow the head toward the earth.
  3. In kumbhaka: mentally Śrīm × 9 (not aloud).
  4. Exhale with full śaraṇu — “I surrender all six arishadvargas, all durguṇas, all vighnas, to Jaganmātā.”
  5. Up to 9 rounds.

The Śkti that receives the offering is the same Śkti that clears the inner Mahiṣāsura and the six envoys.

12. Questions & Answers from the session

Participant (Kalpana)

I have overcome many complaints about in-laws and other relations. With my husband, small things still trigger me — I raise my voice, I cry. I understand it is his stage, yet I cannot come out of the reaction. My constant longing is: I want nirvana in the household, brahma-jñāna, ātma-ānanda; I want to share what I know; I want to balance saṃsāra and grow spiritually. This longing never leaves.

Dr Newton

Wonderful. You have already overcome many patterns through sādhana. The remaining triggers with the husband will also be overcome — one success builds confidence for the next. Those who trigger us most are our real gurus. When we react, the experience is ours, not theirs. Recognise: “This is my experience; why am I triggered? What pain is rising?” That awareness frees. Praise can stick us; triggers keep us humble and on the path. Your longing for jñāna while living in the household is itself the fuel. Continue.

Participant (Kavitha) — on birthdays

My son was born on Mahalaya Navami; this year 14 October also came. Should we celebrate on the tithi or on the calendar date? Some families do both.

Dr Newton

Doing both is fine and even better. Western numerical dates are superficial; our roots are here. Celebrate according to the tithi, nakṣatra, lagna, vāra of birth — that is the deeper way. Those born with both a tithi and a calendar date have a bonus: two birthdays. If through dhyāna one reaches enlightenment, that becomes a third birthday — the day of the inner birth. Newton himself celebrates Kārttika Pūrṇimā as his personal birthday and 24 November as the day the world knows.

Homework

  • Continue Devi Samarpan morning (and optional evening).
  • Name one arishadvarga that rose today (kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada or mātsarya) and offer it in the next Samarpan.
  • Notice one moment of atyāśā or dissatisfaction; return to “what is already here is enough for this moment.”
  • When triggered by someone close: “This experience is mine; they are my guru for this lesson.”
  • Hold both Aham Brahmāsmi and Sarvaṃ Khalvidam Brahma — no spiritual pride.

Remember

Chaitra Navaratri for Aham Brahmāsmi; Śārada for Sarvaṃ Khalvidam Brahma. Twin aspects. Prakṛti is filled with divinity. Tṛpti arises when buddhi expands and the mind approaches zero. The six arishadvargas = the six rākṣasa envoys: Bhāṣpāluḍu (kāma), Durmukhuḍu (krodha), Tāmramuḍu (lobha), Chichchuruḍu (moha), Asilomuḍu (mada), and mātsarya. Garuda = theory without practice. Śrīdevī Śkti washes all six through surrender. Those who trigger us are our gurus. Vinaya keeps knowledge alive.
