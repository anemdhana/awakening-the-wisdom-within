Devi Navaratri · Day 3

Suras & Asuras · Svara & Apasvara · Three Guṇas · Icchā Śakti

Third day of the Navaratri jñāna series. We continue from the great consciousness and the sādhana of filling that tattva within. Today: who are suras and asuras, what is svara and apasvara, how heart-rhythm and breath-rhythm mirror alignment, how the three guṇas work in proportions, what nirguṇa looks like, and how the first three days work with Durgā as Icchā-śakti to clear internal blocks.

1. Suras and Asuras — who are they?

In the Purāṇas every festival carries a story; those stories speak a great deal about asuras (rākṣasas) and suras (devatās). Mahiṣāsura is described at length in the Devī Bhāgavata, Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa, Vāmana Purāṇa and others. Like a strong villain in a film, a strong asura elevates the hero by contrast.

Suras = those who live in rhythm, in anusaṅdhāna (alignment) — body, mind, heart, ātman, pūrṇātman, paramātman connected.

Asuras = those in whom that alignment has broken; the rhythm is lost. They are not only “out there.” Spiritual sādhakas understand: Mahiṣāsura, Śumbha–Niśumbha, Rambha, Karambha and the rest live within us.

2. Svara and Apasvara — rhythm in life, speech, heart, breath

Before sura / asura, understand svara and apasvara.

  • Svara — the right note, the right sequence, the right context. In a sabhā, saying what belongs to that context. In music, the rāga held without a wrong note.
  • Apasvara — the wrong note: speaking out of turn, out of context, out of rhythm; a sequence that misses.

Life itself runs on a rhythm. When life moves in that rhythm, it is svara; when it breaks, it is apasvara.

Heart: the heart beats in a rhythm without our conscious effort. When that rhythm becomes erratic — too fast, too slow, irregular — medicine calls it arrhythmia. The “svara” of the heart is lost.

Breath: breath is also svara — candra-svara, sūrya-svara, suṣumnā-svara. Breath becomes speech (vāk); breath becomes prāṇa-śakti; it works at gross, subtle and consciousness levels. If breath is disordered, problems multiply.

Suras live in rhythm and alignment. Asuras have lost that connection — they think one thing, do another, speak a third; body, mind and speech do not match.

3. Misalignment — the asura pattern within

When rhythm is gone:

  • Body may want rest (tamas), mind may race (rajas), speech may promise great sādhana and change — no anusaṅdhāna.
  • Deha-bhrānti: “I am the body” — living only in tamas: laziness, fatigue, postponement (“today’s work tomorrow, next month…”).

These are asura-lakṣaṇas that we can recognise in ourselves. Rāvaṇa was born a brāhmaṇa by kulam, but birth does not make one a brāhmaṇa; he became asura by quality.

4. Three guṇas — proportions, not labels

Everything arises from the three guṇas in different proportions and combinations. That is why each “villain” has a distinct flavour.

In a sādhaka (approximate balance Newton names):

  • Tamas — very small percentage (~1%). Needed a little; without any tamas one would not rest.
  • Rajas — modest (~8–9%). Needed for dynamism; without it one would not step out of the house.
  • Sattva — the large share in dhyāna practitioners. Heart-centre open; kindness; “they wish well for all.”

In asura patterning: tamas and rajas become disproportionate. Excess tamas → himsā, laziness, ahaṅkāra even in what little is done, addiction to praise. Excess rajas → craving for kīrti and pratiṣṭhā, climbing for recognition.

Desire itself is not wrong — without icchā there is no creation. Atyāśā (desire beyond measure) is what falls under asura: “I must have more wealth than everyone” rather than simply needing resources.

5. Nirguṇa — beyond the three guṇas

Those who cross the three guṇas — nirguṇa — live differently:

  • Their icchā is: “Along with me, may all receive this ānanda; I will also be an instrument (uḍata-sahāyam — the squirrel’s share).”
  • They know: the ātman in me is the ātman in all; sarvaṃ khalvidaṃ brahma.
  • Continuous sevā, continuous sādhana, continuous wishing for the good of all — sarve janāḥ sukhino bhavantu.
  • Their yajñas and programmes are for loka-kalyāṇa.

Tamas-pradhāna people are gripped by ahaṅkāra and mamakāra — often without noticing. From ahaṅkāra everything else sprouts.

6. Asura-guṇas — what not to do, said as story

Himsā, wrong vāk, boundary violation

True sanātana dharma is ahimsā — not harming. Asura-guṇas: harming, disturbing ṛṣis in tapa, pouring hot oil on the fire of their dhyāna, breaking others’ practice.

Wrong speech — saying what should not be said; no proper vāk, no proper conduct, no proper dṛṣṭi or thought. These express as himsā and asura behaviour.

Stories (same inner lesson):

  • Rāvaṇa — boundary violation: took Sītā without consent; separated husband and wife; inflicted pain. Did what must not be done.
  • Mahiṣāsura — did what must not be done (ego, force, adharma).
  • Bhaṇḍāsura, Vṛtrāsura — same pattern: asura-guṇa in different costumes.

Asuras in the stories also do tapa and dhyāna — but with intense ahaṅkāra and mamakāra. Outer practice without inner alignment still feeds the asura.

7. The war is inside — nine nights for transformation

Every Purāṇic story of sura–asura war has one essence: the battle is within. Outer enemies and rākṣasas are secondary; the real field is the antaḥkaraṇa.

These nine nights are especially favourable for sādhakas. Use them. Inner transformation needs sustained practice; in these supportive days the Devī-śakti that pervades the earth-sphere can be drawn upon more fully.

8. Three expressions of Śrī Śakti — first three days = Durgā / Icchā

Parāśakti / Ādiśakti is one; she expresses principally as three:

  • Durgā / Mahākālī — Icchā-śakti (will / desire-power)
  • Mahālakṣmī — Kriyā-śakti (power of action)
  • Mahāsarasvatī — Jñāna-śakti (power of knowledge)

First three days — Mahādurgā: she who takes us across the durgama — the difficult, the obstructing. In simple language: internal blocks. There are no real blocks “outside”; everything plays out within. These three days: clear the blocks with Durgā’s śakti — Icchā-śakti.

Icchā-śakti means: ask Amma. She is karuṇāmayī; when the child asks, the mother’s heart moves. Desire is not wrong; right desire directed to Amma clears the limitations and the repeating patterns that hinder sādhana.

First recognise the block with eruka (awareness). Then use Icchā-śakti — ask Amma — and clear it through dhyāna-sādhana. Do not live like the ostrich.

9. The ostrich — denying the block

Nippu-kōḍi (ostrich) analogy

When a predator approaches, the ostrich runs, then buries its head in the sand and behaves as if the enemy is gone — eyes closed, illusion. The enemy is still there.

In life we often do the same: “I have no blocks; everything is fine” — while sādhana does not move, duty is postponed, the same patterns repeat. The block is known somewhere, but we close the eyes. Icchā-śakti begins when we open the eyes, name the block, and ask Amma for the power to cross it.

10. What do I really want? — going deeper

Ask several times, going inward:

  • What do I want?
  • What do I really really want?
  • If this outer difficulty resolved, what would I feel?
  • If complete praśāntatā came, what then? → ānanda.
  • If ānanda came, what then? → the wish to share ānanda with all.
  • If I share ānanda with all, what do I experience?

Question after question, deeper and deeper, until clarity arises at the level of cit / ātman: what you are truly looking at. That clarity is needed.

11. Participant Q&A (session highlights)

Family / relationship difficulty & sādhana: Stay in practice. Raise energy; the angle of seeing changes. Difficult people can be part of the soul-plan — lessons in kṣamā and karuṇā. Do not abandon sādhana when it is hard.

Husband and dhyāna-family; financial settling so larger lokārthaka work can happen: Newton affirmed the sankalpa. When the family is already a dhyāna-family and the heart wants resources in order to serve more people, Lakṣmī stays with such people. Clarity of “what do I really want” — ātma-tṛpti, resources for others’ good — is itself Icchā aligned with Amma.

Co-created realities (e.g. history, films on masters): Great gurus respect each jīvātman’s plan. They do not always “stop” collective dramas with a snap of the fingers; free will and evolutionary experience are respected. They stand beyond right/wrong, victim/perpetrator — complete acceptance from ātma-dṛṣṭi. Short-cuts to enlightenment for the whole world are not the design; each must go through their sādhana.

Hanumān / Anjaneya film & siddhis: Outer display of power attracts non-meditators toward the divine; the deeper point remains sādhana and alignment, not fascination with miracles alone.

12. How to hold Day 3

  • Notice svara vs apasvara in speech, schedule, breath, and heart-state.
  • Watch for misalignment: thinking / speaking / doing not matching.
  • Sense the three guṇas in proportion — especially excess tamas (postponement, himsā-tone) or rajas (recognition-hunger).
  • Name one internal block honestly (no ostrich). Offer it to Durgā as Icchā-śakti: “Amma, this is what I ask to cross.”
  • Ask “What do I really want?” until it reaches ānanda and the wish to share it.
  • Continue Devi Samarpan / inward energy from Day 2; multiply energy within.

Summary

Suras = rhythm + alignment. Asuras = broken rhythm + asura-guṇas (himsā, wrong vāk, ahaṅkāra, atyāśā). Svara lives in heart-beat, breath, speech and life-sequence; arrhythmia and apasvara are the same principle. Three guṇas need proportion (for sādhakas: mostly sattva, a little rajas, minimal tamas). Nirguṇa = continuous sevā and sarve janāḥ sukhino bhavantu. The Purāṇic wars are inside. First three days: Durgā as Icchā-śakti clears internal blocks — recognise, ask Amma, do not bury the head in the sand. Clarity of real desire leads to ānanda shared with all.
