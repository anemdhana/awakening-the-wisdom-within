Devi Navaratri · Day 3

Suras & Asuras · Svara · Three Guṇas · Icchā Śakti · Mind Fasting · Divine Forces

Third day of the Navaratri jñāna series. Who are suras and asuras; what is svara and apasvara; how heart- and breath-rhythm mirror alignment; the three guṇas in proportion; nirguṇa; the nine days as three blocks (Durgā / Lakṣmī / Sarasvatī); Icchā-śakti clearing internal blocks; Lahiri Mahāśaya’s last desire; mind / social / reels fasting and mauna; Lalitā’s army (Vārāhī, ministers); Kālikā Mātā; and what ātma-jñāna, brahma-jñāna and Sarvāntaryāmī point to.

1. Suras and Asuras — who are they?

In the Purāṇas every festival carries a story; those stories speak a great deal about asuras (rākṣasas) and suras (devatās). Mahiṣāsura is described at length in the Devī Bhāgavata, Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa, Vāmana Purāṇa and others. Like a strong villain in a film, a strong asura elevates the hero by contrast.

Suras = those who live in rhythm, in anusaṅdhāna (alignment) — body, mind, heart, ātman, pūrṇātman, paramātman connected.

Asuras = those in whom that alignment has broken; the rhythm is lost. They are not only “out there.” Spiritual sādhakas understand: Mahiṣāsura, Śumbha–Niśumbha, Rambha, Karambha and the rest live within us.

2. Svara and Apasvara — rhythm in life, speech, heart, breath

Before sura / asura, understand svara and apasvara.

  • Svara — the right note, the right sequence, the right context. In a sabhā, saying what belongs to that context. In music, the rāga held without a wrong note.
  • Apasvara — the wrong note: speaking out of turn, out of context, out of rhythm; a sequence that misses.

Life itself runs on a rhythm. When life moves in that rhythm, it is svara; when it breaks, it is apasvara.

Heart: the heart beats in a rhythm without our conscious effort. When that rhythm becomes erratic — too fast, too slow, irregular — medicine calls it arrhythmia. The “svara” of the heart is lost.

Breath: breath is also svara — candra-svara, sūrya-svara, suṣumnā-svara. Breath becomes speech (vāk); breath becomes prāṇa-śakti; it works at gross, subtle and consciousness levels. If breath is disordered, problems multiply.

Suras live in rhythm and alignment. Asuras have lost that connection — they think one thing, do another, speak a third; body, mind and speech do not match.

3. Misalignment — the asura pattern within

When rhythm is gone:

  • Body may want rest (tamas), mind may race (rajas), speech may promise great sādhana and change — no anusaṅdhāna.
  • Deha-bhrānti: “I am the body” — living only in tamas: laziness, fatigue, postponement (“today’s work tomorrow, next month…”).

These are asura-lakṣaṇas that we can recognise in ourselves. Rāvaṇa was born a brāhmaṇa by kulam, but birth does not make one a brāhmaṇa; he became asura by quality.

4. Three guṇas — proportions, not labels

Everything arises from the three guṇas in different proportions and combinations. That is why each “villain” has a distinct flavour.

In a sādhaka (approximate balance Newton names):

  • Tamas — very small percentage (~1%). Needed a little; without any tamas one would not rest.
  • Rajas — modest (~8–9%). Needed for dynamism; without it one would not step out of the house.
  • Sattva — the large share in dhyāna practitioners. Heart-centre open; kindness; “they wish well for all.”

In asura patterning: tamas and rajas become disproportionate. Excess tamas → himsā, laziness, ahaṅkāra even in what little is done, addiction to praise. Excess rajas → craving for kīrti and pratiṣṭhā, climbing for recognition.

Desire itself is not wrong — without icchā there is no creation. Atyāśā (desire beyond measure) is what falls under asura: “I must have more wealth than everyone” rather than simply needing resources.

5. Nirguṇa — beyond the three guṇas

Those who cross the three guṇas — nirguṇa — live differently:

  • Their icchā is: “Along with me, may all receive this ānanda; I will also be an instrument (uḍata-sahāyam — the squirrel’s share).”
  • They know: the ātman in me is the ātman in all; sarvaṃ khalvidaṃ brahma.
  • Continuous sevā, continuous sādhana, continuous wishing for the good of all — sarve janāḥ sukhino bhavantu.
  • Their yajñas and programmes are for loka-kalyāṇa.

Tamas-pradhāna people are gripped by ahaṅkāra and mamakāra — often without noticing. From ahaṅkāra everything else sprouts.

6. Asura-guṇas — what not to do, said as story

Himsā, wrong vāk, boundary violation

True sanātana dharma is ahimsā — not harming. Asura-guṇas: harming, disturbing ṛṣis in tapa, pouring hot oil on the fire of their dhyāna, breaking others’ practice.

Wrong speech — saying what should not be said; no proper vāk, no proper conduct, no proper dṛṣṭi or thought. These express as himsā and asura behaviour.

Stories (same inner lesson):

  • Rāvaṇa — boundary violation: took Sītā without consent; separated husband and wife; inflicted pain.
  • Mahiṣāsura, Bhaṇḍāsura, Vṛtrāsura — same pattern: asura-guṇa in different costumes. Outer tapa with intense ahaṅkāra still feeds the asura.

7. The war is inside — nine nights for transformation

Every Purāṇic story of sura–asura war has one essence: the battle is within. Outer enemies are secondary; the real field is the antaḥkaraṇa. These nine nights are especially favourable for sādhakas — use them.

8. Nine days in three blocks — Durgā · Lakṣmī · Sarasvatī

Parāśakti / Ādiśakti is one; she expresses principally as three. The festival itself is structured around them:

  • Days 1–3 — Mahādurgā / Kālī — Icchā-śakti (will / desire-power). She who takes us across the durgama — the difficult, the obstructing. In simple language: internal blocks. There are no real blocks “outside”; everything plays out within. Clear the blocks with Durgā’s śakti.
  • Days 4–6 — Mahālakṣmī — Kriyā-śakti (power of action). After blocks loosen, energy moves into right action, resource, and outer flow aligned with the inner.
  • Days 7–9 — Mahāsarasvatī — Jñāna-śakti (power of knowledge). Clarity, discrimination, the flowering of understanding.

We are children before that Mother. When anything is hard, a child runs first to Amma — not first to Father. Amma reports, Amma convinces, Amma arranges. These nine days: stay at Amma’s feet. Amma = Śakti = Ādiśakti / Parāśakti in many forms.

Icchā-śakti means: ask Amma. She is karuṇāmayī; when the child asks, the mother’s heart moves. Desire is not wrong; right desire directed to Amma clears limitations and repeating patterns that hinder sādhana.

9. Goals — give the energy a direction

Without direction, energy is wasted or misused. Newton had already spoken of writing three goals — give the śakti a clear direction. Until life-purpose (jīvita-lakṣya / ātma-plan) becomes clear through sādhana, still set interim goals. Vague “I don’t know what I want” leaves the current without a channel.

Especially in these first three days of Icchā-śakti: write clearly what you ask Amma to clear and what you truly want — then place those desires at her feet.

10. Lahiri Mahāśaya — the last desire

One remaining icchā can still stop the final step

Lahiri Mahāśaya had one desire left from a past life: to live in the feeling of a royal palace. In this birth he did not neglect udyoga-dharma; he worked. On duty at Ranikhet, in free time an inner pull took him up the mountain. At a cave, a person was waiting — Mahāvatār Bābājī.

Bābājī: more than fifty years he had stayed there; he had been waiting for this disciple — spoken of as on the order of a hundred and fifty years of waiting. “Do you recognise me? Your things are here.” Lahiri did not remember. Bābājī showed the kamaṇḍalu, āsana, darbha — a flash of smṛti; full knowing needs dhyāna-sādhana.

Lahiri was already at the peak of ātma-jñāna; one step remained. That single leftover icchā can stop completion. What gurus and Devī Mātā do is remove what blocks the spiritual journey so the path becomes smooth — especially for those whose desire is loka-kalyāṇa, that this knowledge reach all.

Bābājī created a royal palace next to the cave; for twenty-four hours the desire was fulfilled in fullness. The cave is still visited in the Ranikhet region. Point: What do you want? Many never ask. From childhood almost no one asked us truly. Ask. Ask again. Place it with Amma / Icchā-śakti.

11. What do I really want? — going deeper

Ask several times, going inward:

  • What do I want?
  • What do I really really want?
  • If this outer difficulty resolved, what would I feel? → often mental peace.
  • If complete praśāntatā came, what then? → more dhyāna; staying with myself.
  • If I stay with myself, what then? → ātma-ānanda; ātma-jñāna.
  • If ātma-jñāna, what then? → fulfilment, pūrṇatva (completeness).
  • If ānanda / completeness, what then? → the wish to share it with all.

Outer wanting and inner goal are often different. These three days: know what you want, write two or three clear asks, place them at Amma’s feet. Her śakti removes even mountain-like blocks.

12. Ātma-jñāna · Brahma-jñāna · Sarvāntaryāmī

Ātma-jñāna — knowing oneself as the ātman; the shift from “I am this body-story” to the awareness that is aware. From the deeper questioning above, ātma-ānanda and ātma-jñāna arise when one can stay with oneself in dhyāna.

Brahma-jñāna — the further peak: not only “I am ātman” but the recognition that this ātman is not separate from the whole — sarvaṃ khalvidaṃ brahma. Lahiri was already at the peak of ātma-jñāna; the last icchā was the remaining step toward complete freedom in that knowing.

Sarvāntaryāmī — the Lord as the inner controller of all, nearer than near. The asura pattern is to run outward, circling everywhere, while the Divine is already in sāyujya close by. When we chase phones, new models, outer thrill every year, we move away from that nearness — and himsā and directionlessness increase. Navaratri is a time to turn back toward the One who is already within as Sarvāntaryāmī.

13. Fasting — mind, social media, reels, mauna

In these nine days, fast in more than one sense:

  • Mind fasting — empty the mind of the usual traffic of thoughts; stay in inner quiet.
  • Mauna — not only outer silence; antar-mauna as well. Conserve energy; do not scatter it outward.
  • Social media fasting / reels fasting — step back from the feed that pulls time and attention out. These are addictions that take us away from ourselves — a form of asuratva: whatever distances me from myself, recognise it and return.
  • Food — if you eat three times, move toward two; or eka-bhukta / very limited quantity. Not permanent rule after the nine days — but use the time well.
  • Shopping malls, cinema, outings — not “forbidden forever after day nine,” but during sādhana, use time deliberately for the inner work.

14. Lalitā’s army · Vārāhī · Kālikā — divine forces

Names are many; forces are real

With Lalitā Mātā there are great battalions — a vast army. Vārāhī Mātā is spoken of in the role of minister; other forms such as Rājaśyāmalā also stand in that court. Ādiśakti has countless forms. All of these that we visualise as devatā-śaktis are divine forces, divine energies — different names for aspects of the same power. You need not memorise every name; know that the power that clears the durgama is real.

Kālikā Mātā: she can make the mute a great musician; she wrote on the tongue of the ignorant and made a master — the stream of stories around Kālidāsa and Tenāli Rāmalinga points to the same: if that śakti wills, nothing is asādhya; all becomes susādhya. That śakti does it all.

So place the hard blocks with Amma. Durgā / Mahādurgā / Icchā-śakti removes antarāyas. The army of Lalitā, Vārāhī, Kālikā — divine energies — support the clearing.

15. The ostrich — denying the block

Nippu-kōḍi (ostrich) analogy

When a predator approaches, the ostrich runs, then buries its head in the sand and behaves as if the enemy is gone. The enemy is still there. In life: “I have no blocks” while sādhana does not move and patterns repeat. Icchā-śakti begins when we open the eyes, name the block, and ask Amma for the power to cross it.

16. Participant Q&A (session highlights)

Family / relationship difficulty & sādhana: Stay in practice. Raise energy; the angle of seeing changes. Difficult people can be part of the soul-plan — lessons in kṣamā and karuṇā.

Dhyāna-family; resources for larger lokārthaka work: When the heart wants resources in order to serve more people, Lakṣmī stays with such people. Clarity of real desire — ātma-tṛpti plus others’ good — is Icchā aligned with Amma.

Co-created realities: Great gurus respect each jīvātman’s plan; they stand beyond right/wrong from ātma-dṛṣṭi. Each must go through their sādhana; no universal short-cut snap.

Outer siddhis / films: Display can draw non-meditators toward the divine; the deeper point remains sādhana and alignment.

17. How to hold Day 3

  • Notice svara vs apasvara in speech, schedule, breath, and heart-state.
  • Watch misalignment of thinking / speaking / doing; sense guṇa proportions.
  • Name one internal block honestly. Write 2–3 clear goals / asks. Offer them to Durgā as Icchā-śakti.
  • Ask “What do I really want?” until it reaches ātma-ānanda, pūrṇatva, and the wish to share.
  • Mind fasting · mauna · social / reels fasting — conserve energy; turn toward Sarvāntaryāmī within.
  • Remember the nine-day map: days 1–3 Icchā (Durgā), 4–6 Kriyā (Lakṣmī), 7–9 Jñāna (Sarasvatī).
  • Continue Devi Samarpan (left leg up, left-nostril emphasis, Śrīm, surrender) as on Day 2.

Summary

Suras = rhythm + alignment. Asuras = broken rhythm + asura-guṇas. Svara lives in heart, breath, speech and life-sequence. Guṇas need proportion; nirguṇa = continuous sevā and sarve janāḥ sukhino bhavantu. Nine days = Durgā (Icchā) · Lakṣmī (Kriyā) · Sarasvatī (Jñāna). First three days: clear internal blocks — ask Amma; write goals; no ostrich. Lahiri’s story: one leftover desire can delay the last step; the Guru / Devī fulfils and frees. Fast the mind, the feed, and excess speech. Lalitā’s army, Vārāhī, Kālikā — divine forces that make the impossible possible. Ātma-jñāna → brahma-jñāna; Sarvāntaryāmī is already nearer than the chase outside.
