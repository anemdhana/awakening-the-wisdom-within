Devi Navaratri · Day 4 · From Devi Bhāgavata

Rambha & Karambha · Putra · Cleaning Inner Dirt

Fourth day. Every story in our granthas carries a gūḍhārtha — a sealed meaning. Taken only as outer narrative, the festival ends and life returns to “normal.” Taken as sādhana-rahasya, each figure is an aspect inside us. Today: how Mahiṣāsura’s line begins — Danu → Rambha & Karambha — and what “putra,” mind-as-Indra, and inner dirt really mean.

1. Cause and effect — every birth has a reason

Jaganmātā, Parāśakti, appears in many forms and aṃśas for a reason. Nothing arises without cause. Our janma has a cause; creation has a cause. The Purāṇic stories of Devi Navaratri are given in symbolic form by the ṛṣis. The more clearly we grasp the inner meaning, the more sādhana-secret we extract. Mere joyful “feel” of the festival is not enough.

These nine days are also like the nine months in the mother’s womb — a new birth into the dimension of Ādiśakti / Parāśakti / Śrīdevī. Today we take the background of how Mahiṣāsura arose.

2. Danu = tanu — body identification

In the Devi Bhāgavata line as taught here, the father of these asuras is named Danu. Hearing “Danu,” the mind can also hear tanu — body. Symbolically this is pure body identification: deha-bhrānti, tāmasa pravṛtti — “I am only this body,” only physical identity.

From that Danu are born two sons: Rambha and Karambha. The paired names point to dvaita — dual aspects operating in us, not only ancient outer villains.

3. Rambha — aiḥika vāñchā (worldly desire)

Both brothers do intense tapas for thousands of years — for putra-lābha. Their story is written like ordinary human life. But Rambha and Karambha are also aspects within us who “meditate” with a goal.

Rambha is taught as aiḥika vāñchā — intense desire for this world: “I want this, I want that.” Fridge already at home — a larger one appears. Phone bought — next year’s model starts a new thirst. Thoughts of wanting do not stop.

Tapas in water — emotion and māyā

Rambha does tapas inside a water pond. Water is a symbol of emotion. When what we planned does not happen, we are upset, disappointed, grieved. The object-world never arranges itself exactly as the mind demands.

Water also hides: what falls in a river is not visible like something on land. So water stands for māyā. Rambha’s practice “inside water” = trying to do sādhana while still immersed in emotional māyā and worldly wanting.

4. Karambha — seeking mokṣa-jñāna; five fires = five senses

Karambha practises: “I must gain mokṣa-jñāna; I must know myself.” He sits amid pañcāgni — five fires, sun above. As symbol: the five jñānendriyas constantly pull us outward. To withdraw them and become antarmukha is the inner meaning.

Even as an “asura,” he has some sattva mixed with rajas and tamas — he practises, but still within māyā.

5. Indra = the mind — the old form that pulls the sādhaka

Indra’s jealousy and the metaphor

Indra grows jealous: “If they gain boons, my post will fall.” He comes as an old man, grabs Rambha’s leg in the pond, pulls him out — Rambha’s body is vacated; practice ends.

Deeper meaning: The foremost “indriya” is the mind itself. Whoever starts dhyāna, the mind first obstructs. We try citta-vṛtti-nirodha through the breath; still the mind, like Indra in disguise, catches the leg and pulls the sādhaka out of the pond of practice.

6. Karambha offers the head to Agni — mind into ātman

Seeing the brother’s fate, Karambha intensifies practice and offers his head into the fire. Symbol: the head is where the mind sits; offering the head to Agni = merging the mind into ātma-tattva / Paramātma-tattva.

Agni appears and says: ask a boon. Karambha still asks for a mighty son — the same putra-wish. Even deep practice can still carry the old goal-language until the meaning of “putra” itself is corrected.

Paramātma-tattva is aṇor aṇīyān mahato mahīyān — subtler than the atom, greater than the great. The universe expands on the order of tens of millions of light-years; that vastness is still within the field of the One.

7. Putra — protection from punah-naraka (not only “a male child”)

Traditional saying: the putra saves from put-naraka. Misread, this has fuelled preference for male children and terrible wrongs. That is a tragic misinterpretation.

  • Put ≈ hell / the binding cycle
  • Tra ≈ that which protects / saves

Putra = that which protects from punah-naraka — repeated birth and death. The real “hell” is returning again and again through unfinished thirst. Symbolically we have passed through countless yonis (the “86 lakh” figure). Human birth is rare; many births are spent only in postponement.

A dvija is “twice-born”: before dhyāna and after dhyāna in the same lifetime. That inner rebirth is the putra that truly saves — not the gender of a child.

Life is not a 9-to-5 job. When dissatisfaction is there, do dhyāna, know who you are, then live in purpose — not postponement.

8. Wasted births · soul plan · Bhaja Govindam tone

Incomplete lifetimes: we decide “this birth I will do this,” then postpone. Living only for livelihood without “who am I?” wastes the opportunity of human birth.

Stay not only in bhāva-nakṣatra (mood-weather of the mind) but in the star of Śiva / ātman — in Paramātma-tattva. Live according to the soul plan, not only the social timetable.

9. The dirty cloth — vāsanās and how dirt leaves

Master and the soiled cloth

A seeker goes to a siddha. The master gives a dirty cloth: clean it. Water alone — some dirt goes, much remains. Soap and rubbing — dirt lessens; smell remains. Thorough wash again — odour also leaves.

Meaning: We carry saṃskāras and vāsanās across many births — asuric tendencies, tamas, rajas-ahaṅkāra. Surface washing is not enough. Smell = subtle residue. Repeated, thorough inner washing is needed.

10. Main practice point — cause of sorrow is my avidyā

Today’s central point:

  • When sorrow comes — the cause is not the other.
  • The cause of my sorrow is my avidyā / ajñāna.
  • The cause of my joy is also how the “I” is structured.

Hold this one truth. Clean the inner dirt. Grow divine qualities. Rambha, Karambha, and Mahiṣāsura are patterns in us; Devi’s work is clearing those patterns.

There is a great treasure in your own courtyard. Dig three feet in your own yard — the mine is already there. We seek outside; the kṣetra of the ātman is within.

11. Devi Samarpan — continue the process

  1. Block right nostril; nine successive inhalations only through the left.
  2. Namaskāra mudrā; while exhaling, bow the head toward the earth.
  3. Complete surrender to Jaganmātā / Ādiśakti / Kuṇḍalinī Mātā.
  4. Hold; mentally repeat Śrīm nine times.
  5. Rise slowly. Up to nine rounds.

Bowing = offering ahaṅkāra to the Mother of mothers.

12. Session close · Q&A note

Hands raised for questions were taken forward to the next class so the Mahiṣāsura thread could continue. The teaching itself is the Q&A of the inner life: every story answers “what in me is this?”

Homework

  • Devi Samarpan — morning (and optional evening), up to 9 rounds.
  • When upset: “Cause is my avidyā, not the other.”
  • Watch Rambha in you (wanting) and Karambha in you (practice still mixed with old goals).
  • Do not postpone knowing who you are; then live on purpose.

Remember

Danu = body-only identity. Rambha = worldly thirst + emotion-māyā. Karambha = practice aimed at mokṣa still asking for “putra.” Indra = mind that pulls the sādhaka out. Putra = what saves from the birth–death naraka — inner second birth through jñāna. Dirty cloth = vāsanās need repeated deep wash. Sorrow’s cause is my ignorance; treasure is in my own yard.
