Prakriti as the Mother · Sapta Jnana Bhumikas · Standing for Dharma

Seventh day of Devi Navaratri. Newton continues the sadhana under direct guidance. The teaching turns to Prakriti itself as the Mother — harming nature is harming the Devi. Every outer war between devas and asuras is the inner war of the gunas. The session deepens into the Sapta Jnana Bhumikas, the necessity of remaining open, and the Arjuna-like duty to stand against adharma. Participant sharings include healing through soul-order and the power of written sankalpas offered at the Mother’s feet.

1. The final stretch of Navaratri — sadhana only

Navaratri exists only for sadhana. Whatever change must be brought within through the Devi’s Shakti is the purpose of these nine days. Every puranic story of asuras and rakshasas is to be seen inwardly: the war between tamas, rajas and sattva is the real war.

The fruits of the preceding days begin to be tasted. Iccha Shakti, Kriya Shakti and Jnana Shakti were covered earlier; the later days belong especially to the purification that comes through Knowledge.

2. Prakriti is the Mother — harming nature is harming the Devi

The same Paramatma tattva that is worshipped is present in all of nature. When forests are cut, rivers and oceans polluted with plastic, and animals die, we are destroying the Mother herself. On one side we go to the temple and perform puja; on the other side we throw plastic into the same rivers. Without jnana and buddhi we do not even know what we are doing.

The collective consequences must be borne by everyone. The teaching is simple: as human beings we must cause no harm to Prakriti. Prakriti is none other than the Devi we call Mahishasura Mardini, Chamundeshwari, Chandi, Durga. She is everywhere in nature.

3. Outer and inner — as within, so without

Whatever is written as a war between devas and asuras is the war of the gunas within. “As within, so without.” If we attract many outer enemies, it indicates that inner enemies are active. Outer circumstances keep showing us what inner change is required.

The rishis gave this knowledge in symbolic form. Taking only the outer story yields entertainment; taking the inner meaning yields transformation.

4. Sapta Jnana Bhumikas — the seven stages of knowledge

Newton turns to the classical Sapta Jnana Bhumikas. These are progressive stages of jnana that the sadhaka passes through. Each stage purifies a deeper layer. The final stages culminate in the recognition that the Mother is not separate — she is the support of every chakra and of the whole creation.

Dhyana is the means. Through meditation antar-shuddhi occurs; siddhi and buddhi arise. Both gross and subtle ahankara are purified. The stages of nearness (Salokya, Samipya, Sarupya) appear on the way to the full recognition.

5. Remaining open — blocks, root cause, and not escaping

On this path one must stay open. Do not assume in advance “I have blocks.” Emotions are indicators: behind a feeling is a thought, behind the thought a belief, behind the belief a memory. Looking for the root cause when suffering is present is not over-analysis; searching when there is no problem is unnecessary.

Spirituality is not an escape. Saying “everything is fine” and running toward sannyasa is a defence. The Shakti is given so that we can face whatever challenge appears. Do not generalise that everyone carries the same coverings.

6. Arjuna-like dharma — stand against adharma

Participant sharing

A participant shared deep experiences, including giving an order to the soul: “Do not leave my body until it is healed; take Hanuman’s help if needed.” Pain reduced. She also wrote three sankalpas and placed them at the Mother’s feet: to live as a helper, to show the right path to her children and many others, and to share wealth with all.

Dr Newton

Wonderful. Wherever there is injustice one must speak and stop it — like Arjuna. Especially toward women, elders, children, and those who cannot protect themselves. Do it from the soul, without reaction and without later guilt. That is dharma. Conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis have a cause; when the lessons are received they leave. The three sankalpas offered at Amma’s feet are powerful. Continue the sadhana.

7. Devi Samarpan — continue

  1. Left-nostril inhalations only × 9.
  2. Namaskāra + bow the head toward the earth.
  3. In kumbhaka: mentally Śrīm × 9 (not aloud).
  4. Exhale with full śaraṇu — “I surrender all harm done to Prakriti, all remaining guna-doshas, all coverings on the Inner Master, to Jaganmātā.”
  5. Up to 9 rounds. Stay in the felt sense of ananda.

Homework

  • Continue Devi Samarpan morning (and optional evening).
  • Notice one way you interact with nature today and offer any harm or indifference.
  • If an injustice appears, pause and ask: “Can I speak from the soul like Arjuna, without reaction?”
  • Sit an extra period of silent dhyana.
  • Recall: Prakriti is the Mother; the outer war is the inner guna war; the Sapta Jnana Bhumikas are walked by remaining open.

Remember

Prakriti is the Devi herself; harming nature is harming the Mother. As within, so without — outer enemies indicate inner ones. The Sapta Jnana Bhumikas are successive stages of jnana opened by dhyana. Stay open; do not generalise blocks; face life. Stand against adharma from the soul, like Arjuna. Written sankalpas placed at the Mother’s feet carry power. Practice Joy. The same Shakti that destroys the asuras within receives the offering.
