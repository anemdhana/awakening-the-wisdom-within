Saptami · Sumbha and Nisumbha · Staying Open · Inner Blocks

Seventh day (Saptami), Jñānodayam Day 567. The final three days of Navaratri are especially significant — the fruits of the preceding sadhana begin to be experienced. Newton focuses on Sumbha and Nisumbha as the circling tendencies of sukha and dukha, the necessity of remaining open rather than assuming blocks, and the recognition that every person carries an Inner Master covered by layers. Participant sharing continues the practice of meeting life with awareness.

1. Saptami — the final three days and Maha Saraswati

Saptami, Ashtami, Navami — these last three days are held in high regard because the results of the sadhana done so far start to appear in experience. Earlier days covered Iccha Shakti, Kriya Shakti and Jnana Shakti (Mahadurga, Mahalakshmi, Maha Saraswati). These final days belong especially to Maha Saraswati — purification that happens through the sword of Knowledge.

Every story from Devi Bhagavatam and Markandeya Purana is to be taken with its inner meaning. The outer story alone gives entertainment; the inner meaning gives transformation. Many asuras were destroyed by Kalika Mata and Chandi Mata — all of them are symbolic of the inner enemies.

2. Sumbha and Nisumbha — the circles of sukha and dukha

Sumbha and Nisumbha are brothers. In the sadhaka, the tendency toward pleasure (sukha vasana) is symbolised by Sumbha. Nisumbha represents the tendency toward sorrow (dukha vasana). These are mental vrittis. Vritti means circle — they keep turning us in the same loop.

Wherever sukha is, dukha also comes. They never leave each other. When we desire sukha, dukha arrives with it. Their root is ahankara and mamakara. These tendencies live in the subconscious mind and keep saying “I want this, I want that.”

3. Staying open — do not assume blocks

On this path one must remain open. Do not assume in advance “I have blocks.” Understand your own life: how you react emotionally, what feelings arise. Emotions are indicators. Behind a feeling is a thought, behind the thought a belief system, behind the belief a memory.

Do not generalise that everyone has blocks. Krishna was born in a prison yet had no blocks. The Paramatma is a master born with knowledge; he remained in ananda throughout. Our own awareness tells us what is needed. Looking for the root cause when there is suffering is not over-analysis; searching when there is no problem is unnecessary.

4. Spirituality is not escape — face life

Many people say “everything is fine” and run toward sannyasa. That is a defence mechanism. Spirituality and dhyana mean facing whatever challenges appear, with the Shakti that is given. The power is there so that we can resolve, not escape.

Stay open. Increase awareness. Recognise whatever is present. Do not over-analyse. When a problem is there, look for the root; when it is not, there is no need. Move toward jnana and atma-jnana.

5. The Inner Master — every person is potentially great

Every person is potentially a great master. The Inner Master within is covered by many layers. When we recognise those layers, that is enough. The feeling “everyone else meditates well, I cannot” or “I am inferior” is itself a block. It must be overcome.

Saptami is a sacred day. Sit an extra period of silent dhyana. Walking an extra mile. Experience is what matters. Theory has been spoken; now sit in stillness.

6. Devi Samarpan — continue

  1. Left-nostril inhalations only × 9.
  2. Namaskāra + bow the head toward the earth.
  3. In kumbhaka: mentally Śrīm × 9 (not aloud).
  4. Exhale with full śaraṇu — “I surrender the circling sukha-dukha vrittis, all remaining blocks, all coverings on the Inner Master, to Jaganmātā.”
  5. Up to 9 rounds. Stay open.

Homework

  • Continue Devi Samarpan morning (and optional evening).
  • Notice one sukha-dukha circle today and offer it.
  • When a feeling arises, pause and ask: “What thought is behind this? What belief? What memory?”
  • Sit an extra period of silent dhyana.
  • Recall: Sumbha-Nisumbha are the circling tendencies; stay open; the Inner Master is already within.

Remember

Saptami belongs to the final phase — purification through the sword of Knowledge. Sumbha and Nisumbha are the circling sukha and dukha vrittis rooted in ahankara. Stay open; do not assume blocks in advance. Emotions point to thoughts, beliefs and memories. Spirituality is facing life, not escaping. Every person carries an Inner Master covered by layers. Practice the extra mile of dhyana. The same Shakti that destroys the asuras within receives the offering.
