Devi Navaratri · Day 7

Mahishasura · The Six Inner Enemies · “Sarvaṃ Khalvidam Brahma”

Day 7 is the point where the whole teaching becomes sharply inward. The outer story of the goddess destroying the asuras is not just a mythic battle. It is the inner battle against the six enemies that rise within the mind: kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, mātsarya. These are not characters outside of us. They are tendencies in the inner field, and the divine power works through the seeker to cut through them.

The teaching today is the continuation of the earlier pattern: truth is not outside; truth is inside the field of awareness. The seeker must move from merely hearing the story to recognising its living meaning in the body, the mind, and the heart.

1. The deeper message of the ninth-day sequence

Dr. Newton begins by reminding everyone that the final three days of Navaratri are not merely “celebration days.” They are intensely meaningful because they carry the seed of inner purification. By this stage, the practitioner has already worked through several layers of identification: ego, self-image, desire, attachment, and the urge to possess.

Now the teaching deepens: the path is not only to worship the goddess outside, but to experience that all is Brahman. This is the crucial inner sentence: sarvaṃ khalvidam brahma — “all this indeed is Brahman.”

2. Why the story is important

The outer story is a map of the inner war

In the classical telling, the goddess defeats the asuras. In the inner reading, the asuras are the inner conditions that disturb the seeker: ignorance, arrogance, greed, possession, envy, emotional reactivity, and delusion.

The goddess does not destroy the world; she destroys the distortion that keeps the seeker from knowing the Self. The battle is not outside in a battlefield but inside in the field of the mind and the subtle body.

3. Shumbha-Nishumbha and the inner duality

One of the central symbolic pairs in the narrative is Shumbha and Nishumbha. They represent the inner polarity of pleasure and pain, desire and contraction, attraction and aversion. The seeker sees that these are not separate realities but a pair that keeps looping in the mind.

We crave pleasure. We fear pain. When pleasure comes, we cling. When pain comes, we resist. This is the rhythm of the inner struggle. This is the pair that keeps feeding the ego.

4. The six enemies inside us

Today’s teaching names the six major enemies in the mind and the subtle body. They are called ariṣaḍvarga — the sixfold set of enemies.

Enemy Meaning Inner form
Kāma desire the restless wish to possess, achieve, or have more
Krodha anger the sharp reaction that distorts perception
Lobha greed the endless hunger for more, for security, for fulfillment
Moha delusion mistaking the temporary for the real
Mada pride / intoxication self-importance, superiority, inflated identity
Mātsarya envy / spite the feeling that others have what I do not, and the inner contraction from it
These are not merely moral flaws. They are the mechanics of the mind that pull the seeker away from the Self and bind him to the cycle of craving.

5. The inner battle is not a fantasy

The battle is daily

Dr. Newton emphasizes that the deity’s battle is not symbolic only. It mirrors the ordinary life of the practitioner. Every day, the mind rises in desire, the breath tightens in anger, the ego inflates in pride, and the heart contracts in envy. This is the inner asura field.

When we recognize this, the story stops being a distant sacred narrative and becomes a living mirror. The question is no longer “Who is the enemy outside?” but “Which inner tendency is shaping my reaction right now?”

6. Mahishasura as the inner force of a limited identity

Mahishasura is not just an external demon. In the inner reading, he represents the force that rises when the ego feels it must dominate, possess, and conquer. He is the expression of primordial separation — “I am this, I own this, I must win, I must remain in control.”

This force does not disappear automatically. It must be met by awareness and transformed through grace. The goddess appears because the seeker is finally ready to confront the force of identification and not merely its effects.

7. Why “all this is Brahman” changes the whole game

The line sarvaṃ khalvidam brahma is devastating to the ego because it removes the wall between “I” and “the world.” The world is not an enemy apart from me; it is a manifestation of the same reality.

This realization changes how we see conflict. We stop seeing others as enemies to be conquered and start seeing that the same underlying truth is in all beings. The Real is one. The distortions are many. When the seeker sees this, the battle within becomes clearer and the goddess’s force begins to purify the field.

8. The six enemies in daily life

Dr. Newton gives a simple practical mapping:

  • Kāma appears as craving for attention, pleasure, recognition, or a particular result.
  • Krodha appears as irritation, sharp words, impatience, and reactivity.
  • Lobha appears as never feeling enough and always wanting more.
  • Moha appears as confusion, attachment, and mistaking emotion for truth.
  • Mada appears as egoic self-importance, comparison, and the need to feel superior.
  • Mātsarya appears as envy, resentment, and the inability to rejoice in another’s growth.

9. Additional highlights from the transcript

Dr. Newton also brings in a few additional themes that are central to the Day 7 understanding:

  • Icchā-śakti, Kriyā-śakti, Jñāna-śakti are not separate powers; they are the expression of the same divine force in the seeker. The goddess is not only worshipped as a form, but recognised as the inner power of will, action, and wisdom.
  • Shumbha and Nishumbha represent the inner pairing of pleasure and pain, desire and contraction, attraction and aversion. They keep the mind cycling until the seeker sees the deeper unity of life.
  • Ego and mamakāra are the subtle anchors of the lower self. The self-image (“I am this, I own this, I deserve this”) keeps the seeker in personal identification and emotional bondage.
  • Self-support means standing in one’s own inner clarity without depending on constant approval, possession, or external validation. The mind must learn not to live as a slave to attention and reaction.
  • Unconditional love is not sentimental softness. It means seeing the other person without possession, without expectation, and without trying to control their life. Real love gives freedom.
  • Universal oneness: “We are all one” is not a slogan; it is a recognition that from the smallest atom to the greatest field, all is one living reality. The path is to bring this understanding into daily life.
  • Zero-state is a state where the ego has become transparent enough to allow awareness to remain without needless self-contraction. In such a state, the seeker is not trying to become somebody; he is simply resting in the truth.
  • True education is not merely information. It is ātma-vidyā and brahma-vidyā — the knowledge that transforms the inner field and frees the self from identification.
  • Material and spiritual life are both necessary. The task is not to reject the world but to become balanced: material life in harmony with inner wisdom, not as a trap of greed or status.
  • Mahāśakti / Kālī / Cāmuṇḍeśvarī / Kausika Mātā / Sarasvatī are different names of the same divine energy, but the seeker must move beyond name and form into the living experience of that power.

10. The practice for Day 7

To work with this teaching, the seeker does not try to suppress the enemies by force. Instead, the practice is to observe them without identifying with them. This is the inner discipline of Navaratri:

  • sit quietly and watch the breath
  • notice the first rise of desire, anger, envy, or pride
  • do not immediately act; observe and name it
  • return to the awareness that knows the disturbance
  • offer the whole field to the Divine Mother

In this way, the mind stops becoming the battlefield itself and begins to become the witness of the battle. That is the beginning of a true inner victory.

11. The essential takeaway

Day 7 teaches that the real battle is inside. The asuras are not only ancient mythic figures; they are the hidden enemy tendencies in the mind. Kāma, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, and mātsarya are the six enemies of the inner field. When the seeker understands that all this is Brahman, the urge to conquer the world externally subsides and the inner war is gradually healed by awareness, surrender, and grace.
