Gunas for Sadhana — Proportions & Practical Examples

From the Navaratri teachings: sura lives in rhythm and alignment; asura patterning is when tamas and rajas become disproportionate. For steady sādhana we do not aim at zero tamas or zero rajas — we aim at a sattva-predominant life so the mind stays fit for dhyāna, Samarpan, and surrender to Amma.

1. Ideal proportion for a sādhaka

Guṇa Ideal share Role in practice
Sattva 60–70% (dominant) Clarity, peace, devotion, discrimination, steady practice
Rajas 20–30% (moderate) Energy to act, serve, work, and complete sādhana without laziness
Tamas 5–15% (minimum needed) Only enough for deep sleep and physical rest — not inertia
In Newton’s framing for a serious practitioner: tamas is kept very small (enough to rest), rajas modest (enough to move), and sattva carries the large share — open heart, kindness, wishing well for all.

2. Food — strongest daily lever

  • Sattva ↑ — Fresh fruits, milk, curd, rice, green vegetables, moderate ghee, soaked almonds, light khichdi, seasonal fruit.
  • Rajas moderate — Mild spice, light tea/coffee if needed, moderate salt.
  • Tamas ↓ — Heavy fried food, leftovers, excess onion–garlic, non-veg, alcohol, very spicy or stale food, late heavy dinner.

Example day: Breakfast = fruits + milk or light porridge · Lunch = rice + dal + sabzi · Dinner = light and early (before 7–7:30 pm).

3. Sleep

  • Ideal: 6–7 hours of deep sleep.
  • Too much sleep / daytime sleeping / rising after sunrise → tamas rises.
  • Sleeping very late + phone scrolling → rajas + tamas both rise.

Practical: Sleep by 10–10:30 pm · Wake by 4:30–5 am (Brahma muhūrta). This single habit raises sattva strongly.

4. Speech & media

  • Sattva — Truthful, soft, necessary, kind speech; silence when not needed.
  • Rajas — Arguments, gossip, constant talking, comparing, complaining.
  • Tamas — Harsh words, backbiting, or dull silence that avoids responsibility.

Practical: Phone on Do Not Disturb after 8 pm. Limit reels/shorts to zero or at most 10–15 minutes. Speak less; listen more. During Navaratri: social-media / reels fasting as mind-fasting.

5. Daily energy flow

  • Morning — Bath + short prayer/japa + full Devi Samarpan (9 left-nostril rounds with mental Śrīm) → sattva.
  • Day — Focused work or sevā without restlessness → balanced rajas.
  • Evening — Walk, light scripture, or silent sitting → sattva recovery.
  • Night — No heavy discussions, no news, no arguments → protect sattva before sleep.

6. When the mind shifts — what to do

Quick responses

Anger, jealousy, strong craving (rajas + tamas rising): Pause → one deep left-nostril breath → silently “Śrīm” or “I surrender this to Amma” → return to the breath.

Dullness, procrastination, “I’ll do sādhana tomorrow” (pure tamas): Immediately do 3 left-nostril rounds + one short bow. Action breaks tamas.

7. Nightly self-check (simple scorecard)

  • Did I sleep on time and wake early?
  • Did I eat light and on time?
  • Did I waste time on phone / arguments?
  • Did I do Samarpan / japa with attention?
  • Did I feel more peaceful — or more restless / dull — today?

Mostly peaceful and steady → sattva rising.
Restless and desirous → rajas high.
Lazy, heavy, unmotivated → tamas high.

8. Two non-negotiables (start here)

1. Sleep early + wake in Brahma muhūrta.
2. One full Devi Samarpan (9 rounds) every morning with mental Śrīm.

These two alone shift the proportion strongly toward sattva for the remaining Navaratri days and beyond.

Link to the teachings: On Day 3 Newton maps sura/asura to rhythm (svara / apasvara), heart and breath, and the three guṇas in proportion. Asura patterning = high tamas + high rajas inside. Cleaning the “dirty cloth” (vāsanās) becomes easier when sattva is deliberately increased through food, sleep, speech, and daily Samarpan.
