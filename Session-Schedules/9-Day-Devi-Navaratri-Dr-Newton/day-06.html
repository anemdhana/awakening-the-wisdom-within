Devi Navaratri · Day 6

Soundaryalahari · Manisha Asura · Arishadvargas · 18 Arms · Triguna · Practice Joy

Sixth day. Newton opens with Adi Shankaracharya’s Soundaryalahari: without Shakti, even Shiva cannot move. He deepens the vision of Jaganmata as the source in which the Trimurtis themselves exist, then moves into the complete sharanagati of the Navaratri sadhana shloka, Manisha Asura’s four changing forms (animal nature of the mind), the six Arishadvargas (inner enemies), the eighteen arms and the Trishula that cuts the three gunas, the dhatus (rakta, mamsa, ojas, tejas), the four states (Jagrat–Swapna–Sushupti–Turiya), antar-shuddhi through dhyana, and the constant practice of Joy. The session closes with the living example of cab-driver Anand and a participant’s realisation about arishadvargas attacking more during sadhana.

1. Soundaryalahari — Shiva is powerful only with Shakti

Adi Shankaracharya in the Soundaryalahari:

Śivaḥ śaktyā yukto yadi bhavati śaktaḥ prabhavitum
Na ced evaṃ devo na khalu kuśalaḥ spanditum api

“If Shiva is united with Shakti, he is capable of creating. If not, even he cannot stir.” Without the Mother, the Trimurtis themselves have no movement. Brahma carries a particle of her dust on his head and thereby creates; the whole Trimurti tattva exists within Jaganmata, within Parashakti.

Every puranic statement is a form of jnana. The Navaratri sadhana is the practical means to experience this Shakti directly — not merely to hear about her.

2. The Navaratri sadhana shloka of complete sharanagati

A famous verse is recalled:

Na mantraṃ no yantraṃ tadapi ca na jāne stutim aho
Na cāhvānaṃ dhyānaṃ tadapi ca na jāne stuti-kathām
…

“Mother, I know no mantra, no yantra, no tantra. I do not even know how to praise you; my mouth cannot form your name.” This is pure sharanagati. At the beginning stage we need not master every technique. The Mother, as Kundalini Mata, already lives within us and helps. We simply offer whatever we have.

3. Mahishasura’s changing forms — Manisha and the animal nature of mind

Four faces / four forms

Yesterday the arising of the Shakti with eighteen arms was recalled. Today Newton focuses on Manisha Asura (Mahishasura in his human-animal nature). He takes many forms: buffalo, man with sword, lion, elephant — four principal forms are mentioned. Each form change mirrors the mind’s processes: the mind never stays in one shape. Desire, anger, fear, pride — each gives the personality a different face.

The Devi uses her weapons against each changing form. The teaching is practical: do not be surprised when the inner enemy changes shape the moment you attack one pattern. Stay steady; the Shakti has the right weapon for each.

4. The Six Arishadvargas — the inner enemies that change form

The changing forms of Manisha are the six classical enemies of the mind — the Arishadvargas (ari = enemy, shad = six, varga = group). They are not outside; they live in the mind and keep changing shape so that we cannot pin them down. When one is attacked, another appears. This is why they attack more fiercely during sadhana: the light of dhyana makes them visible, and they resist being seen.

EnemyWhat it looks likeHow it changes formHow to meet it
Kama (desire)Wanting, craving, “I must have this”Becomes anger when denied; becomes pride when fulfilledOffer the wanting itself; do not fight the object
Krodha (anger)Heat, reaction, “How dare they”Becomes desire for revenge; becomes fear of loss of controlFeel the heat without speaking; let the wave pass
Lobha (greed)Holding, collecting, “More is safer”Becomes fear of loss; becomes pride of possessionGive one small thing deliberately; break the grip
Moha (delusion / attachment)Confusion, clinging, “This is mine / I am this”Becomes anger when the attachment is threatenedName the attachment clearly; offer the identification
Mada (pride / intoxication)“I know, I am better, I have done more”Becomes anger when contradicted; becomes fear of being ordinaryRecall one failure; sit with it without defending
Matsarya (jealousy / envy)Comparing, “Why them and not me”Becomes anger toward the other; becomes self-pityWish the other well once, sincerely; offer the comparison

These six are not separate. They feed one another. Desire denied becomes anger. Anger satisfied becomes pride. Pride threatened becomes fear, which is another form of desire. The mind’s animal nature (Manisha) is exactly this constant shape-shifting. The eighteen arms and the Trishula are the powers that meet each form as it appears.

During sadhana they often intensify. This is not failure; it is the light revealing what was already there. Meet the attack with the same ānanda. The attack itself becomes the material for growth.

5. Eighteen arms and the Trishula that removes triguna doshas

The eighteen arms are symbolic of the many powers that unite in the Devi. The central weapon is the Trishula (three-pronged spear). It removes the three guna-doshas:

  • Tamas — inertia, dullness, ignorance
  • Rajas — restlessness, craving, activity that binds
  • Sattva — even the refined attachment of “I am good / I am pure”

All three are doshas when they bind. The Mother is beyond the three gunas; she is present everywhere. The Trishula cuts the binding aspect of each.

Related to the body: the ashta / sapta dhatus (rakta, mamsa, meda, asthi, majja, shukra, and the subtle ojas and tejas) are purified as the gunas are balanced. Ojas and tejas rise when the doshas are reduced through sadhana.

6. Triputi and the four states — Jagrat, Swapna, Sushupti, Turiya

Triputi (the triad of knower–knowing–known) operates in the three states:

  • Jagrat — waking, sthula (gross) body
  • Swapna — dream, sukshma (subtle) body
  • Sushupti — deep sleep, causal
  • Turiya — the fourth, the transcendental state that witnesses the three

The energy and the “weapons” of dhyana already exist in the body. Through meditation the antar-shuddhi happens; then siddhi (capacity) and buddhi (clear intelligence) arise. Both the gross ahankara and the subtle ahankara are purified. Stages of nearness appear: Salokya (same realm), Samipya (nearness), Sarupya (same form)… culminating in the recognition that the Mother is not separate.

When Sahasrara is reached, she is experienced as the all-pervading Parashakti. In every chakra she is the same Shakti; she is the support of all.

7. Practice Joy — the constant state

From the first day the instruction has been: these nine days are for the practice of ānanda. Joy is not something that happens after the work; it is the practice itself.

“Practice Joy. Practice Joy. Everything is ānanda.” When arishadvargas attack more fiercely during sadhana (as they often do), meet them with the same ānanda. The attack itself becomes the material for growth and healing.

8. The cab-driver Anand — how the universe redesigns plans

Coordinated events for transformation

Newton shares a recent experience. On the way to an Ahimsa Dhyana Mahasabha, their car broke down (battery). Time was short. A cab was booked. The driver (named Anand) was highly stressed, speaking tensely on the phone. They began talking about meditation and the Ahimsa event. The words entered his ear. He asked questions: his brother-in-law had spoken of dhyana, but he had never paid attention. He said he would also come.

He stayed through the programme, participated in the meditation, and declared that day: “From today I am giving up meat.” He had a three-year-old child; in the meditation he saw that animals also have families. He refused other bookings and drove them home. His stress — financial worries, loans — left completely.

Meaning: We think “this is bad” when plans break. There is no absolute bad. Coordinated events rearrange the design so that a transformation can occur. The universe worked through a broken car and a stressed driver. When we stop interpreting only through the mind and stay in ātma-drishti, we see the wonder of how Shakti moves.

9. Participant sharing — Mallareddy on healing and arishadvargas

Participant (Mallareddy)

From the first day of Inner Child work a transformation has been happening. In Family Constellation I learned more. Earlier I came wanting healing in one moment; now I understand I must heal myself — and the healing is happening. Every moment I meet with joy and grow. In these nine days I have understood how the arishadvargas work, how they attack more during dhyana sadhana, and how, even in the attack, we can meet them so that growth and healing occur. Grateful.

Dr Newton

Wonderful. Meet everything with ānanda. From day one we said these nine days are the practice of joy. Practice Joy. Everything is ānanda. Thank you.

10. Devi Samarpan — continue

  1. Left-nostril inhalations only × 9.
  2. Namaskāra + bow the head toward the earth.
  3. In kumbhaka: mentally Śrīm × 9 (not aloud).
  4. Exhale with full śaraṇu — “I surrender all six Arishadvargas, all changing forms of the mind, all three guna-doshas, all remaining ahankara, to Jaganmātā.”
  5. Up to 9 rounds. Stay in the felt sense of Joy.

The same Shakti that carries the eighteen arms and the Trishula receives the offering and clears the inner Manisha.

Homework

  • Continue Devi Samarpan morning (and optional evening).
  • Notice one Arishadvarga today (name it: kama / krodha / lobha / moha / mada / matsarya) and offer it in the next round.
  • When it changes form, name the new form and offer that too.
  • When a plan “breaks,” pause and ask: “What coordinated event might Shakti be arranging?”
  • Practice Joy deliberately for one minute whenever stress rises — even one extra minute raises the tolerance level.
  • Recall: without Shakti even Shiva cannot move; with sharanagati she moves through us.

Remember

Soundaryalahari: Shiva is powerful only when united with Shakti. Jaganmata contains the Trimurtis. The sadhana shloka is pure sharanagati. Manisha’s four forms = the animal nature and changing processes of the mind. The six Arishadvargas (kama, krodha, lobha, moha, mada, matsarya) are those changing forms; they intensify during sadhana because the light reveals them. Eighteen arms and Trishula remove the binding doshas of tamas, rajas and sattva. Triputi in Jagrat–Swapna–Sushupti is witnessed in Turiya. Dhyana brings antar-shuddhi, siddhi, buddhi. Practice Joy. The broken car and Anand the driver show how the universe redesigns for transformation. Those who trigger us, and the arishadvargas that attack more in sadhana, are the very material of growth.
