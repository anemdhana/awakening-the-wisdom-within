Brahmarshi Premnath Gyan Dhara • PMC Hindi

Master Speech • Talk 2

The Secret to Conquering Your Mind Through Meditation

A precise English rendering of Brahmarshi Premnath’s full talk. The discourse centres on the single target of Oneness (Ekatva), the difference between mind and intellect, the simple method of placing the mind on the breath to become Vayu-Putra, the conquest of the mind in the present moment, the need for non-judgment, and the practical technique of meditation. Original: Gyan Dhara · PMC Hindi.

The Discourse

1. The one target – Oneness (Ekatva)

My dear Masters and my dear children, all of us have only one target, one goal – Oneness. The pyramid is the symbol of Oneness. If we have not attained the state of Oneness, we have to take birth again.

There are five kingdoms: the mineral kingdom, the plant kingdom, the animal kingdom, the human kingdom and the divine kingdom. When all five come together, that is Earth. This Earth is the meeting place of the five kingdoms. They are not separate.

The mineral, plant and animal kingdoms have no intellect; they have mind. Human beings have intellect. Yet man, having become intellect-less, has turned into a demon – committing violence against living beings and destroying nature. Why do we have intellect? Intellect is meant to be used. This knowledge of intellect we have forgotten; the whole world has forgotten. That is why our Guru, Brahma Pitamaha Subhash Patriji, took birth on this Earth.

2. Mind and Intellect – two different powers

We had forgotten that we possess intellect. The knowledge of countless births is hidden inside the soul, yet we had forgotten and were living as intellect-less beings. Now we have come to know: body, mind, intellect and soul – everything.

Mind is connected with the body. Intellect is connected with the soul. Mind works with human energy; intellect works with divine energy. Mind works through the five senses; intellect works through the sixth sense.

When will the work of the mind stop and the work of the intellect begin? When the five senses stop, the sixth sense begins to function. Therefore, to stop the mind, what must we do? First we must know what the mind is. Mind means thought and imagination. Thought means the wandering mind. This mind is not with us; it keeps roaming in the past and in the future. Roaming like this, the mind has become a monkey.

What must we make of this monkey-mind? Keep the mind on the breath and turn it into Vayu-Putra. Make the monkey into Vayu-Putra. That is why our Guru has said: from Dhyan comes Gyan, from Gyan comes Mukti.

3. The inner journey and becoming Vayu-Putra

When will the mind stop? When we undertake the inner journey – antar-prayana. Then we will come to know how much knowledge is hidden inside us.

I myself did not know. I was a great ignoramus. I did not know how to think or how to speak. Only after coming into meditation did I come to know. This very knowing is knowledge; there is nothing else.

When we become Vayu-Putra the mind stops, the brahmarandhra opens, we become filled with energy and we become powerful like Hanuman. We have to become Hanuman – powerful. For that, energy is needed – a great deal of energy. Yet we do not know how easy the method of obtaining energy is. It is very simple.

Sit in any comfortable asana – whatever posture is comfortable. Keep the mind on the breath. Become Vayu-Putra. Stop the mind. You will be filled with energy. Energy is needed so that no bacterium or virus can attack us; so that we remain healthy. What is difficult? For meditation only time is needed – nothing else. Time itself is sadhana.

4. Time is the only requirement – every present moment is divine

How much time are we wasting? Why waste time uselessly? Anything that is lost can be regained – money can be regained, another marriage can be done, sons can be born again. But time will never return. Every present moment is a divine moment. Our mind should not wander even for one second into the past or the future; it should remain with us.

In Badami, a village in Karnataka, there is a hill that has been made into an artificial cave. Inside the cave stands a statue of Vardhaman Mahavir. In Telugu we call him Vartaman Mahavir; in Hindi, Vardhaman Mahavir. Looking at that statue a question arose in me: “Your name is Vardhaman – how did you become Mahavir?” Eyes may remain open or closed – it does not matter. I did not allow my mind even for one moment to go into the past or the future. I won the victory of remaining in the present. I conquered the mind. That is how I became Vartaman Mahavir. We too must become the same.

We have to conquer the mind. Alexander conquered the whole world yet could not return. The victory we need is the victory over the mind. What is required for the victory of the mind? Only time.

5. What each part of us truly needs

I myself was caught in maya. Whatever the mind desires, we give it. Do we know what the intellect wants? The body needs health, the mind needs peace, the intellect needs knowledge, the soul needs energy. All of them together need liberation and happiness.

I used to drown completely in meditation and forget everything – remaining in Brahmananda. Then the mind would remember: “It is time for the serial – open your eyes.” That is the work of the mind. The mind’s job is to remind the body of its needs. Listening to the mind we open our eyes. Why open the eyes? To watch the serial, to drink tea, to eat food. We can sit for three or four hours watching a list of programmes, yet for meditation we say “one day is enough” or “one sitting is enough.” Meditation is joyous. Why not do more? Why not increase the time? The play of the mind continues. The intellect does not speak. What does the intellect want? The knowledge of countless births that is obtained only through meditation.

6. No judgment – every soul is God

Those who have become Gods have no enemies. Ram and Krishna had no enmity with Duryodhana. When Duryodhana died, Krishna felt no joy. When Abhimanyu died, Krishna felt no sorrow. Krishna knew: the soul of Duryodhana is also God; the soul of Abhimanyu is also God. Every soul is God. The soul that dwells in every living being is God. Therefore we have to become God.

Let anyone remain however they are. We must not judge. “Why is he like this? Why does he not listen to me? He will not become a vegetarian, he will not meditate…” Just tell them. Whether they change or not is not our work. I have six brothers; none of them meditate. I must not consider them bad. Their time has not yet come; the merit of previous births is not yet sufficient. To even hear about meditation requires the accumulated merit of many births. It is not easy.

In a building of 32 apartments, only one apartment knows anything about me; the rest of the world may think those people are bad. They are not bad – the condition of those souls is still low from previous births. Time will come. From 1994 onwards for twenty-five years the Colonel used to come to our village. In 1997 I met Sir. My time had come. Sir used to come and go; I did not even know his name. Someone took me to him with a small plate. Whoever has given that plate – the one who listens is a Pandava; the one who does not listen is a Kaurava. The one who speaks is Krishna Bhagwan. Krishna spoke to Duryodhana also; Duryodhana and Dhritarashtra did not listen. He spoke to the Pandavas and the Pandavas listened. Therefore our dharma is to speak. The mouth is for speaking, the ears are for listening, the nose is for meditation, the feet are for prachar. This body is a chariot.

7. The body is a temple and a chariot

Dear friends, this body is a temple – for meditation, for self-study (swadhyaya), for the company of the wise (sajjan sangat) so that knowledge may be obtained. This body is a chariot for prachar. We have to use this body. That is why we have taken birth. Birth means the entry of the soul into the body. Therefore we have taken birth – to increase sadhana, to increase energy, to obtain knowledge, to become God, and to see God in every living being.

When we see God in every living being, judgment, complaint, interference and violence – all of them disappear. In our family – no judgment. In the pyramid society – no judgment. Outside – no judgment. Everyone is God. We will love God; we will not judge. That is Oneness.

8. Practical method of meditation

We will do ten minutes of meditation. I will first explain the method; do not close the eyes yet. Close the eyes only when I say so. Join both index fingers, place the hands in the lap, cross both legs below. First listen to me; afterwards we will meditate.

After closing the eyes: for one minute forget the body, forget life, forget the family, forget yourself. We are not the body; we are consciousness. Not merely the consciousness dwelling in this body, but the consciousness that fills every living being in the entire universe. The feeling with which we meditate determines the energy level at which we remain. Not the feeling of a human being, not the feeling of the body – only consciousness. Forget everything. You have come into meditation today because you were a sadhaka in previous births. No thought will come. There is no thought; the mind is peaceful. Remain like that.

Second: after closing the eyes many sensations may arise – pain, irritation, heat, cold, a feeling of rotation, the head may droop, the body may lean. A great deal happens. These are the karmas of many births coming out through ekagrata-upasana. Let them happen. Whatever happens is the same outgoing karma.

Third: Vipassana. Ordinary seeing with the eyes is of the outer world. After closing the eyes, seeing the inner world with the divine eye is Vipassana. Darkness, light, colours, stars, sun, moon, some God, some rishi – something may be seen. If the physical eyes open, nothing is seen. Even darkness is a black colour. From where is it being seen? It is the vision of the divine eye. Why keep the mind on the breath? So that thought stops and the vision of the divine eye can occur. In previous births you have done much sadhana; therefore the vision of the divine eye is happening directly. The Himalayas may appear, Mahavatar Babaji may appear. If Babaji appears, do not keep looking; bring the mind back to the breath. Or, if no thought is arising, simply keep watching whatever is appearing. Only when thought comes, place the mind again on the breath.

If there is no thought, everyone will simply sit. Whatever appears, keep watching. When does thought come? On the natural breath – the gentle, flowing breath. Is the breath flowing by itself right now? It is flowing by itself. We never say “I am pulling it.” This is not effort; this is not exercise. Simply notice the touch of the breath going in from the tip of the nose to the beginning of the nose, and the touch of the breath coming out from the beginning to the tip. No mantra to be chanted, no imagination to be done. If a finger is placed near the nostrils, the outgoing breath feels warm, the incoming breath feels cool – that is another way of knowing the touch. Remain only with the natural breath.

Summary

Core teaching
• The single goal is Oneness (Ekatva); the pyramid is its symbol.
• Five kingdoms (mineral, plant, animal, human, divine) together form the Earth.
• Mind works with the five senses and human energy; intellect works with the sixth sense and divine energy.
• The monkey-mind is conquered by placing it on the breath → one becomes Vayu-Putra (like Hanuman).
• From Dhyan comes Gyan; from Gyan comes Mukti.
• Only time is required for meditation; time lost never returns. Every present moment is divine.
• Vardhaman Mahavir became “Vartaman Mahavir” by keeping the mind in the present.
• Body needs health, mind needs peace, intellect needs knowledge, soul needs energy – all need liberation and joy.
• Do not judge anyone; every soul is God. Speak (share), but leave the result.
• The body is a temple for dhyan, swadhyaya and satsang, and a chariot for prachar.

Meditation method
Sit comfortably → join fingers, hands in lap, legs crossed → close eyes → forget body, family, self → remain as pure consciousness filling the universe → allow all sensations (outgoing karma) → keep attention on the natural touch of the breath at the nostrils → if visions appear, stay with them; if thoughts arise, return to the breath. No mantra, no imagination.
