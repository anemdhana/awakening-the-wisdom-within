Bhagavad Gita · COVID-19 Wisdom

The Song Eternal in the Time of COVID-19

A complete restructuring of the live Sunday session by QLU Trainer Geeta K. Every spoken idea is preserved, organised into clear sections, with shlokas highlighted and practical applications added for daily life. The virus is not treated as an enemy but as a teacher that arrived exactly when humanity needed to shift.

1. Opening – Welcome & Context

Namaste and the unprecedented situation

Good evening, dear friends. Namaste and a very, very warm welcome to the Sunday session. This evening we will be spending some time together reflecting on the relevance of the Bhagavad Gita and the wisdom that it offers in the current COVID-19 scenario.

I am very thankful to Quantum Life University and Masters Dr. Newton and Dr. Lakshmi for having given me this opportunity to interact with everyone on this platform today.

COVID-19 is sweeping the globe, leaving a trail of damage in its wake. There is so much social damage, economic damage, and of course tremendous emotional trauma. COVID-19 has presented mankind with an unprecedented situation. So how equipped are we to handle this?

For the past few months we have been hearing so much. We are flooded and bombarded with news of COVID-19 from all angles, from everywhere across the globe – our own country, other countries. Every day we are bombarded with statistics, with so many stories that it is very easy to sink into despair and a feeling of hopelessness. Most of us go around thinking: “When are we going to come out of this? Or are we going to come out of it at all? Is there going to be light at the end of the tunnel?”

So how do we connect to the Bhagavad Gita in this scenario? This is what we will be doing today in this evening session. And I thank all of you who are joining us for this session today.

2. Not a Battle Against the Virus – The Virus as Teacher

Pause and rethink the “war” language

While it would be very, very easy to draw a parallel with Kurukshetra and say that mankind is fighting an unseen enemy – the coronavirus – we see that being frequently said around as we see it in the news: terms like “battle against COVID” or “the fight against COVID”.

This is where we need to take a pause and rethink. It is not a battle against the virus – not at all. The virus is not the enemy. It has appeared at the right time as a philosopher’s guide and teacher to us, when humanity needed to make that shift. So we need to accept it for what it is.

The Song Eternal – the Bhagavad Gita – is a treatise of universal significance, as we all know, and it is considered a panacea for all ills: physical, mental, emotional and spiritual. After all, we exist on this earth plane and our goal is happiness – liberating ourselves from suffering. That is the bottom line. And we aim for physical well-being, mental well-being, emotional stability and spiritual peace. This is what we aim for in life. This is what a fulfilling life should be made of.

And the battlefield of Kurukshetra is often taken as a metaphor for the canvas of the sum of life experiences. We know that Lord Krishna was not an advocate of war. War just happens to be a setting or the scenario in which the teacher – the Jagadguru – uses to inculcate the spirit with which all work on this earth plane has to be carried out.

So the coronavirus, therefore, in this time, in this moment, is our teacher who is delivering valuable lessons for us to integrate.

3. Arjuna’s Despondency – Mirror of Our Present State

The situation today is much like Arjuna at the beginning of the war

A situation today, if we look at it, is much like Arjuna’s at the beginning of the war. In the first chapter (Arjuna Vishada Yoga) we find Arjuna is despondent, is engulfed in sorrow, is overwhelmed. His fundamental response was intensely emotional.

Come to think of it – are we not going through something similar today? The mind of Arjuna, who was otherwise a very courageous, great warrior – he was a hero – but in that context his mind was clouded, his convictions were unsettled, and he was in a state of complete confusion and despair. So we are also in a very similar situation today.

Look around us. COVID has killed thousands across the world. All of a sudden it has rendered totally meaningless many of the things which we as a society invested in – many of our goals, burning desires, pursuits and pleasures which had so much meaning a few months ago – they no longer seem relevant or important. A huge shift has taken place in the way we view life now.

And this pandemic no doubt is taking a toll on humanity physically, mentally and emotionally. Doctors and healthcare workers are under intense physical strain and emotional strain. Many are gripped by fear and anxiety. People are losing loved ones suddenly. There is the trauma of family members’ inability to even hold a decent farewell to their loved ones. There is the trauma of isolation, the trauma of quarantine, the trauma of doctors watching patients succumbing to the virus while they stand by and watch helplessly. There is the trauma of stigma attached to a patient who has tested positive.

All of this can bring about a lot of fear, worry, anxiety – because we are dealing with the unknown. Every day something new is being discovered about the virus. There is a lot of rumour floating around. There is a lot of news regarding the virus, but we really don’t know. We are fed with horror stories that provoke anger and hopelessness at the same time. And there is widespread confusion and a sense of helplessness and despair.

Arjuna’s words on the battlefield

Arjuna: Seeing my own kinsmen all arrayed for battle, my limbs are giving way, I am shaking and trembling, my mouth is drying up. What do I do? I am in despair.

At one point he actually throws down his Gandiva, puts it on the ground and just collapses.

Krishna: My dear Arjuna, from where has this faint-heartedness come to you? How has this delusion overcome you in this hour where your courage is needed the most? This kind of weakness is not befitting an honourable person. It will not lead you to higher abodes; it will actually lead you to disgrace. So arise, awake, Arjuna. This is not how you should be behaving.

This is what Arjuna hears from his mentor Krishna on the battlefield. Throughout the Gita we see that Krishna’s advice to Arjuna is to uphold his Dharma – the Dharma of a Kshatriya warrior – and to uphold it selflessly amidst the chaos, not becoming paralysed by fear or despair or even anxiety, fear of the outcome of the war.

4. What is Dharma? Swadharma in the Present Hour

Right thought and action – do what is needed without attachment

What is Dharma? Dharma is the philosophy of right thought and action governing the order of things. So the most fulfilling life is one aligned with one’s own Swadharma, one’s own karma.

From that perspective, what matters most for all of us now – wherever we are, whatever we are, whatever the role we are playing in life right now – is to be able to do what is needed without succumbing either to fear or to despair or to anxiety or weakness caused by any of these, and without being attached to the outcome.

Doctors and healthcare workers need to discharge their duties selflessly and courageously without being attached to or shaken by the outcomes – not an easy thing in this scenario. Renouncing the fruits of action, they must remain steadfast. Seeing death all around them, they must not lose composure amidst news of death rates and mortality rates and ticking counters that keep reminding us of figures and statistics.

Practical example: A doctor on night shift in a COVID ward can silently repeat – “I perform my duty to the best of my ability; the result is not mine to carry.” This single inner statement reduces the emotional load while keeping professional excellence intact.

5. The Eternal Soul – There is No Real Death

Krishna’s direct teaching on birth and death

What can we draw solace from? What are the Master’s words in such a scenario? We are dealing with death on a daily basis. There is news of death sometimes in the neighbourhood, sometimes the very next door, sometimes about other countries. And there is always comparison – the US is doing badly, Italy is doing badly, now India is third, India is fourth, Brazil is doing badly… People are dying. Horror stories. And this can have a tremendous impact on us.

When we succumb to fear, automatically our immune system gets suppressed. So what does the Lord say? What does Krishna say?

na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin
nāyaṃ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ
ajo nityaḥ śāśvato ’yaṃ purāṇo
na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre The soul is never born and never dies. It is unborn, eternal, everlasting and primeval. It is not slain when the body is slain. (Bhagavad Gita 2.20)

The soul has no birth and no death. The Self is unborn, eternal, from evil and knows no death. The soul is not slain when the body is slain. And we can draw immense solace. Maybe we have lost someone dear to us, maybe someone whom we know, an acquaintance, or maybe it is just the news of so many deaths and we feel “Why our innocence? What is happening? Why are we becoming victims to this virus?” There is a reason.

So the Dharma of every individual in this COVID scenario is to do one’s best in that particular hour. You may be a doctor, you may be a healthcare worker, you may just be a common citizen, you may be in the police, you may be a frontline worker – we call them COVID warriors today. Yes – it is to follow. We follow government directives. Our Dharma is to look after ourselves and our family members to the best of our ability, follow practices, follow all that has been laid down – the rules that have been laid: social distancing, simple things, hygiene, wearing masks, helping others wherever possible – paropakāram wherever possible. Let us reach out, let us help, let us help each other, let us help family members. Let us not get caught up in turmoil.

All said and done, we must embrace the fragility and vulnerability of life and respect the fact that neither life nor death are in our complete control. We have to act in the world as it is now, accepting all that is. That is very important – accepting things as they are and at the same time doing our best to improve things.

6. Equanimity – Sthita-prajña in the Face of Adversity

Samatvam – the word of the Gita

What is called upon here is equanimity in the face of adversity. And in the Gita it is the word samatvam – equanimity. We must not turn away in disgust or despair even when we look at the worst that life can do to us. Even when we are plunged in every kind of loss, of bereavement or humiliation, we must not let go of equanimity. For this one has to be a sthita-prajña – one who resides in stabilised consciousness.

In the second chapter Arjuna asks Krishna: What is the definition of a sthita-prajña? How does he walk? How does he talk? How does he conduct himself?

Krishna: O Pārtha, it is he whose mind is untroubled in the midst of sorrows, free from desire amid pleasures; he who has given up passion, fear and anger – he is called a sthita-prajña. He who is able to withdraw his senses from sense objects like a tortoise which withdraws its limbs into its shell.

At this point let us remember that much of the misery that we have brought upon ourselves today and also to Mother Earth is because of the lack of self-control. There is so much lust, greed, materialism – all of it has contributed to the plight in which we find ourselves today. That is what we need to understand. We need to understand that at this hour what is called for from us is to follow our Swadharma, do the best we can.

svadharme nidhanaṃ śreyaḥ
para-dharmo bhayāvahaḥ It is preferable to die in the discharge of one’s own duty than to follow another’s path, which is fraught with fear. (Bhagavad Gita 3.35)

For Arjuna, Krishna says: Considering your duty as a warrior, don’t waver. For a warrior there is no better engagement than fighting for the upholding of righteousness – which is what Arjuna was called upon to do. So Swadharma is what the Gita talks to us about.

If you are a doctor – we are so thankful and grateful to the number of doctors today who are standing there. They are waging a battle. They are also waging a battle with their own fears. Some of them are away from their families for days together, and many of them have lost their lives. And today we salute them. We thank them from the bottom of our hearts. We express gratitude to all those frontline workers who are standing there fighting for us to keep us safe. That is important because they are following their Swadharma.

7. Austerity of Speech & Mind – Spiritual Hygiene

Physical hygiene is given; spiritual hygiene is equally urgent

We know that stress during these times is inevitable – the stress created by misinformation and fear-mongering, the stress created by the actual situation we are in. There is lack of effective containment strategy, there is a lot of politicisation of issues, there is lack of a vaccine, there is lack of a known cure. There are unethical practices which are being spurred by greed and opportunistic practices going on even in this situation. People want to make money – maybe they are doing it out of fear, maybe out of a fear of survival. There are people losing their livelihoods, people have lost homes and of course loved ones.

So what can we do? What should one’s mindset be in this hour? What should we practice at this time? We must practice austerity of speech and austerity of mind.

There is so much every day we are also hearing a lot about fake news. Every day you open WhatsApp – in fact it is better to be away from a lot of social media unless it is doing something useful for you – because you are truly bombarded by a lot of things, most of which you don’t know whether it is true or not. So every day we see the papers and they keep warning us: please do not fall victims to fake news, do not fall victim to rumours. There is a lot of rumour being spread. We do not know. We are at a stage where we do not know what is true and what is untrue.

We cannot control certain things which are beyond us. We cannot control others. But we can exercise a lot of self-control. And the Gita talks about this. So let us practice austerity of speech and austerity of thought. Therefore it is as important to practice spiritual hygiene as it is to practice physical hygiene. Physical hygiene is a given now. Over the past few months we have all got used to practicing intense physical hygiene to keep ourselves and those around us safe. But what about spiritual hygiene, psychic hygiene, mental hygiene, emotional hygiene?

anudvegakaraṃ vākyaṃ satyaṃ priya-hitaṃ ca yat
svādhyāyābhyasanaṃ caiva vāṅ-mayaṃ tapa ucyate Words that do not agitate others, that are truthful, pleasing and beneficial, and the regular practice of the study of the scriptures – these are declared to be the austerity of speech. (Bhagavad Gita 17.15)

Who is one who practices austerity of speech? The one by whom others are not agitated and one who is not agitated by others; one who is free from joy and fear and anxiety – such a person is dear to Me, says Krishna. Austerity of mind lies in simplicity, self-control and purity of thought. Use words in control. Do not agitate others with your words and stick to the truth and be simple. That is very important.

Practical example – digital austerity: Before forwarding any message, pause and ask: (1) Is it true? (2) Is it necessary? (3) Is it kind? If any answer is “no”, delete. This single filter cuts the majority of fear-spreading content.

The mind – friend or enemy

And then Arjuna says: Krishna, but the mind is very turbulent. It is strong, it is obstinate, and now it appears to me that it is more difficult to control than even the wind. What do I do? It is as if I am swept away by my thoughts.

uddhared ātmanātmānaṃ nātmānam avasādayet
ātmaiva hy ātmano bandhur ātmaiva ripur ātmanaḥ One must elevate, not degrade, oneself by one’s own mind. The mind is the friend of the conditioned soul, and his enemy as well. (Bhagavad Gita 6.5)

Krishna says: Use your mind to liberate yourself with the help of your own mind. The mind can be your greatest enemy but it can also be your greatest friend. So train yourself. Train yourself to use your mind to lift yourself, not to sink yourself into some kind of abyss of despair, abyss of hopelessness – because the mind is like a wild untamed horse. It will run here and there and it will lead you along with it. And once you get entangled in all the drama of the mind, then you will not be able to escape.

And therefore we must act with conviction and act without fear. And in this hour of need we must set high standards. This is what our doctors are doing, this is what our policemen are doing, this is what people at the borders – our soldiers – are doing, this is what all the COVID warriors are doing. And no one is big or small or great or less great. Each one of us – you may be just sitting in your home, you may not be a doctor, you may not be a healthcare worker, you may not be a policeman – but you are doing your best by contributing to the safety of your society by following rules, staying at home, not troubling others. Are you not?

You have to act with conviction. Wherever possible lend a helping hand, say a kind word. If you know somebody who is affected, please lend a helping hand. You can say a word of encouragement. We know today that many, many, many people are suffering from the stigma attached to COVID-19. And what is killing people is more the fear than the virus itself. In fact the numbers say something very different. There are a lot of people dying of dengue, there are people dying of malaria – but we have created a monster out of COVID. Is that actually necessary? No, it is not. And that is why austerity of speech and austerity of thought is very, very important in these times.

8. Death is Only a Change of Garment

There is no “death rate” for the soul

Therefore Krishna advises Arjuna and he says: Whatever is done without faith – whether it is sacrifice, it may be your Swadharma, it may be austerity, any other act – it is useless. So he says aśraddhayā – don’t be careless or callous in your approach to life. Act with conviction, act with courage, and you know that you are right. You may die in the process, you may lose your life, but it’s okay – because you are not really dying.

Now if we look at it, there is so much talk about mortality rate, there is so much talk about death rate, and there is this counter always ticking. If you just browse news channels – which I have stopped doing for quite a number of days now – you will see that there is this counter ticking at 3 o’clock and then at 5 o’clock and at 9 o’clock… So it’s very important. There is a lot of statistics about death being given out to you. But if you are secure in the knowledge that death is not there at all, then there is no death rate really. That is what Krishna seeks to tell you. There is no death rate. The soul is unborn, it is eternal.

vāsāṃsi jīrṇāni yathā vihāya
navāni gṛhṇāti naro ’parāṇi
tathā śarīrāṇi vihāya jīrṇāny
anyāni saṃyāti navāni dehī As a person puts on new garments, giving up old ones, the soul similarly accepts new material bodies, giving up the old and useless ones. (Bhagavad Gita 2.22)

All you are doing – all your soul is doing – is changing the garment, the garment of the body. That is all your soul is doing. So does COVID-19 really bring death? Not at all. Maybe it is bringing you a new life. That is what it is doing. After all, when you die all you are doing is shedding your old garment to don a new garment. That is all you are doing. Fear not. Don’t fear death and don’t talk about it all the time, creating monsters in the mind. We do not need to deal with those demons. All you need to be is a sthita-prajña – in stabilised consciousness. That is true awakening.

Once you are awakened to that knowledge you are automatically a self-realised person. You do not have to don saffron robes. You do not have to run away to some Himalayas. You do not have to shut yourself away. No. You can be a householder, you can be in the thick of work, you can be whatever you are doing – you may be a professional, you may be a banker, you may be a student. All of life has come to a standstill today. We underwent lockdowns. We went through lockdowns and somewhere we also became locked down. We locked ourselves to knowledge and we opened ourselves to fear – which is not necessary.

Krishna: Please, Arjuna, awake, arise. Sever the ignorance and doubt in your heart with the sword of self-knowledge. Use the sword of self-knowledge and cut away all that ignorance and doubt that is arising – because you know the mind: it’s like bubbles rising every minute, every second, with every breath. The mind brings up so much of turbulence and turmoil that you feel that you are unable to handle it. At some point it overwhelms you.

Change is inevitable. One must learn from tough times and we must learn to embrace change.

9. Lifestyle of Moderation – Yuktāhāra-vihārasya

The middle path the Gita and the Buddha both advocate

What about the lifestyle that we should adopt? Are there any guidelines for the lifestyle that we should adopt? Yes. Today there is a lot of talk about hygiene – and we’ve already spoken about it – and the Gita in Chapter 13 talks about śaucam or cleanliness, and it refers to both internal and external cleanliness. It’s very, very important. In fact the lack of cleanliness is said to be the quality of one who is of demoniac nature. Krishna talks about the divine nature and the demoniac nature, and the demoniac nature is in direct opposition to the divine nature. And we all know that we are the divine in mortal form. That is all we are. We are all interconnected and the Divine resides in every one of us – ahaṃ brahmāsmi. We are all one. And every little thing that we do creates ripples and goes across and affects the whole globe, all of humanity. So let us not consider ourselves petty or trivial and say “Oh after all I’m just this… I’m just sitting at home… I’m just practicing a little reflection… I’m not doing anything much.” No. Every little thing that you do has a tremendous effect on all of humanity. And in fact what we have collectively done is what has brought us to this juncture today. Let us remember that.

So what does the Gita tell us about lifestyles? Krishna advocates for us the middle path. The middle path is very important. Moderation is important – because somewhere deep inside let us acknowledge that it is extravagance – extravagance in following the demands of the body and the mind – that has led us to this. We have brought this upon ourselves. We may admit it or we may choose not to admit it. We may choose to have a very fatalistic approach and we may say that “Oh we are very unlucky to be living in such times and this dreaded outbreak has killed our lives and it has brought so much misery to us.” But let us delve deep. This is a time for introspection. Let us use this time. Let us not say we are stuck at home. No, no – we are not stuck. Not at all. We’ve been given this break by Mother Nature. We’ve been given a beautiful break and this break has been given to us for us to spend in reflection, in contemplation, and to use it to uplift ourselves. That is what we need to do.

And therefore, like the Buddha said – the Buddha advocated the middle path. He did not tell any of us “Go and sit for six years under a tree, go meditate.” No. He said: See good, do good, be good. Do the best you can. That is what he advocated. And Krishna says the same.

yuktāhāra-vihārasya yukta-ceṣṭasya karmasu
yukta-svapnāvabodhasya yogo bhavati duḥkha-hā For one who is moderate in eating and recreation, moderate in effort in actions, and regulated in sleep and wakefulness – yoga becomes the destroyer of all misery. (Bhagavad Gita 6.17)

What is yoga that can take away this misery from you and make your life truly fulfilling? Moderation in everything – moderation in eating, moderation in recreation, moderation in sleep in the sleep state and the waking state. That is true yoga which will deliver you from misery of all kind.

Now if you were to just reflect on this – these two lines – we will realise and we will admit to ourselves that this is not what we have been doing. Yes of course many of us are practicing to the best of our ability, but as a collective if we see – with globalisation and with all the timelines going haywire and with so much travel and with this tremendous speed at which we are progressing – we call it development and we call it progress, but in some areas we have actually regressed. We have totally done away with nature and her cycles. We have broken circadian rhythms. And you know we don’t respect our bodies as much anymore because we have broken timelines. We spend late nights, we wake up when we want, and we eat what we want. And there is this very famous term and it became very famous especially in the lockdown – and you know there are advertisements coming – “Why don’t you binge-watch the serial?” It’s called binge-watching, binge-eating. These are the terms, the new-generation terms.

Now if we were to compare those terms with this shloka – yuktāhāra-vihārasya – that which is in moderation: your efforts in moderation, your eating in moderation, the food that you consume, moderation in sleep. As we know, Arjuna was called Gudakesha – the one who has conquered sleep. We may not be referring to physical sleep here. He had conquered the slumber – the slumber of ignorance. That is why he was called throughout the Gita – Krishna addresses Arjuna as Gudakesha in many, many places – the one who has conquered sleep, the sleep brought about by ignorance and darkness. And so we have the Guru – the dispeller of darkness – the one who guides us into awakening, into inner awakening.

Practical example – daily rhythm reset: Fix one non-negotiable: dinner by 7:30 pm, lights out by 10:30 pm, and 20 minutes of quiet breath observation before sleep. Within two weeks most people report clearer mornings and lower anxiety – living proof of yukta-svapnāvabodha.

10. COVID as a Gift – Lessons in Simple Living & Contentment

Grateful for the teacher that forced us to stop

And therefore I feel personally that we must be extremely grateful – extremely grateful – for our friend coronavirus who has done humanity a beautiful favour by appearing at this time. No doubt, no doubt there are losses and no doubt there has been tremendous pain and anguish and despair. And of course we empathise. We empathise with all those who lost people who were dear and near to them out of the blue – suddenly they were not even mentally prepared for it. We empathise with those in hospitals today. We empathise with those who are struggling with fear today. We empathise. But we also pray for them and send love and light to them and wish for awakening to happen to them. That is very important.

So going back to moderation – to the lifestyle that the Gita prescribes – the Gita prescribes a lifestyle of moderation, simple living, healthful eating and looking within for peace. That is important. You see what has happened. The progression of COVID-19 – it has humbled us into some kind of submission, isn’t it? It is almost as if sudden brakes have been applied to our materialistic spree and the mad, mad rat race. We were living in a very frenzied way, as if you know we were running a race everywhere. Students, little ones – even there was this huge competitiveness and living as if you know we had to consume and consume this consumerist tendency and we had to compete. Egos were inflated. So much was happening. There was so much lust, so much greed, so much selfishness. Everybody was trying to be opportunistic and making hay while the sun shines. And unfortunately it’s happening even now. But then we cannot do – we have to accept things. A lot of things we have to accept and move on. And wherever we can, we make those changes. First we make those internal changes and then those internal changes translate into changes in our environment. So we be the change that we want to see outside of us.

So now what is happening? What has happened? How has COVID-19 actually humbled us? Families are now spending more time together. We are under the roof, we are with each other, we get to interact more. We are eating home-cooked food – we’ve been forced to – and we must enjoy it. There is nothing like eating food that is cooked with love, eating together, praying together, maybe meditating together. And people are just buying just what they need. So today wherever you see you see this word “essentials”. Just a few months ago – maybe towards the end of last year if you were to look at November-December 2019 or even early 2020 when we had just welcomed the new year – we were not talking about essentials. Today we are talking about essential supplies, essential groceries, and we are talking of essential amenities. And the focus has shifted away from luxuries. Today life has become a luxury. So there is a lot of focus on simple living. And this was always what was prescribed – do not go into extravagance, do not go into that mode. Just look within, introspect.

Nature today is undoing the damage which we have done – which merciless mankind has done to her – and we have no choice but to submit humbly to Mother Nature. She knows what to do and she has done it in time for her to recuperate, for her to come out of all the torture that we have inflicted upon her. And how have we inflicted this? What have we done? We have misused Mother Nature, we have misused our bodies, we have misused the resources that she has offered, and we have gone about life with a lot of pride, conceit, harshness, ignorance. And who do these qualities belong to? These are qualities which belong to those who are of demoniac nature according to the Gita.

11. Steadfast in Duty – Equanimity Again

Abandon attachment to success and failure

So what is important for us is to realise where we are right now, where we stand right now. It is very important for us. And the description – Krishna advised Arjuna when Arjuna is overwhelmed by the kind of despair with which we are overwhelmed today – he says: Be steadfast in the performance of your duty, O Pārtha, abandoning attachment to success and failure. Just be unwavering when faced with this situation. Do not waver. That is very important. Equanimity in the face of adversity. Lead your lifestyle knowing that there is adversity. We are faced with it. We are facing it. It’s staring us in the face. So what can we do? Do we choose to still sink or do we choose to stay afloat? How do we stay afloat? By practicing equanimity, equilibrium – samatvaṃ yoga ucyate.

yoga-sthaḥ kuru karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā dhanañjaya
siddhy-asiddhyoḥ samo bhūtvā samatvaṃ yoga ucyate Perform your duty equipoised, O Arjuna, abandoning all attachment to success or failure. Such equanimity is called yoga. (Bhagavad Gita 2.48)

The Lord tells Arjuna that Arjuna – what you need now in this hour of grief – for you it is an hour of grief. He is going to see his own kinsmen, his own relatives, his uncles, his grandsire, his own cousins – all of them being destroyed in the war, all of them losing their lives. There are going to be millions of deaths. They say it was about 1.6 million if we were to talk in today’s terms – that was the loss of life that happened during the Kurukshetra war. And just like what Ashoka faced at the end of the Kalinga war which prompted him to take up Buddhism – Ashoka was appalled at what he saw: the bodies lying one on each other, blood all around – and that was the moment when the shift happened in Ashoka and he chose to give up being a Kshatriya and he said “I want to adopt the path of the Buddha.” That is what happened with Ashoka. But here Arjuna is called upon by the Lord to perform his duty, his righteous duty. And that is why Krishna says: Be a sthita-prajña. Move around like a sthita-prajña. Do your duty. Stay unwavering. Stay in equilibrium. Practice samatva and just go ahead. And if in the discharge of your duty you are to lose your life then it’s okay – because you are not really losing your life. You never do – because you were never born and you never die.

ahaṃ ādiś ca madhyaṃ ca bhūtānām anta eva ca I am the beginning, the middle and the end of all beings. (Bhagavad Gita 10.20)

These words give us immense consolation in this time of stress, in this time of fear, in this time of anxiety. Indeed they do. And the moment we are attacked by fear and anxiety we know that the stress response is immunosuppressive. We can do a lot of things – you know we can build up immunity today. A lot of stuff is doing the rounds: drink this, do that, have this juice, have that medicine… Yes indeed a lot of things people are resorting to because it’s a desperate measure to keep yourself away from the virus. Many of them of course may be effective. But what is most effective? What keeps your immune system really strong? What keeps your vibrations harmonious? What is it that keeps you strong? It is the lack of stress. When you are in the stress mode immediately your immune system is compromised. We need to understand this. Immune system becomes depressed and compromised when we are victims of stress, of fear, of anxiety. We are severely compromised and we cannot afford for our immune systems to get compromised. So if we really want a vibrant immune system, if we want to stay immune, we want to stay healthy – then we need to have a healthy mind in a healthy body. And we need to practice, we need to train our minds for this.

12. Becoming Sthita-prajña – Practical Path

Discard selfish desires; find satisfaction in the Self

Krishna says: O Arjuna, when one discards all selfish desires and unnecessary cravings of the senses – and these are the ones that form into the mind – and becomes satisfied in the realisation of the Self – when you are realised in your own Self – such a person is said to be a sthita-prajña or transcendentally situated, stabilised in consciousness. That is very important. And I think it would do us all good to actually practice becoming sthita-prajñas.

How do we do this? Each one of us has different coping mechanisms. Some of us – you know we are doing our daily work. Many of us are working from home. Even that is a big stress of it. It is not easy. We may not be going to our offices or workplaces. Many of us may be going, some of us may not be going. We are not in a complete lockdown now. In fact the unlock process has been set in motion and slowly things are normalising. As and when required states are going in for temporary lockdowns. But on the whole there is resumption of work. So what does one do now? The fear is more. You see when the lockdown was in place we were more safe and secure. We were in the secure confines of our home. We were taking care of ourselves and we were hoping for a better tomorrow. But now that the lockdown is no longer there and we have to step out and we have to go to our workplaces – or even if we are working from home we have to go out for certain work – fear increases. There is increase in stress because in the outside world nothing has really subsided. Every day there is news of the COVID-19 surge and inflated figures and they say “You know we are not getting the real figures, we do not know what is happening, what is the ground reality.” And the moment we step out we are stepping into fear psychosis that has been created. So how do we deal with that? What do we do? What can we do to keep ourselves in equilibrium in that kind of a situation?

We need to practice. We need to practice keeping the mind stable. We can adopt any practice we feel like. We can adopt breath practices. We can practice simple meditation. We can set aside a certain time of the day – because this is a huge coping mechanism which has to become part of our daily lives. It is very, very important. And therefore it is important for us to realise that we can seek solace. We can seek solace from this ancient text even today. It is as relevant as it was thousands of years ago and it will remain relevant – because the Gita is not bound by any religious orthodoxy. It is not bound by any religious doctrine. It is universal truth that has come to us in the form of this dialogue between Arjuna and his mentor, master Krishna. And let us use this beautiful celestial song to strengthen ourselves in this period of distress and despair. Let us turn to it.

Mahatma Gandhi said he used to turn to the Gita when he used to be plunged in sorrow, plunged in confusion. All he did was turn to the Gita. 13. Stigma, Equality of Vision & Seeing the Divine Everywhere

The truly learned see with equal vision

What about stigma? What about the social stigma that is happening? That is also a reality. The other day I read about a mother who had been kept – the son said the mother had come back from a different state where she was stuck and the son refused entry to a mother who was 70-plus. And I was shocked to read that. And then later on I found many such news – many such news items of people being ostracised in their neighbourhood, people being put away, people being driven out of their homes because they tested positive or somebody known to them tested positive or because a family member tested positive. And it was appalling. Are we really progressing? Can we call ourselves a progressive society if we are doing this to our fellow men? I don’t think so.

What is learning? What is awakening? What is the sign of a truly learned man? Is it that “I am able to chant so many verses, I know so many pujas, I do so many rituals, I am conducting homas every day in my home to stay safe from the virus, I am conducting a yajna”? Is that what is required in this hour of crisis? How does one deal with it? What is the sign of a truly learned man?

vidyā-vinaya-sampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini
śuni caiva śva-pāke ca paṇḍitāḥ sama-darśinaḥ The humble sages, by virtue of true knowledge, see with equal vision a learned and gentle brāhmaṇa, a cow, an elephant, a dog and a dog-eater. (Bhagavad Gita 5.18)

With true knowledge comes humility. Humility is the trademark. Seeing everyone as equal – equality of vision. So the truly learned man, the truly progressive man, with the eyes of divine knowledge, he sees with equal vision a brāhmaṇa, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcast. He sees everyone sama-darśinaḥ – with equality of vision. Looking at everyone with equality. Is it somebody’s fault that he has fallen prey to the COVID virus? It can come to anyone at any time. So is it right on our part to treat somebody who has become a victim to this virus as an outcast? Is it right? There are horror stories doing the rounds of bodies being discarded… Of course we understand these are all fear reactions. And it is this fear that we must fight – because this fear is coming out of what? It is coming out of ignorance. It is stemming out of deep ignorance. And it is not the virus that we must fight. It is this fear, this darkness and this ignorance that we must fight so that we can stand together, stand united. That is very, very important.

yo māṃ paśyati sarvatra sarvaṃ ca mayi paśyati
tasyāhaṃ na praṇaśyāmi sa ca me na praṇaśyati For one who sees Me everywhere and sees everything in Me, I am never lost, nor is he ever lost to Me. (Bhagavad Gita 6.30)

The Lord says: I have your back. All you need to do is see Me everywhere. Now how do we integrate this to our current scenario? Can we see the divine in all those whom we see around us today – whether COVID-affected, whether COVID-free, whether a patient who has recovered, a family member, a friend who is going through some trauma? Is it possible for us to bring ourselves to truly see the divine in everything and everyone around us? And that is what we need to practice now.

You see it is very easy to preach. It is very easy to talk of lofty ideas when times are good. It is extremely easy – you know to recite the scriptures, to fall back on a lot of maybe Vedic texts or texts connected to each one’s religion and to say “This is quoted here, this is for today and this is how we must live.” All very easy in good times when everything is going well. Now today this scenario has called on our inner reserves of strength, challenged our resilience like nothing ever has. Yes there was the flu 100 years ago but we were not there then – or maybe we were in different bodies. A few hundred more years ago there was something else. And many such pandemics have come and gone. And today we are here. Probably we signed up for this when we came to the earth plane. We signed up to learn certain lessons, to be part of this pandemic. Probably yes we have signed up and that is why we are here as part of this pandemic. And we need to do something to bring ourselves out and to see what the real persona is – what we really are. Let us awaken to the divine within us. And we can awaken to the divine within us not just by talking of ahaṃ brahmāsmi. That will never happen. It is not that easy. But can we see the divine? Can we see the divine in our pet? Can we see the divine in our spouse? The divine in our parents? The divine in our colleagues? Can we see the divine in our neighbours? Can we truly see it – or is it just lip service that we are doing? That is very important. We need to contemplate on that. We need to reflect on that. The more reflective and contemplative we become, the more inner stillness we achieve. And the more inner stillness we achieve, all the turbulence slowly settles down. All that inner turmoil settles down. And when we have reached that inner stillness then we are out of confusion, we are out of delusion and we are away from the clutches of Maya. That is very important. The most important thing for us now is to be away from the clutches of ignorance which is created by a lot of fear, a lot of confusion, anxiety and a lot of stress.

14. Three Austerities – Body, Speech, Mind

Right action, right speech, right thought

So how do we keep ourselves immune? We keep ourselves immune by practicing, as I said before, austerity – austerity of body, spiritual hygiene, physical hygiene, emotional and mental austerity of speech and austerity of thought – three karaṇa: kāyena, vācā, manasā. Do the right action, whatever you are. Follow your Dharma. Right speech, right thought – and you are in the right place. That is all it takes. You see we look around for very complicated solutions when life itself is extremely simple. We like to make it complex. We look around for extremely complex-sounding terms. Life is not so complex. When it all boils down to something extremely simple: let us rest safe and secure in the thought that this is not who we really are. We are innately powerful beings. We are divine sparks. 15. The Great Lessons COVID Has Taught Us

Slow down, need very little, live every moment meaningfully

In the past few months life has taken a 360-degree turn for all of us as the virus took over the world with tremendous speed. We have been reeling under its impact. We have blamed it and we have cursed it for messing up our lives. Somewhere in some corner of our minds people have done it. Many of us may not have done it. Many of us may have understood that yes the virus has appeared as a teacher. But for most part many have not understood. It’s all right. We can always start from now. But COVID has done humanity a tremendous favour and it has taught us mainly to slow down. No more frenzy. No more mad rush. It has taught us to stand still, to step back. We were rushing blindly so it told us: Wait. Stop there. Step back and take a re-look. Rethink what is the kind of life you are leading. Stop and think. It has taught us pause. Stop there. And we cannot deny that we were leading extremely mechanical lives propelled purely by materialistic pursuits, materialistic goals. And what did we compromise? We compromised big time on simple joys. We compromised on meaningful interactions. We compromised on connecting with nature. We compromised. We started seeking happiness in frivolous things. And COVID has taught us that we really need very, very little to be happy and contented. We really do not need all that we thought we actually need. Not at all. We had a long list. Many of us – most of the population – “If I have this I will be happy… If I have that I will be happy… If I eat this I will be happy… If I get to party for so many hours I’ll be happy… If I go and visit this place I’ll be happy… If I have a home like this I’ll be happy… If I have this kind of a salary I’ll be happy… If I have two-three cars I’ll be happy…” It could be anything. These are simple examples I’m giving. But there was a burning desire to get from here to there. We wanted to jump. We wanted to reach somewhere. And there was a mad rush and we blinded ourselves to reality. We disconnected in a big way from nature. We disconnected – whereas nature was there all along. Nature didn’t change. We changed. We changed and so nature changed and she gave us the virus and she told us: “Here is a gift I’m giving you. Do what you want but step back and think and rethink and then unlearn and then relearn.” And that is what we need to do. And the biggest lesson as I said we have learnt is: we really need very, very little to be happy and contented. Most of what we thought we needed has – if you have seen it as it is – submerged now. It has gone into the background, totally put on the back-burner. We don’t need any of that. All the big lists that we had made thinking that “If I have all of this I will reach some happiness somewhere” – some far-off goal. Happiness is lying somewhere there and I have to reach, I have to cross so many milestones and then I will find that happiness. And happiness is elusive. It is like the butterfly: you run behind the butterfly and you are not going to catch it. But you sit still, sit quietly and calmly and very soon you might just find the butterfly sitting on your shoulder flapping its delicate wings. And that is what we have to realise now. It is time. The time is now – not yesterday, not tomorrow. COVID has taught us to live every moment meaningfully. And this lesson is the most beautiful lesson that we needed.

16. Who is Dear to the Lord? Contentment & Compassion

Santoshaḥ satataṃ yogī

Talking of contentment – what does the Gita say? When Krishna is asked by Arjuna: Tell me, O Krishna, please define those qualities – who is dear to You? Who actually rests in You? He says… (similarly in every chapter the questions of Arjuna are few and the answers of Krishna run into many, many verses). Basically Arjuna is just asking on behalf of all of us – Nara with Narayana – he’s asking Narayana: Tell me how does one behave?

santuṣṭaḥ satataṃ yogī yatātmā dṛḍha-niścayaḥ
mayy arpita-mano-buddhir yo mad-bhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ One who is ever content, who is a yogi, self-controlled, of firm determination, with mind and intelligence offered to Me – such a devotee is very dear to Me. (Bhagavad Gita 12.14)

How beautiful it is to even imagine such a life – santuṣṭaḥ satataṃ yogī – eternally contented, determination strong-willed and yet contented – contented because you’re self-realised – and body, mind, intellect, spirit all surrendered to that One, to Oneness. You may choose to call that One Krishna or Shiva or Allah or Jesus or God or the Supreme or Divine Consciousness – many names but one reality – and you live in a state of surrender. And that is divine bliss. So santuṣṭaḥ satataṃ yogī – ever peaceful, ever calm, not tormented. How do you get tormented? You get tormented because the mind plays with you. It plays games with you. You get caught up in all that entanglement, in a lot of story and drama and you’re caught up in mind. And the mind plays with you. And when you run behind the mind then it is endless. You cannot fathom what’s happening to you. And before you realise you have become overwhelmed and you do not know – you have sunk into the depths of despair, into the depths of helplessness. You become powerless. So how do you reclaim your power? How do you reclaim your power? You reclaim your power by remaining – or choosing to remain – in steadfastness, in stabilised consciousness.

adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānāṃ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca
nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ kṣamī One who is not envious but is a kind friend to all living entities, who does not think himself a proprietor, who is free from false ego and equal both in happiness and distress, who is tolerant… (Bhagavad Gita 12.13)

Simple things. These are the simplest of verses in the Gita. There are many, many, many and we can go on – but the simplest of verses, the most commonly used is what I have picked up. These are very close to my heart and they keep playing because these are extremely simple – even a child can understand. Compassion towards all living beings – compassion towards animate and inanimate. Do not see anything as different. Stay away from discrimination. Stay away from anger. Stay away from envy and jealousy and pride, conceit and all of that. Nirmama – without sense of proprietorship “this belongs to me”. Mamatā creates a lot of attachment, a lot of entanglements. And today COVID is teaching us that mamatā is of no value. Nirahaṅkāra – put that ego aside. An inflated ego does not do you any good. And then you are without this – what happens? Sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ – stable-minded in sukha and duḥkha. That is you are not overjoyed and elated and going on cloud nine and getting carried away by pleasures and joys, neither are you sinking into depths because of the sorrows that have befallen you. So you are equipoise. That is the word – equipoise. Stable in the face of death and destruction, stable in the face of victory and triumph and joy.

17. Karma Yoga – Right to Action, Not to the Fruits

Hypocrisy of outer restraint vs true karma-yoga

Those who restrain all the external organs of action and you know they go about saying “Oh I am very restrained” but they continue to dwell on sense objects – they take their pleasures and they keep driven by pleasures – so the Lord says these people are in a state of delusion. They act as if they have gained complete victory over their senses but in the mind they are dwelling on those sense objects. So does it help? He says such people are hypocrites. So they are not truly away from the senses. He says only those people who sincerely try to control their senses with their mind and engage in working without attachment – discharging their duties without attachment to the fruit of action – only such people are truly karma-yogis.

karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana
mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr mā te saṅgo ’stv akarmaṇi You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty. (Bhagavad Gita 2.47)

So he says: You have a right only to action. Surrender the fruits of action to Me. Perform your prescribed duties – whatever they may be. You may be a sweeper, you may be a cleaner, you may be a cook, you may be an engineer, a doctor or an IT professional, a teacher – do your prescribed duties and let go of the fruits of action. Don’t consider yourself to be the doer – because immediately that will lead to inflation of ego. And when you are caught up in the ego you’re caught up in the mind. So he says: Don’t be attached to action or inaction. There can never be a time when you are not acting. That is what Krishna says. You’re always in action. Karma is part of you. You cannot be devoid of karma. You cannot say “Oh karma will lead to karma – in the sense of action will lead to Karma for me – so let me just sit inert and lazy, let me resort to inaction, let me just sit quietly in one corner and do nothing and I can escape karma.” No. The Lord Himself says: Arjuna, I Myself am engaged in karma all the time. There is not a moment when I am in inaction. I am always engaged in karma. This multiverse is happening all the time. It is in motion. There is no split second when things have come to a standstill. So Krishna says: Perform your work. Set an example for the good of the world. And whatever actions such people do – when they do it with sincerity, with commitment – the common people follow such actions. This is for our leaders maybe, for the government, for people who are leading us. So Krishna has a word to say for them also.

yad yad ācarati śreṣṭhas tat tad evetaro janaḥ
sa yat pramāṇaṃ kurute lokas tad anuvartate Whatever action a great man performs, common men follow. And whatever standards he sets by exemplary acts, all the world pursues. (Bhagavad Gita 3.21)

Krishna compares – he says: Look at Janaka Maharaja. He did not give up. He was a karma-yogi. He was rich, he was a king, he enjoyed all the pleasures that a king enjoys – but he was a yogi. He was a householder. He was not sitting in some mountain and meditating. He discharged all his duties but he was a highly evolved soul. And that is why Krishna says: Perform your work to set an example for the good of the world. Whatever actions great people perform, common people follow them. Whatever standards you set, all the world will pursue. So when leaders set great standards then the people follow them. So it is therefore imperative that those of us who are in positions of leadership – we need to do our duties with commitment, with honour – so that others learn and follow. We must set the right example. We must become good role models. It is very important.

• Practice three austerities daily: body (hygiene + moderation), speech (no agitation, truth, kindness), mind (simplicity, purity of thought).
• Lifestyle prescription of the Gita: yuktāhāra-vihārasya – moderation in food, recreation, sleep and effort.
• COVID has already delivered its greatest gift: the realisation that we need very little to be content, and that happiness is found by sitting still, not by chasing the butterfly.
• See the Divine in every being – the recovered patient, the frightened neighbour, the frontline worker. Equality of vision is the hallmark of true knowledge.
• You have a right only to action; the fruits belong to the Divine. Lead by example – the world follows what the best among us actually live.
• Five minutes of silent offering for the welfare of all beings is one of the most powerful contributions any of us can make right now.
