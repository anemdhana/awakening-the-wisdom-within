Honoring Our Ancestors – Message, Practice & Guidance

Complete restructuring of the AHA Full Moon session held at the beginning of Pitru Paksha. Every major teaching from Dr. Newton and Dr. Lakshmi is preserved – the meaning of this sacred window, how to connect daily with ancestors, what to do with inherited patterns, and answers to deep questions on detachment, healing and love – so the wisdom can be read, contemplated and practised throughout the fortnight.

1. Opening – Cycles of Renewal & the Gift of Life

Dr. Newton: Every time we look forward to these AHA Full Moon conversations. In our Bharat Desh culture there is deep wisdom, and the same exists in other cultures too. Ganesh Visarjan carries a powerful message: everything that is created can also be uncreated. We need to be ready to let go, dissolve, and then form again – recreate ourselves anew. These cycles of renewal keep happening. Ganesh Chaturthi begins, is celebrated for nine or ten days, and finally the immersion takes place – a dissolution process.

Today is a beautiful Full Moon. Pitru Paksha has already begun from yesterday. This Full Moon is completely dedicated to our ancestors – to reconnect with them. Mostly we have lost the connection. We do not realise that behind us the ancestors are always there. We never acknowledge them. It is so important to acknowledge our ancestors, their presence, the gift of life, the love and life-force they passed on through many generations through our parents. We received this gift of life. We need to definitely acknowledge and honour them. Then a certain strength we feel in us. We need to be grateful. Without gratitude, without thankfulness, there is no real meaning; nothing progresses in our life.

2. Dr. Lakshmi – Why We Must Be Grateful

Dr. Lakshmi: Namaskar. Happy Purnima to all of you. We are who we are because of those who existed before us. We received this life because they existed. If they did not exist, we would not exist today.

Right now our life is so comfortable compared to even a hundred or two hundred years ago, leave alone a thousand or five thousand years back. People did not even have clothes; when they felt cold they had nothing. Today we have sweaters, jackets, shoes, chappals. If we want to go somewhere we catch a flight and within a few hours we are in USA or Europe. Our elders, if they had to go to Kashi, it used to take months – they travelled by foot. Just imagine how comfortable our life is. We have everything, and yet we take this life for granted. We say “I don’t have this, I don’t have that, I want to die, I don’t want to live.” That means we are taking this precious life for granted.

If you were the first person who had to prepare a shoe or a chappal, how difficult it would be. Our ancestors went through those challenges and struggles and made our life comfortable. These sixteen days (from yesterday) are such a beautiful time to remember them, honour them, and be grateful. If you are not grateful for all that they have done, for all those challenges they faced so that we could live comfortably, life becomes meaningless.

They have left us with this beautiful life-force and life-energy. What we are going to do with that is in our hands.

3. The Inheritance We Carry – Refuse What Does Not Belong

Dr. Lakshmi: Most of us are simply carrying negative patterns from our ancestors. One day Mulla Nasruddin shouted at his son without any reason. The son said, “Dad, I didn’t do anything wrong. Why are you shouting?” Mulla replied, “It’s not only me. My father also shouted at me without any reason, and his father shouted at him. As far as I know this has been happening for the past three or four generations.” It is being passed on without a ticket – like an inheritance. The son said, “Dad, I refuse this inheritance.” Mulla started crying: “You are the first man in the family who is free. Please teach me also how to refuse this inheritance.”

We too have inherited pain, suffering and so many patterns, and sometimes we even feel great about carrying them. Actually they passed this life to us so that we can become better, live in a better way. They underwent the struggles so that we could survive and become a better version of ourselves.

For the next fourteen to sixteen days let us connect to their energies – being grateful, acknowledging them, and at the same time handing back all those negative patterns that do not belong to us. Their energies are available on this planet right now. It is very easy to let go of whatever you want to let go. Their blessings are accessible. Let us receive the blessings and become the best version of ourselves.

4. How to Honour Ancestors Every Day (Practical Method)

Dr. Newton: Everyone talks about honouring ancestors – but how? Pitru Paksha is one paksha – roughly fifteen days from Full Moon to New Moon. Why only these fifteen days? Because this is the time window when ancestral energy is available. Pitru Loka is open. The frequency of that dimensional world is open. We can easily access the benevolent energies and blessings of the ancestors. Any transformation of patterns is possible now.

Every day, how we can honour them:

  • Acknowledge the pain – Look at those ancestors who had pain and suffering. Silently say: “Dear ancestors, you failed in your life, you went through challenges. I acknowledge your pain. I see your pain.”
  • Promise a different life – Say: “Dear ancestors, I see your pain. I will be better than you. I will be totally different. I will live without this battle, without this health issue, without these psychological traits, without this violent energy. I will make you proud of me as a descendant of this family tree.”
  • Draw inspiration from the successful ones – Look at those ancestors who succeeded, who prospered, who excelled. Say: “I feel inspired when I look at you. Thank you. You are role models for me.”
Daily practice (5–10 minutes): Sit with closed eyes. If you feel like it, light a diya. Connect from the heart and speak these words silently. That is enough. Do it every day without fail during these fifteen days. Pass on new energies and new information to the future generations, and receive their blessings.

5. Should We Avoid New Beginnings & Purchases During Pitru Paksha?

Question (Rahul): There is a belief that one should avoid starting anything new or auspicious, or making significant purchases, during Pitru Paksha. What is your perspective?

Dr. Newton: Those who are on the path of truth and meditation sometimes need to override limiting beliefs. If something is truly essential and cannot be postponed, it is okay to purchase. We do not hold rigid rules that bind us when life itself requires action. Some of these beliefs are valid in certain contexts; jyotish may also have its reasons. I am not well-versed in that, so I will not speak much on it.

Dr. Lakshmi: From another perspective: when you are connected to the truth, many outer rules lose their absolute hold. Yet this particular period is specially, specially for honouring the ancestors. In a year this is the only concentrated time we remember them, acknowledge them and express gratefulness. That is one reason the tradition says: spend this time expressing gratitude and doing service.

In the Padma Purana and Garuda Purana it is said that the ancestors are available on this physical plane during these days. If you offer food to the hungry, to the destitute, if you help them, that reaches your ancestors and makes them happy; they bless you. This includes feeding dogs, cows, animals, crows – crow has a very important place. You are so busy in ordinary life; these sixteen days give the opportunity to serve everybody around us who is in need, and even the animals.

Practical guidance: Ordinary daily needs (rice, dal, essentials) are fine. Significant new purchases – a big house, jewellery, major new beginnings – can be deferred for two or three weeks so that the focus remains on remembrance, gratitude and seva.

6. Detachment, Changing Personality & Feeling “Less Driven”

Question (Sepal): As I progress on the spiritual journey I am getting detached from many previous activities. My personality is changing. I used to be extremely money- and career-driven; lately I cannot feel that drive, even though outer things are not the way I wanted and everything feels like it is crashing. I am still disciplined and face my fears. Is this normal? Is there something I need to do?

Dr. Newton: This is a normal part of the journey. As a meditator you raise your vibration. Before the practice there may have been himsa – violent speech, certain social habits, a friend circle of gossip and drinking. After regular meditation you cannot waste time that way. You no longer want to gossip or booze. Your friend circle changes; you want heart-to-heart conversation with spiritual friends. It feels as if the old world is crumbling, but a new life and a new circle are emerging. Accept this phase. We have all gone through it.

Dr. Lakshmi: There is a classic description of stages: first “mountains are mountains, trees are trees”; then “mountains are not mountains, trees are not trees” (everything feels different, you don’t like the old friends or activities); finally again “mountains are mountains, trees are trees” – you accept everything, you do not judge, you can be with everyone yet remain unaffected, like Sri Krishna or like the joker card that fits with every suit. You carry your fragrance wherever you go.

Dr. Newton: Sometimes the conditioned mind judges: “Am I becoming lazy?” Meditators can appear lazy from outside, but inwardly there is intense activity – inner growth. Meditation is not a passive process. One of the treasures of the Shatsampatti taught by Shankaracharya is uparati – withdrawal from outer pleasure-seeking so that the permanent joy (paramananda) can be discovered. Outside joy is conditional (only when you get an award or success). Here you become happy for no reason – a bundle of joy.

Lord Krishna gave two words: abhyasa (practice) and vairagya (conscious non-attachment). When we are completely indulging in the outer world, when do we get time to go within and awaken the wisdom faculties? A little detachment is very much needed. Contentment in the material plane and a healthy discontentment on the spiritual path is the right combination. Accept this phase, Sepal.

7. When Healing Does Not Happen Despite Many Sessions

Question (Usha): I hear of people who heal with a single past-life or constellation or inner-child session. I have needed multiple sessions and different modalities, yet one major issue has not changed significantly. Could it be lack of trust, a belief that healing cannot happen quickly, a limiting belief that my issue cannot be resolved, or the feeling that I have to keep trying harder?

Dr. Newton: Sometimes we keep certain things with us; we do not want them to heal for reasons the conscious mind does not know. There can be secondary gains. Example: a soul that was very elevated and arrogant in a past life may attract an illness in this life that constantly keeps them humble. Subconsciously they know, “If I heal completely I may become arrogant again; I need something that grounds me.” Many yogis and masters keep one small limitation so they remain humble. This is only one possible example.

Look into yourself: in what way is this unhealed limitation helping you? Sometimes there are blind spots. The mind distracts us to this past life or that past life, while the real key may be in the womb memory or early childhood. The subconscious protects the most painful memory. Request a dream from the universe – unfiltered information often comes symbolically in dreams – asking what you need to work on so that healing can accelerate.

Dr. Lakshmi: Notice that the very beliefs you listed in your question are often what stop the healing: lack of trust, “healing cannot happen quickly,” “my issue cannot be resolved,” “I have to keep trying harder.” Your subconscious has already given you the answer through the question itself. If it is possible for someone else in one session, why not for you? Often we only touch superficial layers and conclude nothing happened, because we did not touch the right switch.

Guidance: Give yourself time. Sit and honestly ask those questions. Be patient and gentle – especially with the inner child. Rigid expectations (“everyone else heals fast, why not me?”) lead to “I am not good enough” and further blocking. Do the work, yet surrender the outcome. Healing happens when we are ready.

8. What Is Love? Rising in Love, Not Falling

Question: What is love, and how many times can one fall in love?

Dr. Newton: A beautiful definition from Scott Peck’s The Road Less Traveled: “Love is the will to extend oneself for the purpose of nurturing one’s own or another’s spiritual growth.” Time is love. The quality time you spend with anyone is love. If a husband truly loves his wife he gives quality time: “I have all the time, my dear.” Teaching meditation, sharing the joy and wisdom one has experienced so that others may also experience it – that is love in action, not merely a word.

The second part – “how many times can you fall in love?” – better to rise in love. Falling in love often leads to falling out of love. Rising in love means love becomes your very nature, because the fundamental nature of every human being is love. The great truth is that this whole universe is made of love. Planets follow a rhythm, the universe follows a rhythm – all created out of love. Parents created us out of love. There is only love. If we have the eyes we see love everywhere. Each of us is an ocean of love; we only need to wake up and recognise the love we already have. Then we rise in love and see love in every tree, plant, stone, star and moon. Love only heals us.

Dr. Lakshmi: (With a smile) One day Mulla was searching for a greeting card for his lover. He saw a card that said “To the only woman whom I love the most.” The shopkeeper asked if he liked it. Mulla said, “Yes – give me six cards.”

Everything that is created came from love. Being in the being-state, what you experience is only love. When you rise in that state you also give your hand to others so they can rise with you. Let us connect to that being-state and experience love every second.

9. Guided Ancestral Meditation (Full Moon Practice)

Dr. Lakshmi (guiding): Close your eyes. Connect to the energies of the Full Moon. With each breath connect to the energies of your ancestors. Visualise yourself stepping into a beautiful ancestral garden. Feel gratefulness, respect and reverence.

Allow all your ancestors to come from different directions and form a circle. You stand in the middle. Perceive them as energy, as light, or simply feel their presence. Look around and silently say: “I can see you. I acknowledge you. I acknowledge your pain and your suffering.”

Hear them reply: “We suffered. You do not need to suffer like us. Release the pain and take the strength from us.”

One ancestor from the maternal line (grandmother or great-grandmother) steps forward, places her hand on your heart and transmits a beautiful positive quality you need right now – perhaps unconditional love. Receive it and say thank you.

One ancestor from the paternal line (grandfather or great-grandfather) steps forward, places his hand on your head and transmits resilience, courage, strength – whatever you need. Receive it fully. As you inhale say “I receive this gift”; as you exhale say “Thank you.”

Feel the strength, courage, resilience or love inside you. Tell your ancestors: “Please bless me, my family and my children. In your honour we live well.” Feel all of them placing their hands on your head in blessing. Bow down, receive, and with deep gratitude slowly return.

You may use this short meditation every day of Pitru Paksha. First identify one quality you most need (freedom, courage, love, clarity…). Then request that specific gift from the ancestors while sitting in the ancestral circle.

Core Take-aways for Pitru Paksha

• These 15–16 days the Pitru Loka frequency is open – the easiest time to connect, receive blessings and release patterns.
• Daily 5–10 minutes: acknowledge their pain, promise to live differently and make them proud, take inspiration from the successful ones.
• Hand back negative inherited patterns; refuse the “inheritance” that does not serve life.
• Express gratitude through seva – feeding the hungry, destitute, crows, dogs, cows; it reaches the ancestors.
• Defer major new purchases and purely personal gratification so the fortnight remains dedicated to remembrance.
• Detachment and loss of old drive are normal stages of rising vibration – not laziness; they create space for inner growth (uparati, vairagya).
• When healing stalls, look for secondary gains, blind spots and the very beliefs listed in the question; request a clarifying dream; be gentle and patient.
• Love is the will to nurture growth; time given is love; rise in love until love becomes your nature – the universe itself is made of love.
