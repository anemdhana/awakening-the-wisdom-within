Swasa Maha Vidya · Day 1

Moksha in the Subtle

This page follows the full discourse after meditation — principles, stories, examples — then live questions from the same session.

Main discourse

1. Today's topic after meditation

Touch the eyes lightly with both hands for a moment, open the eyes with a smile and joy. We rose before sunrise and sat for practice — continue in the same way. From today: Swasa Maha Vidya — for these 11 days we speak of the breath. There is so much to know; however much we learn about the breath, it is still little.

2. Breath is everything — why does a doctor check the breath?

Breath is everything. When breath stops, life stops. To know whether one is alive or dead, the breath is checked. The physician first checks the breath.

First principle: Breath is everything; breath is the root. Of all things, the root is the breath.

3. What, once known, makes everything known?

In spiritual science we must know that by knowing which everything is known. The link of Self-knowledge, mind-knowledge and inner knowledge is with the breath. Knowing the breath, we come to know all. Breath leads to the Self; it reveals the state of the Self.

4. Hanuman, Sita–Rama — what does the breath unite?

In Valmiki's Ramayana the story turns around Hanuman. Hanuman is Vayuputra — the great master who introduced Swasa Maha Vidya to the world. With breath practice one can feel Rama near, as Hanuman did.

Hanuman united Sita and Rama. When Ravana held Sita in Lanka, Hanuman burned Lanka — he reduced ignorance to ash. Sita is the infinite power within us — Mulaprakriti, Kundalini. Breath practice unites individual power with supreme consciousness.

5. Why do all techniques revolve around breath? Without practice?

Study the world's traditions and techniques — they mainly revolve around the breath. Even a doctor says: breathe deeply. In pain, dizziness, or after an accident — keep breathing well. The secret science of all is Swasa Maha Vidya.

Hanuman reached that state through this vidya. Gods and yogis too held Swasa Maha Vidya and attained mastery. Breath introduces Shakti and the conscious Self. Knowledge without practice is zero. Knowing is not enough — daily sadhana is needed.

6. Patriji: “Attention on the breath”; what did Ramana say?

Brahmarshi Pitamaha Patriji — my Guru — taught one small point: attention on the breath, observing the breath. With continuous practice the answer becomes clear. Ramana Maharshi pointed to the source of the “I” — the inward journey often begins with the breath as the nearest doorway.

7. Totakacharya — Ganga, Kumbh Mela, the infant

Totakacharya (8th century): Adi Shankaracharya made him the first Jagadguru of the seat. At the Kumbh, near the Ganga, a mother's infant had died. Totakacharya entered samadhi, left body-consciousness, entered the child's body (parakaya pravesha), and revived the child — mastery of subtle consciousness over the gross body.

8. Parakaya pravesha; Tvamevaham; the Jagadguru seat

The Guru closed his eyes, entered samadhi, left his own body and entered the child's body. Consciousness can move; identification is not limited to one body. “Tvam eva aham” — You alone am I. Such mastery comes from deep sadhana, not from book-knowledge alone.

9. Gross vs subtle — ice, water, steam

Moksha is subtle. Its opposite is the gross. Water frozen is ice — gross. When temperature rises, water — a little subtler. Heated to 100°C it becomes steam — still subtler. Attention on the breath moves awareness from the gross body toward the subtle. Liberation is found in the subtle.

10. Japa-yajna, upamshu, manasa japa, ajapa Gayatri

Methods were placed even in the gross: mantra japa. Gayatri — loud japa, then upamshu (whispered), then manasa (mental). Highest is ajapa — the mantra that runs with the natural breath without effort. Gayatri merged with the breath becomes living practice.

11. Homeopathy potency; inner yajna / nityagnihotra

In homeopathy the lab may show nothing biochemically; vibration / potentisation remains. The subtler it is made, the stronger the potency. Outer fire rituals have an inner counterpart: attention on the breath as nitya-agnihotra — the daily inner yajna.

12. Moksha in the subtle — tree in the seed; Raja-vidya

Ordinarily we do not observe the breath. Observing that most subtle breath-movement is moksha in the subtle — the path to liberation; liberation is in the subtle itself. The tree is already in the seed. This is Raja-vidya — the royal science.

13. Village basket — attention in daily work

In childhood in Machilipatnam / Kaikaluru, people worked in the fields with steady rhythm. Attention need not leave work: while working, keep a light touch on the breath. Practice is not only on the cushion; it continues in ordinary action.

14. Snake in the anthill — the mind; breath language / rhythm

A snake lives in an anthill. Approach and watch — it may put out its head, see you, and hide. If you stay and keep watching quietly, after a long time it may come out and stay. The mind is like that snake. Sit with the breath; do not chase the mind. The breath has its own language and rhythm — learn to listen.

15. Vivekananda — 32 volumes; the subtle guru

Swami Vivekananda held Swasa Maha Vidya. His memory was extraordinary — he asked for 32 volumes of the Britannica. The subtle guru is the breath itself when attention is steady. Outer books help; inner observation completes.

16. Daily practice — Sukhasana; touch during work

Daily life practice: sit comfortably in Sukhasana, eyes closed, observe the natural breath. Later, in any work, keep a soft attention on the breath. This is the continuous thread of Swasa Maha Vidya.

Live Q&A

17. Parvati: Energy, consciousness, Self — are they one?

Parvati: Energy, consciousness and Self in the body — are they different?

At first energy and consciousness seem separate. With meditation and experience they are understood as one. Kundalini in Muladhara rises with practice; at Ajna it unites with consciousness — Ardhanarishvara. Shiva = pure consciousness; Parvati = Mulaprakriti / Kundalini. Their union is knowledge.

18. Samadhi — savikalpa, nirvikalpa, Sahasrara

Parvati: Is samadhi the highest state in meditation?

Samadhi is the highest state of meditation — where answers are obtained. Seventh state: crossing six chakras, Sahasrara opens, all-knowledge; questions cease. First savikalpa, then nirvikalpa. Only through meditation.
