Swasa Maha Vidya · Day 6

Language of the Breath

Breath has its own language — puraka, rechaka, kumbhaka; ida, pingala, sushumna; fear and calm.

Main discourse

1–2. Does the breath have a language?

Attention on the breath is great practice. The breath is a language. Not only we breathe — the whole cosmos breathes: earth, trees, animals. Learn to read this language.

3. Puraka, rechaka, kumbhaka

In-breath = puraka; out-breath = rechaka. The nano-pause between them is kumbhaka — inner (after inhale) and outer (after exhale).

4–5. Natural kumbhaka vs force

In higher states the breath slows and natural kumbhaka increases — not forced. Consciousness stabilizes near Ajna. Rama at Bharadvaja ashram points to natural stillness, not strain.

6. Ida, pingala, sushumna

Left nostril = ida / moon / cooling; right = pingala / sun / activating; balanced flow opens sushumna — the central channel.

7–8. Fear vs calm

Fear: fast breath, right-nostril dominance, sympathetic fight/flight. Calm satsang: left/moon, parasympathetic, softer breath.

9–10. Nonverbal breath language

Soldiers hold breath in silence; sighs, yawns, dry throat speak boredom or tension without words. Read the body's breath signals.

11–12. Cosmos and empathy

Mother Earth, trees, cows also signal through life-breath. Empathy differs from mere sympathy — feel the shared field of prana.

Live Q&A

Live Q&A (summary)

Do not force kumbhaka. Observe which nostril is active. Return to soft attention on natural breath as the daily language practice.
