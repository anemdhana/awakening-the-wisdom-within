Day 1 · Extended Q&A

Moksha in the Subtle

Condensed Q&A from Dr. Newton Kondaveti · full extended text in Telugu source.

1. Today's topic after meditation

Touch the eyes lightly with both hands for a moment; open the eyes with a smile and joy. Rise before sunrise and continue practice. For these 11 days we speak of the breath — Swasa Maha Vidya. However much we learn about the breath, it is still little.

2. Breath is everything — why does a doctor check the breath?

Breath is everything. When breath stops, life stops. To know whether one is alive or dead, the breath is checked. First principle: breath is the root of all.

3. What, once known, makes everything known?

In spiritual science we must know that by knowing which everything is known. The link of Self-knowledge, mind-knowledge and inner knowledge is with the breath. Knowing the breath, we come to know all.

4. Hanuman, Sita–Rama — what does the breath unite?

Hanuman is Vayuputra — the master who introduced Swasa Maha Vidya. Breath practice unites individual power (Sita / Kundalini) with supreme consciousness (Rama).

5. Why do all techniques revolve around breath?

World traditions and techniques mainly revolve around the breath. Even a doctor says: breathe deeply. In pain or accident — keep breathing well. Knowledge without practice is zero; daily sadhana is needed.

6. Patriji: “Attention on the breath”; what did Ramana say?

Brahmarshi Patriji taught one point: attention on the breath. Ramana Maharshi pointed to the source of the “I” — the inward journey often begins with the breath as the nearest doorway.

7. Totakacharya — Ganga, Kumbh, the infant

8th century. Totakacharya practiced Swasa Maha Vidya before meeting Adi Shankara. At the Kumbh, a mother's infant had died. He entered samadhi, entered the child's body (parakaya pravesha), and the child revived.

8. Parakaya pravesha; Twamevaham; Jagadguru seat

After the revival, the child grew joyful and silent for years. Later he expressed the truth “You alone am I.” Adi Shankara made Totakacharya the first Jagadguru of that seat.

9. Gross vs subtle — ice, water, steam

Moksha is subtle. Ice is gross water; heat makes water, then steam — subtler. Attention on the breath moves awareness from the gross body toward the subtle. Liberation is found in the subtle.

10. Japa-yajna, upamshu, manasa japa, ajapa Gayatri

Gross methods include mantra japa: loud, then whispered (upamshu), then mental (manasa). Highest is ajapa — the mantra that runs with the natural breath without effort.

11. Homeopathy potency; inner yajna

In homeopathy, subtler potentisation means stronger effect. Outer fire rituals have an inner counterpart: attention on the breath as nitya-agnihotra — the daily inner yajna.

12. Moksha in the subtle — tree in the seed

Observing that most subtle breath-movement is moksha in the subtle. The tree is already in the seed. This is Raja-vidya — the royal science.

13. Village basket — attention in daily work

Attention need not leave work. While working, keep a light touch on the breath. Practice continues in ordinary action, not only on the cushion.

14. Snake in the anthill — the mind

The mind is like a snake in an anthill. Sit with the breath; do not chase the mind. The breath has its own language and rhythm — learn to listen.

15. Vivekananda — subtle guru

Swami Vivekananda held Swasa Maha Vidya. The subtle guru is the breath itself when attention is steady. Outer books help; inner observation completes.
