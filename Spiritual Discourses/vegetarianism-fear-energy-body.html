Should I Be Vegetarian? Fear, Consumption & the Subtler Bodies

A clear, practical answer from Master Minood: if you want to go deep in spirituality, vegetarianism is the way forward — not as a rule for its own sake, but because of what enters the energy body when a being dies in fear.

1. The direct answer

Should I be vegetarian or non-vegetarian?

Obviously, if you really want to move deep in spirituality, vegetarianism is the way forward.

Why? Let us discuss this.

2. What happens when a being is killed

When you kill anything, the one which is being killed is not dying in habits — it is dying in fear.

The moment it dies, it absorbs or retains that fear within itself.

When you consume that, that energy goes into your body. You cannot consume anything and leave out the part you do not want. It does not work that way.

100% — what we consume, we become.

3. Physical part and energy part

When you take something which has fat and protein, you cannot decide after you consume it: “I will take only the protein today.” The fat also enters the body.

In the same fashion, the energy also enters the body.

Whatever you consume:

Aspect of what is consumed Where it goes
Physical part (matter, nutrients) Outer gross physical body
Subtler / energy portion (including fear-vibration) Inner energy bodies

So you are, in a way, not only building muscles in the physical body — you are also building your energy body by what you consume. Therefore you have to be careful what you take in.

4. Why this matters for meditation

Every day we meditate. Where are we trying to go?

We are going to our energy part — the energy body, the subtler body — and we are trying to be in our soul consciousness.

The soul is governed by four inner energy bodies and one outer physical body.

If the inner bodies are repeatedly fed with the vibration of fear and violence, the very field we are trying to purify and rest in during meditation is being clouded by what we eat.

5. The simple premise

Always go for things which have less fear and less violence in them.

That is simply the premise of why one should become vegetarian (and, for those who go further, why some choose vegan).

It is not only a moral slogan. It is a practical care for the quality of the energy body — the same body we meet every day in meditation as we move toward soul consciousness.

6. Practical reflection points

1. What am I building?
Just as food builds the physical body, the subtle charge of that food builds the energy body. Ask: am I feeding clarity or fear into the field I meditate in?

2. You cannot filter after the fact
You cannot take the protein and leave the fear. What is in the being at the moment of death is part of what enters.

3. Align means with goal
If the goal is deep spirituality and stable soul-consciousness, choose food that carries less violence and less terror.

4. No self-hatred — only clarity
Past choices can be met with understanding. Present choice can be made with awareness. The teaching is an invitation to care, not to guilt.

5. Small steady steps
Moving toward less fear in food is itself a form of respect for the inner bodies and for the beings whose lives intersect with ours.

7. One clear distinction

Consuming what dies in fear Choosing food with less fear / violence
Physical nutrients + fear-energy enter Physical nourishment with quieter subtle charge
Energy body is repeatedly tuned toward agitation Energy body is supported toward the clarity needed for meditation
Outer goal (spiritual depth) and inner fuel diverge Means and goal begin to align

If you really want to move deep in spirituality, vegetarianism is the way forward.

Not because of a rule written somewhere, but because a being that dies in fear retains that fear — and what you consume, you become, in both the physical body and the energy body.

We meditate to rest in soul consciousness. Care for the quality of what enters the subtler bodies is part of that path.

— Master Minood · Temple of Inner Wisdom
