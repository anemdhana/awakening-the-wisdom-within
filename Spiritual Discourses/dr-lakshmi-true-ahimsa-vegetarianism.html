Dr. Lakshmi True Ahimsa · Humaneness · Vegetarian Living

Shakahara Dhyana Yajna · Ahimsa

True Humaneness Begins on the Plate

A passionate appeal delivered at a vegetarian–meditation gathering. Health benefits of plant food have already been spoken by many elders. Here the focus shifts: real humaneness (mānavatva) and real non-violence (ahimsa) must appear in what we eat and how we treat other living beings — otherwise talk of peace and compassion remains incomplete.

1. Opening context

Respected elders on the stage and all the friends of knowledge who have come for this dāna–dhyāna–śākāhāra gathering — greetings.

Until now many elders have explained beautifully how good vegetarian food is for our health and why we should take it. There is no need to repeat what is already known. I know that many of you are already vegetarians. Let all the vegetarians raise their voice strongly once — let us take the pledge together.

2. India’s spiritual culture

Our Bhārata Bhūmi is a land of merit, a land of karma, a land of spirituality. Vegetarian living is woven into our culture, our tradition and our spiritual knowledge.

Yet today we are becoming used to Western trends and forgetting our own culture, our ācāra and our vyavahāra. Buddha Bhagavān and Mahāvīra — our spiritual teachers — have for thousands of years taught the importance of vegetarian food and of ahimsa. That we should now have to speak of vegetarianism as something new, and try to change everyone, is itself a matter of shame.

మన భరత భూమి పుణ్య భూమి, కర్మభూమి, ఆధ్యాత్మిక భూమి. ఈ శాఖాహారం మన సంస్కృతి, మన సాంప్రదాయం, మన ఆధ్యాత్మిక జ్ఞానంతో కూడినది. కానీ ఇవాళ పాశ్చాత్య పోకడలకు అలవాటు పడి మన సంస్కృతిని, ఆచారాన్ని మర్చిపోతున్నాం. బుద్ధ భగవానుడు, మహావీరుడు వేల సంవత్సరాలుగా శాఖాహారం మరియు అహింస గురించి బోధించారు. ఇవాళ కొత్తగా చెప్పుకోవాలి అనడం సిగ్గుపడవలసిన విషయం.

3. From “good for my health” to true humaneness

Most talks say: “Vegetarian food is very good for your health.” That is true, yet incomplete. We call ourselves human beings and we speak of humaneness. What is real humaneness?

How do we love ourselves? How do we love our children? Then how many of us are truly living as human beings? How many of us are living our lives with humaneness?

A great soul once said: “You who keep meat on your plate have no right to speak of love, compassion and kindness.” How can one speak of dayā while eating food born of himsa? The whole world wants peace, yet as long as food of violence remains, how is peace possible? Peace will never come that way.

Only when we live as true human beings can we actually experience peace, love and compassion.

4. Animals also have families and feelings

If we are human and if humaneness is our religion, then we must also recognise this: every animal has a family. Does a cow not have children? Does a deer or a goat not have offspring? They have joy, they have sorrow — they experience all of it.

When we understand this and live with humaneness, we not only live our own lives with wonder and happiness; we also help other beings to live the life that is their right. That is real humaneness.

ఆవుకి బిడ్డలు లేరా? ఒక జింకకి, ఒక మేకకి బిడ్డలు లేరా? వాటికి కుటుంబం ఉంది, సంతోషం ఉంది, దుఃఖం ఉంది. మానవత్వంలో జీవించే మనం ఇది అర్థం చేసుకున్నప్పుడు మన జీవితాన్ని అద్భుతంగా జీవించడమే కాకుండా, జీవులు జీవించే హక్కుకు కూడా తోడ్పడతాం. అదే మానవత్వం.

5. We are only caretakers of the Earth

We think of Mother Earth and nature as our property. They are not. We are only guardians. Our responsibility is to hand over this Earth — with pure water and pure food — to future generations.

Today species are disappearing. Living beings cannot bear the cruelty and inhumanity of man, so they leave this planet. In such a situation, if we are still living here, we should hang our heads in shame. Knowing this, we must change our behaviour, live as true human beings with humaneness, and help other creatures live. Then we will have achieved something real. Divinity can wait; first become truly human.

6. Who needs whom?

One truth we must recognise: on this Earth, if human beings disappeared, the other living beings could still continue. But if all other living beings disappeared and only humans remained, humans could not survive.

Who is greater? Who needs whom? When we recognise this and wish to live happily on this Earth and to know divinity, the first action required is to love fellow beings and to change our food habits — not only in words, not only with the hands, but in the whole of life, every moment.

7. The parable of the branch

A man sat on a tree branch and began cutting the same branch he was sitting on. A passer-by said, “Fool, what are you doing? You will fall.” The man replied, “I am doing the right thing — I need this tree.” When he cut the branch, it broke and he fell with it and died.

This is the condition of humanity today. We are cutting the very support of life while sitting on it.

8. Reflection from Acharya (A Soul’s Journey)

Acharya · Group soul & fear

Acharya teaches that animals of the same type share a group soul. Fear of the human being is the great retarding force in that kingdom. When man multiplies terror through needless killing, the progress of the group soul is slowed. Kindness and the removal of fear open the way toward individualisation.

Dr. Lakshmi’s call to see that animals have families and feelings is therefore not only emotional — it is evolutionary. Reducing the injection of fear into the web of life is part of human responsibility on the path.

9. Reflection from Ramtha

Ramtha · Consistency of life

Ramtha’s teaching centres on living as the Creator in this Now — in joy, without the constant fuel of fear. When the daily act of eating carries the vibration of violence and terror, the emotional field is repeatedly tuned to that frequency. Speaking of peace while feeding on fear creates an inner split.

True change begins when the outer action and the inner aspiration no longer contradict each other. Choosing food that does not multiply fear is one concrete way of aligning the life one lives with the peace one claims to want.

10. Practical points for daily life

Speech Do not speak of dayā and śānti while the plate still carries himsa
View of animals See family, joy and sorrow in them — not only utility

Vegetarianism is not only a health choice or a cultural habit. It is a test of whether humaneness is real or only spoken.

Animals have families and feelings. We are caretakers of the Earth, not its owners. When we cut the branch we are sitting on, we fall with it.

First live as true human beings. Then the Earth can become a realm of love and the divine can be known in daily life.

— Dr. Lakshmi · with reflections from Acharya & Ramtha
