When Mother Criticizes, Hurts & Does Not Speak Softly

How to stay, how to behave, and how to keep your centre — according to the spirit of Ramtha’s teaching on the Now and Acharya’s evolutionary understanding of the soul.

1. You are not the personality she is attacking

Ramtha’s core point: you are not the limited self that feels wounded. That self was trained to seek love, approval, and softness from the mother. When it does not receive them, it suffers.

The real you is the awareness that can watch the pain arise and not become it.

Acharya’s line of teaching: every soul is at its own evolutionary point. Your mother’s hardness, criticism, or lack of softness is the present expression of her own fear, conditioning, and unfinished learning — not the final truth of who she is, and not the final truth of who you are.

2. Do not absorb the frequency

Ramtha repeatedly shows that thought creates feeling, and feeling creates the condition of life. If you take her words in, replay them, argue with them inside, or try to force her to become soft, you are feeding the same frequency of lack and hurt.

The practical move:
• Hear the words.
• Notice the feeling in the body.
• Do not add a second layer of story (“She never loved me,” “I am unlovable,” “I must fix her”).
• Return to the breath or to the simple knowing: “This is her present state. It is not my identity.”

3. Stay, or create distance — from clarity, not from revenge

Neither teaching says you must remain in daily emotional injury.

If you still live with her or must interact often:
Keep the outer behaviour respectful and clean, but keep the inner centre unmoved. Speak less when she is in criticism mode. Do not explain, justify, or beg for softness in those moments. Softness that is forced is not real; it only creates more tension.

If the pattern is chronic and damaging:
Physical or emotional distance is allowed. Leaving the house, reducing contact, or setting clear limits is not “unspiritual.” It is refusing to keep feeding a loop that harms both of you.

The measure is simple: does this interaction leave you more in fear and contraction, or more in your own centre?

4. The deeper work

Witness (Sakshi). Watch the hurt arise without becoming the hurt child. This is the same witnessing that frees you in meditation and in after-death states.

Do not wait for her to change. Waiting keeps you bound. Your peace cannot depend on another person’s evolution.

Love without demand. You can still hold a quiet goodwill toward her soul while no longer needing her personality to give you what it cannot presently give. That is closer to real love than continual hurt and hope.

Your own frequency. Every time you refuse to match her criticism with your own inner criticism or despair, you raise the field. That is the practical application of “raise the vibration” that Acharya’s evolutionary teaching points toward.

5. Short daily practice when the pain is fresh

  1. Feel the sensation in the body for 30–60 seconds without story.
  2. Soften the belly and return to natural breath.
  3. Silently: “This pain is here. I am the one who sees it. I am not the pain.”
  4. If you must reply, reply only what is necessary and true, without emotional charge.
  5. Later, if needed, take physical space.

Core reminder
You are not required to stay soft while being repeatedly hurt.
You are invited to stay awake — neither collapsing into the wounded child nor hardening into the same criticism you received.

That is the way both Ramtha’s “live and be happy in the Now” and Acharya’s evolutionary patience meet in daily life with a difficult parent.
