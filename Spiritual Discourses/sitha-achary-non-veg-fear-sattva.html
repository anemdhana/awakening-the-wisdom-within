Sitha Achary On food, fear & the quality of consciousness

Food · Fear · Sattva

Non-Veg, Absorbed Fear & the Call to Sattvic Living

A direct, fiery teaching on why the choice of food is not only about health or scripture, but about the quality of thought, emotion and growth that enters the system. Original Telugu voice preserved alongside a clear English rendering, with deeper reflection drawn from Acharya (A Soul’s Journey) and Ramtha.

1. The original voice (Telugu)

నాన్ వెజ్ తినకూడదు అని ఏ పురాణంలో రాసింది అంటే తినమని నీకు ఏ పురాణంలో రాసింది?

ఐదు నిమిషాలని జిహ్వ చాపల్యం కోసం మీ రుచిని చంపుకోలేక ఇంద్రియ నిగ్రహం లేక — సర్వజీవులలో భగవంతుడు ఉంటాడు అని శాస్త్రం చెబుతున్నా కూడా ఒక జీవిని చంపి తినే హక్కు నీకుంది అని నువ్వు భావిస్తున్నావా?

అది నీ కర్మ అనుకుంటే తిను. కాకపోతే శాస్త్రం ఏమంటుందంటే — నువ్వు నాన్ వెజ్ తినడం వల్ల ఎవర్ని చూడు: నాకు నెగటివ్ థాట్స్ వస్తున్నాయి, నెగటివ్ థింకింగ్, ఓవర్ థింకింగ్, భయం.

ఒక జీవిని చంపుకొని తిన్నప్పుడు దాని నరనరాల్లో ఇంకిపోయింది భయమే. ఒక జీవిని చంపేటప్పుడు దాని నరాలు నిండిపోయింది భయమే కదా. ఆ భయాన్ని కదా నువ్వు తిన్నావు. మరి భయం రాకుండా ఇంకేం వస్తది?

నీకు పాజిటివ్‌గా ఉండాలి అనుకుంటే నువ్వు శాకాహారం తీసుకో. పాజిటివ్‌గా మాట్లాడు. నాన్ వెజ్ తింటావు — నాకు పాజిటివ్‌గా ఉండాలంటే? నాన్ వెజ్ తింటావు — నాకు మంచి జరగాలంటే? నాన్ వెజ్ తింటావు — నాకు జీవితంలో మంచి జరుగుతుంది, గ్రోత్ అవుతుంది అని ఎలా అనుకుంటావు?

2. Clear English rendering

You ask: “In which Purāṇa is it written that one should not eat non-veg?” Then I ask: “In which Purāṇa is it written that you must eat it?”

For five minutes of tongue-craving you cannot master your taste; there is no sense-restraint. The śāstra itself declares that the Divine dwells in every living being — yet you still feel you have the right to kill a being and eat it.

If you insist “this is my karma,” then eat. Otherwise hear what the teaching points to: look at what non-veg eating brings — negative thoughts, negative thinking, overthinking, fear.

When a living being is killed, fear saturates its every nerve. That fear is what enters the food. That fear is what you take in. Then what else can arise in you except more fear?

If you truly want to be positive, take sāttvic (vegetarian) food. Speak positively. You eat non-veg and still demand positivity? You eat non-veg and still expect only good to happen? You eat non-veg and wonder why growth is not coming in life?

3. Core teaching unpacked

Sitha Achary’s thrust

The argument is not merely “scripture says so.” It is causal and experiential:

What is taken in What is absorbed What tends to arise
Flesh of a being killed in fear The vibration of terror that filled its nerves at the moment of death Fear, restless thinking, negative loops, difficulty in sustained positivity
Food chosen from craving alone Weakening of indriya-nigraha (sense mastery) Tongue rules the person instead of the person ruling the tongue
Ignoring “God in all beings” A subtle sense of right to take life for taste Inner contradiction that blocks deeper growth

The call is simple and sharp: if positivity, clarity and growth are the real goals, then the quality of what enters the body and nervous system matters. Sāttvic food is presented as the practical support for a positive inner state.

4. Reflection from Acharya (A Soul’s Journey)

Acharya · Evolution & the animal kingdom

Acharya teaches that animals function under a group soul. Fear of the human being is the great retarding force in that kingdom. When man kills only for genuine need, the evolutionary cost is one thing; when killing is driven by taste, fashion or indifference, fear is multiplied and the progress of the group soul is slowed.

The fear that saturates a being at the moment of violent death does not simply vanish. In the language of subtle bodies, emotion is substance. What is strongly charged with terror can leave an imprint that a sensitive nervous system may register as unrest, anxiety or heaviness.

Acharya’s larger point is evolutionary responsibility: human kindness (or cruelty) toward animals is not sentimental — it is an evolutionary factor. Choosing food that minimises the injection of terror into one’s own system is consistent with reducing the fear-colour one carries into sleep, meditation and daily thought.

5. Reflection from Ramtha

Ramtha · Thought, feeling & the Now

Ramtha’s core statement is simple: thought creates feeling; feeling shapes the condition of life. If you continually feed the system with the vibration of fear (whether from outer events or from what is taken in as food), the emotional field remains tuned to fear. In that state, joy — “the grandest state of being” — is harder to sustain.

“Live and be happy” is not a command to force a smile while the body is loaded with agitation. It is an invitation to remove, as far as possible, the unnecessary fuels of fear and to choose what supports clarity and presence in this Now.

From this angle, the question is not only moral or scriptural. It is practical: does this choice increase the frequency of fear in my field, or does it support the frequency of peace and joy I say I want?

6. Practical reflection points

1. Honesty about motive
Is the choice driven by five minutes of tongue-craving, or by a clear intention for the quality of consciousness I wish to live in?

2. Fear as substance
If a being dies saturated with terror, that vibration is not abstract. Notice, over days or weeks, whether reducing such food correlates with quieter nights, fewer fear-dreams, or less background anxiety.

3. Sense mastery is training
Indriya-nigraha is not sudden perfection. It is the gradual ability to let a craving arise, be felt, and pass without automatic obedience. Each small refusal is practice.

4. Consistency of goal and means
Wanting positivity, growth and freedom from overthinking while repeatedly taking in the vibration of fear creates an inner contradiction. Aligning food with the stated goal removes one layer of that contradiction.

5. No self-hatred, only clarity
The teaching is not an invitation to guilt. It is an invitation to see the causal chain and to choose more cleanly. Past choices can be met with understanding; present choice can be made with awareness.

7. One clear distinction

Craving-driven eating Conscious sāttvic choice
Tongue decides; fear-vibration may enter Intention for clarity and positivity decides
“I have the right” is the inner stance “I share life with all beings” is the inner stance
Often followed by wonder: “Why so much fear / negative thought / no growth?” Supports the nervous system in holding a quieter, more positive baseline

The original voice is fierce because the stake is the quality of the inner life.

When a being is killed in fear, that fear does not disappear — it can be taken in. If positivity, clear thinking and real growth are the goals, then what enters the body and the nervous system is not a small matter.

Acharya shows the evolutionary cost of multiplying fear in the animal kingdom. Ramtha shows that thought and feeling create the condition of life in this Now. Sitha Achary brings both to the plate: choose the food that matches the consciousness you say you want.

— Sitha Achary · with reflection from Acharya & Ramtha
