How to Get Through Old Thought Patterns & Deeds

According to Ramtha — and in the practical tone shared with Acharya — old thought patterns and old deeds are not broken by fighting them. They dissolve when you stop feeding them and deliberately install a new way of thinking and feeling.

1. See what the old pattern actually is

Ramtha is clear: you are what you have thought long enough to believe.

Old patterns — guilt, fear, “I am not enough”, revenge, self-pity, the need to control — are simply repeated thoughts that have become the neural program. Deeds follow those thoughts.

The first move is not “fix me.” It is:

Notice the thought without becoming it.
“This is an old program speaking. It is not the truth of who I am.”

That simple recognition already loosens the grip.

2. Do not fight the old thought — starve it

Fighting an old pattern still gives it attention and energy. Ramtha’s way is different:

  • Do not argue with the old thought.
  • Do not suppress it.
  • Do not rehearse the old story (“I always do this… because of what happened…”).

Just stop feeding it. When the old thought arises, gently refuse to continue the conversation with it.

“Whatever you contemplate long enough, merely by desire, you will become.” — Ramtha

3. Desire a new thought more than the comfort of the old one

The old pattern continues because some part of you still finds it familiar or “safe.”

The new pattern begins the moment desire for freedom, love, or knowingness becomes stronger than the need to stay in the old groove.

Practically:

  • Decide what you now choose to be — calm, free, loving, powerful, open.
  • Desire that state more than you desire the old reaction.
  • Feel it — even for a few seconds — as if it is already true.

Feeling is what writes the new program into the cells.

4. Closed mind vs opening the mind

Closed mind Opening the mind
“What will people think?” “I allow this new thought. I feel it.”
Immediately lists why change is impossible Allows the desire and takes one small aligned step
Judges self and others Sees the pure Isness — no comparison
Reacts from the wound Responds from the present

Opening the mind is not a technique first — it is willingness + desire + self-love. When that is present, the old neural net loses its monopoly.

5. About old deeds

Ramtha does not treat past deeds as a permanent sentence. They are the result of past thought.

Once the thought that created them is no longer fed:

  • The emotional charge fades.
  • The need to repeat the deed disappears.
  • New deeds naturally arise from the new consciousness.

You do not have to “pay off” every old action through suffering. You outgrow the consciousness that produced it. That is the real release.

Acharya’s practical voice adds: respond from the present, not from the wound. When the old trigger comes, pause, stay centred, and choose the response that belongs to who you are now — not who you were when the pattern was formed.

6. A simple daily practice

1. Catch the old thought the moment it appears.
2. Name it silently: “I see you. I no longer need you.”
3. Replace it immediately with a thought you desire to live.
4. Feel that new thought for 10–20 seconds. (This is the most important part.)
5. Act from the new feeling in the next small moment.

Consistency matters more than intensity. Thought by thought, feeling by feeling, the old program is rewritten.

Essence
Old patterns and deeds end when you stop believing them and start desiring — and feeling — a greater truth about yourself. That is the heart of Ramtha’s teaching, and it aligns with Acharya’s practical counsel: do not live from the old wound; live from the freedom that is available now.

“You are precisely what you are thinking. Everything you think, so will it be in your life.” — Ramtha, The White Book
